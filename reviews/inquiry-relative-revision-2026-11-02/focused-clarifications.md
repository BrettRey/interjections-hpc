## 2. Explain the stronger category claim in §3.2

Proposed rationale:

> An admission rule fixes which cases enter a class. A category claim makes a further commitment: membership supports informative expectations beyond the evidence used to assign it. The distinction matters because assembling a grouping and warranting those expectations can succeed or fail separately. Here, category standing names the latter achievement; it isn’t a verdict on whether a descriptive class is scientifically useful.

Distinguish the reach of a claim over realizations, environments, and sign-types. Extending an observed use to new occurrences or constructions can already involve induction. Category-level projection can concern another independently identified sign-type. A target needn’t be a differently named property: the same property in a genuinely new environment can supply informative reach.

Connect informative reach to scope and alternatives. A property shared by nearly everything outside the grouping gives membership little additional inferential work. An imperfect probabilistic association may be useful. This concerns the proposed expectation; it doesn’t impose a requirement that a category representation outperform every alternative model. Make the corresponding wording changes in the nearby assessment paragraph and table.

Proposed statement of this paper’s verdict:

> I provisionally accept the grammatical and action-specific categories used here for the relations documented in the cited descriptions. This inherits their evidential warrant at that scope; it doesn’t establish every extension considered here. The present comparisons defend expressive and depictive distinctions and reject an unsupported transfer from phonology to grammar. They leave the broader projective standing of the proposed semantic, phonological, and ancestry groupings open.

The intent is to identify inherited commitments, not to claim that this paper independently validates them.
