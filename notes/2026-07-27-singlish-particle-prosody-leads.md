## Obtained and read

**Chong, Adam J. (2012). "A preliminary model of Singaporean English
intonational phonology." *UCLA Working Papers in Phonetics* 111, 41–62.**
Open access, eScholarship (`qt9d46z3sb`). PDF pulled and read.

Proposes the first AM-framework model of SgE intonation: three prosodic units
above the word, Accentual Phrase, intermediate phrase, Intonational Phrase.
Three speakers, read declaratives and questions.

The passage that matters, verbatim from the discussion:

> "the SgE lexicon contains a number of question and discourse particles that
> have been argued to have lexically-specified tone (see Lim 2004 for a
> summary). How these would fit into the present intonational model is an open
> question."

**Two things follow.** There is a rival to the "particle carries the host
unit's nuclear fall" analysis, namely that the particles carry their own
lexically-specified tone; this is why the literature notates them with tone
numbers (`meh55`, `what21`, `sia24`). And the prosodic constituency question
is explicitly open in the source that would settle it, so neither analysis can
be cited as established.

## To get (all paywalled or blocked to me)

1. **Lim, Lisa (ed.) (2004). *Singapore English: A grammatical description*.
   Amsterdam: John Benjamins (VEAW G33).** The summary reference Chong points
   to for lexically-specified tone. Lim's own chapter is the target.
2. **Lim, Lisa (2007). "Mergers and acquisitions: On the ages and origins of
   Singapore English particles." *World Englishes* 26(4), 446–473.** Traces
   *ah* and *lah* to Bazaar Malay and/or Hokkien, and *leh*, *lor*, *meh* to
   later Cantonese, with tone borrowed as part of the package.
3. **Soh, Y. Q., Lee, J., & Tan, Y.-Y. (2022). "Ethnicity and Tone Production
   on Singlish Particles." *Languages* 7(3), 243.** doi:10.3390/languages7030243.
   Open access, but MDPI returns 403 to my fetcher, so it needs a browser.
   444 tokens of `sia24`, `meh55`, `what21` from the National Speech Corpus;
   finds no significant ethnicity effect on the acoustic parameters.
4. **"Prominence and intonation in Singapore English", *Journal of Phonetics*
   (2023).** Unverified relevance; listed because prominence is the other half
   of the question.
5. Background if wanted: Deterding, D. (1994). "The intonation of Singapore
   English." *JIPA* 24(2), 61–72; Ng, E-Ching (2012). "Chinese meets Malay
   meets English: origins of Singaporean English word-final high tone."
   *International Journal of Bilingualism*.

## Bearing on the manuscript

Don't assert either analysis. The defensible statement is that the prosodic
status of clause-final particles in Colloquial Singapore English is unsettled,
with one line of work assigning them lexical tone, and that this is why
*lah*'s membership in \intj{syn} can't be read off written corpus position.
That is a stronger reason for caution than "written data can't show prosody",
because it is citable.
