## 2. NEW properties

- **NEW:EXPRESSIVE_STRENGTH**: a lexically specified minimum strength. *damn* is "mild" and *fucking* is "stronger" (p. 187).
- **NEW:VALENCE_SHIFT**: characteristically negative items can be construed affectionately from context, "But they are not always negative." (p. 188). Krifka's limit applies to referential *bastard* (p. 188).
- **NEW:GRADABLE_INTENSITY**: expressive intensity varies continuously and expressives can be compared, "potential for inﬁnite gradability" (p. 178). Restricted by "racial epithets and curses are incomparable in their expressivity" (p. 179).
- **NEW:EXPRESSIVE_CONSISTENCY** (a co-occurrence restriction): a context contains "at most one expressive object" for every salient pair of entities (pp. 179, 184). Mixing conflicting expressives is incoherent (pp. 179, 188).
- **NEW:PREDICATIVE_COPULAR**: expressives can't be copular predicates (p. 194).
- **NEW:SYNTACTIC_EMBEDDING**: some unnamed classes can't be syntactically embedded at all (p. 169).
- **NEW:OPTIONAL_EXPRESSIVE**: the speaker can leave the expressive content out. Expressive modifiers, epithets and Japanese honorifics have this option; German address pronouns don't (p. 192).
- **NEW:DESCRIPTIVE_ARGUMENT**: the expressive takes a descriptive meaning (an entity or a proposition) as its argument (pp. 168, 175, 186–187).

## 3. Warnings against using a property as a diagnostic

- **Independence is incomplete.** "It is not quite true that the two dimensions of meaning operate independently of each other." (p. 168). Evidentials: "But to what extent do they manifest the independence property?" (p. 195).
- **Surviving negation (P11N) doesn't single out expressives.** Negation, conditionals and *maybe* are presupposition holes: "These involve some of the standard presupposition holes" (p. 170). What separates expressives from presuppositions is attitude plugs (pp. 170–171).
- **Escaping embedding isn't enough by itself.** "The content of deﬁnite descriptions can also es- cape up through holes, plugs, and tense operators." (p. 172). What is diagnostic is that the wide reading is forced: "It is this invariance that accounts for much of what is special about expressive content." (p. 172).
- **Speaker orientation is a default, not an invariant.** "It appears to be a marked option to evaluate expressives with a judge who is not also the speaker, but we need to allow for the possibility." (p. 176). Researchers' disagreement about embeddability comes from this (p. 176).
- **P07 caveat.** Expressives carry conversational propositional implications: "This is the kind of information that speakers volunteer when asked what expressives mean." (p. 178).
- **Descriptive ineffability is shared.** Discourse particles have it (p. 176), and so do evidentials: "What exactly they convey can be di‰cult to specify using language not drawn from the evidential system itself (descriptive ine¤ability; see Faller 2002: 3)." (p. 195). For German pronouns Potts is unsure (p. 191).
- **The undeniability test for immediacy is shared.** "The same is true of assertions, requests, demands, exclamatives" (p. 180).
- **Repeatability is neither necessary nor clean.**
  - "The repeatability property is challenging from the point of view of the proposed characteristics of expressives." (p. 191).
  - "However, repeated use of the familiar feature does not deepen the sense of familiarity" (p. 191).
  - For Japanese honorifics, "too many, and the e¤ect is one of sarcasm or irony" (p. 182).
  - Some descriptive repetitions aren't redundant: "Apparent exceptions to this pattern such as big big big apple make sense compositionally" (p. 182), and *salad salad* is "More problematic" (p. 182).
  - Ineffability makes minimal pairs "hard or impossible to con- struct" (p. 182).
- **Valence isn't fixed.** "But they are not always negative." (p. 188).
- **Syntactic position doesn't fix the expressive's argument.** Potts notes "the pressing need for a theory of how the syntax does, or does not, constrain the possibilities for expressive function–argument structures" (p. 189).
- **The class is defined by the theory.**
  - The copular exclusion follows from the type system (p. 194).
  - *lurk* and *few* are excluded because "it fails all of the tests for expressivity identiﬁed here" (p. 194), which "is not to say that the analyses of lurk and few are mistaken".
  - ja is unresolved (pp. 194–195).
