## 2. NEW: properties
- **NEW:SCOPELESS.** CIE content is "not affected by semantic operators" (p. 6). Tested with negation, conditionals, modals and questions; see (6) and (12).
- **NEW:DENIAL_IMMUNE.** "the CIE content cannot be the target of denial" (p. 7). Narrowed on p. 41: "What I will call truth-directed denials like those in (46) cannot target" CI content; indirect denials can.
- **NEW:UNBINDABLE.** A conditional antecedent that entails the CIE content doesn't cancel its projection; "It differs in that it cannot be bound" (p. 7). This is what separates it from presupposition.
- **NEW:RESOURCE_INSENSITIVE.** "a resource sensitive logic is one that consumes resources as they are used in proofs" (p. 12). Supplementary CI types aren't resource-sensitive: they hand back their at-issue argument as well as the CI (p. 14, R4). Example: in "John, a swimmer", the term for John is still there.
- **NEW:SHUNTING.** These are resource-sensitive CIE types that move content into the CIE dimension "without leaving anything behind" (p. 18). "The reason that shunting types allow for utterances with only CIE content is the resource-sensitivity of these types." (p. 31)
- **NEW:MIXED_CONTENT.** An item introduces at-issue and CIE content at once, and is monomorphemic (pp. 4–5).
- **NEW:MAIN_CIE.** The utterance conveys only CIE content: "a particular sentence (or utterance) can convey only CIE content" (p. 30). The conclusion's three-way typology is "three distinct types of conventional implicature: supplementary CIEs as modeled in Potts 2005, CIEs that provide main content" (p. 49), and the third type is mixed.
- **NEW:NONFUNCTIONAL.** A main CIE that "expresses a constant" and takes no argument (p. 35), as opposed to a functional main CIE such as *man*(p) or *yokumo*.
- **NEW:EXPRESSIVE_MODIFIABLE.** Purely expressive content can be modified by other expressive items, here *man* and *totally* (pp. 35–36). This is her argument for t^s over t^c.
- **NEW:EXPRESSIVE_CORRECTNESS.** The item has no truth conditions but can be "expressively correct (appropriate) or not" (p. 34; the notion comes from Kaplan, p. 32).
- **NEW:POLARITY_CONTEXT_DEPENDENT.** Whether the emotive meaning is positive or negative is set by context (p. 29; footnote 50, p. 38).
- **NEW:IDENTITY_INERT.** Expressive content stays out of identity operations such as anaphora and ellipsis (footnote 33, p. 23, reported from Potts et al. 2009).

**English assignments by type:**
- Supplementary: appositives, *frankly speaking*.
- Mixed: Kraut and other pejoratives, *bum*, *asshole*, *steed*, *nag*.
- Main/shunting: stand-alone *man*, Ouch!, Thanks!, Good morning.; *totally* as an operator; tentatively "You damn fool!" and exclamatives.
- Prenominal *damn*: untyped.

## 3. Warnings about the diagnostics
- **Most important for the EXPRESSIVE grouping:** "I think that, in terms of their combinatorics, there might well not be any difference." (p. 49) Also: "My suspicion is that the difference between expressive and CI lies in the type of meanings that are carried rather than how those meanings behave in composition" (p. 50). So every structural test here (scope, denial, binding, resource sensitivity) picks out CIE content in general, appositives included, not expressives in particular. This is the paper's own working assumption too: "both conventional implicatures and expressives make use of roughly the same combinatoric system" (footnote 2, p. 2).
- **Scopelessness has an exception:** "The two show a similar lack of interaction with most kinds of semantic operators (embedding under attitudes being a significant exception)" (p. 49).
- **Potts's list is contested:** "Potts lists a number of properties that these kinds of content are meant to have, some of which have been called into question by various authors" (p. 6). She keeps only two tests, and "I will use only examples that have not been controversial." (footnote 7, p. 6)
- **The denial test needs the right kind of denial.** "Denials like (47) can target either type of content" (p. 41); only truth-directed denials discriminate.
- **The binding test depends on the paraphrase:** "If the paraphrase given is incorrect, or, even worse, if the expressive portion of pejoratives is such that it does not admit a linguistic paraphrase at all, then the test is invalidated." (p. 9) Also: "A reviewer notes that the projection behavior may not be very surprising, given that we also have expressive content in the antecedent, which has nothing to bind it." (footnote 14, p. 9)
- **Ineffability:** "Geurts (2007) notes that something similar holds for other, non-expressive words like green" (footnote 13, p. 9). McCready answers that the two differ in degree.
- **Ouch! not asserting:** "this is so only if one does not accept relevant aspects of the performative hypothesis" (footnote 46, p. 34).
- **Stand-alone particle analyses can't be told apart:** "it’s not clear that there are empirical tests to distinguish between the two positions" (p. 34). The two positions are *man* modifying a contextual proposition versus *man* as a simple exclamation. Footnote 44 adds that binding tests can't be used. On p. 34, the shunting analysis of particles "turns on the empirical question of whether or not the propositional content of sentences modified by particles can serve as answers to questions."
- **The tests can't apply where the item can't go:** "Because yokumo is ungrammatical in these contexts, it is impossible to test its scope behavior, and, as a result, the operator test for conventional implicature cannot be applied." (p. 42) The same holds for binding.
- **Monomorphemicity is theory-dependent:** "Exactly what counts as monomorphemic is, in part, a theory-dependent notion" (p. 5), and "one could always decompose, after all" (p. 28).
- **Judgments vary:** in footnote 32 (p. 22), a reviewer accepts "He’s German but at least he’s not a Kraut", while "speakers I have consulted (including myself) find the examples infelicitous". Possibly "some of the CIE content has been reanalyzed as at-issue".
- **The typology is provisional:** "I doubt it is exhaustive, however." (p. 49)
