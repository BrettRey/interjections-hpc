# Check Projectibility: A Projectibility-First Audit

Read the specified file (or `main.tex`) and audit it against the current projectibility-first framework. This skill replaces the former `check-hpc`. The change is not cosmetic: HPC (homeostatic property clusters) is no longer the frame. It is one world-side commitment at the strong end of a partial order. Projectibility is the frame; homeostasis is a claim you reach only with its own evidence.

Three failures dominate these drafts, and Claude's training bias makes all three easy to miss:

1. **Mechanisms or kindhood crowd out projection.** The payoff becomes "here are the mechanisms" or "so it's a natural kind" rather than "observing part of this licenses you to expect what about the rest, and with what warrant."
2. **Warrant gets fused with world-side claims.** A precise claim is treated as a warranted one; a reliable projection is treated as having a known causal ground; a stable association is treated as if its causal ordering were established.
3. **`Homeostatic` is overclaimed.** Corrective control is the strongest world-side commitment; drafts apply its vocabulary to profiles that are merely stable, entrenched, or transmitted.

## Context

- Current directory: !`pwd`

## The framework (current)

**A projective claim is the primary bearer. Projectibility is licensed reach beyond the cases in hand: observing a source feature or result warrants an expectation about a target member, setting, time, or outcome.** Predicates and categories are projectible only derivatively, when they figure in families of warranted projective claims. A worldly pattern can make a claim reliable, but it is not itself the bearer of warrant.

Four questions come apart, and can fail independently:

| Question | What it fixes | Independent failure |
|----------|---------------|---------------------|
| **Declaration** | What is projected from what, for which bearer, over which scope | A claim can be perfectly precise and still unwarranted |
| **Warrant** | What evidence and inferential standard license the projection | A reliable projection can lack any known causal ground |
| **Profile and ground** | Which worldly pattern makes source informative about target, and what grounds its reliability | A causal dependence can be transient; a stable association can recur with its ordering unknown |
| **Discovery and revision** | How the claim, its warrant, and its ground are found, tested, revised | An institution can preserve measurement comparability without stabilizing the phenomenon |

**The projective declaration** fixes ten fields: source, outcome, bearer, population, conditions, transformations, timescale, tolerance, evidential standard, and failure conditions. A draft that "projects a category" without most of these has not made a checkable claim.

**Two separations are the sharpest discipline, and the biggest change from the old HPC audit:**

- **Degrees of warrant (for a fixed claim) are separate from world-side commitments.** How well-evidenced a projection is does not tell you what the world has to be like for it to hold, and vice versa.
- **The world-side commitments form a partial order, not a single ladder.** *Stability* (the profile projects from observed subcluster to unobserved features across contexts; Slater) and *causal order* (an ordering relation, core-to-derivative or conditioning or licensing, explains which projections are licensed; Khalidi) are **initially incomparable**: a profile can have either without the other, and neither ranks above the other. *Effective maintenance* (a stabilizer whose removal would fragment the profile; Boyd) and *corrective control* (perturbation-sensitive correction preserving a higher-scale relation through lower-scale variation; the strict `homeostatic`) are genuinely **stronger** commitments that sit above both. Do not narrate stability and order as rungs of one ladder, and do not let either imply maintenance or control.

**Stabilizer vs controller** remains the place `homeostatic` goes wrong. Two counterfactuals get run together:
- *Maintenance:* what happens to the profile if the stabilizer is removed?
- *Control:* does some process register and answer a perturbation that threatens the profile, bringing it back toward a projectible relation?

Entrenchment, transmission, analogy, or a frozen accident can satisfy the first and fail the second. Corrective control has four marks: (a) a perturbation threatening the profile, (b) a variable or relation the response pathway is coupled to, (c) a response that changes lower-scale conditions, (d) a result in which that response preserves the higher-scale relation supporting projection. Feedback alone doesn't qualify. A clamp that holds the regulated variable fixed can sever the very feedback that constitutes control, so stillness under a clamp isn't evidence of no control.

**Prospective demotion.** Declaration fields and failure conditions are fixed *before* the outcome. A projection that fails its declared conditions is demoted, split, scoped, or retired; it may not be rescued by narrowing the population or conditions after the fact to exclude the misses. Post hoc narrowing that turns a failed projection into a successful one is the signature move to catch.

**Field-relativity is scope, not truth.** The field selects which question to ask; it does not make the projection succeed. "Projectible for a purpose" must not slide into "real because it's useful to someone."

## The audit

Read the whole file first. The gaps are structural, not local. Then check:

### 1. Declaration
- Is there a projective claim with its fields fixed (at least source, outcome, bearer, population, conditions, timescale, tolerance, failure conditions), stated before mechanisms or kind-talk?
- **RED:** the draft opens with a cluster, its mechanisms, or its claim to kindhood; what the category lets you project appears late, vaguely, or not at all.
- **GREEN:** "observing X of a member warrants expecting Y, for this population under these conditions, within this tolerance, and here is what would count as failure" is stated up front.

### 2. Non-trivial projection
- Does the projection go beyond the tests used to *detect* the category?
- **RED:** the category predicts only its own membership diagnostics ("member of X predicts the features we used to test for X"). Circularity presented as coherence.
- **GREEN:** at least one warranted inference not used as a membership test (acquisition order, processing signature, diachronic behaviour, perturbation response, cross-linguistic correlate, behaviour in untested frames).

### 3. Warrant kept separate from world-side claims
- Are "how well-evidenced" and "what the world must be like" argued separately, or does one stand in for the other?
- **RED:** a precise or formal claim treated as thereby warranted; a well-warranted projection treated as thereby having a known causal ground; strength of evidence used to license an ontological upgrade.
- **GREEN:** the draft says how good the evidence is *and*, separately, which world-side commitment (if any) it has established.

### 4. World-side order: incomparability, not a ladder
- When the draft moves among stability, causal order, maintenance, and control, does it respect that stability and causal order are incomparable and that maintenance/control are strictly stronger?
- **RED:** stability and order narrated as consecutive rungs; "it's stable, so it's causally ordered"; "they co-occur regularly, so they form a causal network"; "the cluster is reliable, so it is maintained"; *therefore/thereby/so* carrying a free upgrade.
- **GREEN:** stability and causal order are argued from their own evidence and neither is inferred from the other; maintenance and control are claimed only with their own evidence; the draft says which commitment it has reached and which it has not.

### 5. Stabilizer vs controller (don't overclaim `homeostatic`)
- Is `homeostatic`, `self-regulating`, `regulates`, or `corrects` applied to a profile that is merely persistent, entrenched, transmitted, or maintained?
- **RED:** control language with no perturbation, no tracked relation, no response pathway, no scale split. Maintenance relabelled as control.
- **GREEN:** the four marks of corrective control are present at a specified scale, or the draft deliberately stops at "stable / maintained" and declines the `homeostatic` label.

### 6. Explanatory level and mereology
- Is the explanatory level marked, and are whole-agent predicates attributed to the right bearer?
- **RED:** "the grammar regulates," "the system corrects," "the category decides" — mereological slippage that usually smuggles in an unearned control claim.
- **GREEN:** the corrector is a specified system or practice (a monitored production loop, a metalinguistic sanction, an acquisition pathway), not "the grammar."

### 7. Scope and field-relativity
- Is projectibility kept distinct from the scope of projection?
- **RED:** "projectible for a purpose" used so that usefulness does the work of truth ("real for anyone whose purposes it serves").
- **GREEN:** interests select the question; evidence and structure decide whether the projection holds. The same extension can support different true projections for syntax, semantics, typology, or diachrony.
- **Typology sub-check:** if the draft projects across languages, does it separate convergence from contingency? Convergent profiles rebuilt under shared constraint can project across unrelated lineages; contingent profiles project only within a lineage, area, or pathway. "Homeostatic here, so homeostatic everywhere" is extrapolation, not projection.

### 8. Prospective demotion and failure modes
- Are failure conditions declared in advance, and does the draft honour them rather than narrowing after the fact?
- **RED:** no scope conditions; every case is a win; a failed projection quietly rescued by excluding the misses from the population; `homeostatic` granted wherever a cluster recurs.
- **GREEN:** the draft names where the profile is too thin, too local, wrong-level, over-mechanized, over-homeostatized, or over-exported, and treats failed projection as a reason to scope, demote, split, or retire the category rather than something to hide or narrow away.

### 9. Positioning and conclusion
- Is the advantage over rivals "better or newly-licensed projections", or "finer decomposition"? Does the conclusion cash out in what you can now *do*, at the world-side commitment the evidence actually reached?
- **RED:** "this identifies mechanisms rival F doesn't"; "future work should map the mechanisms in finer detail"; a blanket `homeostatic` verdict.
- **GREEN:** the conclusion states what the category now lets you project, explain, or intervene on, and reports honestly which commitment (stable, causally ordered, maintained, controlled) the evidence secured.

Report as a table:

| Check | Status | Evidence |

- Respect source grounding: if a genuine non-trivial projection is missing, note that one is needed; do not invent acquisition orders, processing signatures, or corpus results to fill the gap.
- This audit is read-only. Report findings. Don't edit the file.

    68	\begin{abstract}
    69	Is \term{interjection} a genuine lexical category of English? Grammars
    70	have doubted it, and the doubt is about standing rather than about how
    71	much has been written. This paper argues that the category earns its
    72	place, and that what earns it isn't a defining property but
    73	projectibility: observing some properties of a case licenses predictions
    74	about others (a defeasible default, not an entailment). Membership licenses
    75	inferences about
    76	prosodic packaging, supplement function,
    77	use-conditional stance, sequential position, response function, and
    78	interactional uptake. Those inferences are pragmatic rather than
    79	truth-conditional, which is a result about the category and not a change
    80	of subject.
    81	
    82	The paper argues that they're licensed by a stable causal network among
    83	those properties. Invocative, verbal, and onomatopoeic routes recruit forms into a
    84	similar profile, while leaving different pathway residues.
    85	Regional/indexical conditioning supplies a further source of boundary
    86	structure. Supplementary Corpus of Global Web-Based English (GloWbE) and
    87	Corpus of Historical American English (COHA) probes illustrate boundary
    88	behaviour, exit residue, and attrition. The result is a path-dependent
    89	account of category membership. What membership warrants is bounded: for a
    90	lexical item in a grammatical environment in present-day English, observing
    91	part of the cluster licenses expectations about the rest, because the
    92	prosodic, syntactic, semantic, and interactional diagnostics are linked.
    93	\end{abstract}

   100	\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
   101	
   102	English interjections are small forms with large consequences. A
   103	speaker's \mention{oh}, \mention{wow}, \mention{hey}, \mention{damn}, or
   104	\mention{mm-hm} can package a response, display stance, open or sustain a
   105	sequence, initiate repair, or invite uptake. Grammars have never been
   106	settled about what to do with them. This paper asks whether
   107	\term{interjection} is a genuine lexical category of English, and what
   108	a form's membership in it licenses us to predict.
   109	
   110	In 1586, William Bullokar wrote the earliest grammar of English. It
   111	included interjections, which he defined as words that betoken
   112	\enquote{a sudden passion of the mind} \citep[373]{bullokar1586}.
   113	
   114	Four centuries later the standing is less secure.
   115	\textcite[90]{jespersen1924} treated interjection as a manner in which
   116	words of other categories may be used, which is to deny it categoryhood.
   117	\textcite[67]{quirk1985} kept the category but called it
   118	\enquote{marginal and anomalous}.
   119	\textcite[22]{HuddlestonPullum2002} listed interjection as one of nine
   120	lexical categories in \textit{The Cambridge Grammar of the English
   121	Language} (\textit{CGEL}) (\mention{ah},
   122	\mention{damn}, \mention{gosh}, \mention{hey}, \mention{oh},
   123	\mention{ooh}, \mention{ouch}, \mention{whoa}, \mention{wow}), while the
   124	companion textbook set it aside as \enquote{the minor category of
   125	interjections \dots\ about which there really isn't anything interesting
   126	for a grammar to say} \citep[16]{HuddlestonPullum2005}. Its second
   127	edition returns interjection to the inventory, but concedes that
   128	\enquote{grammarians typically say very little about interjections, and
   129	we don't plan to be an exception} \citep[24]{HuddlestonPullumReynolds2022}.
   130	Readmitted to the list, and still largely undescribed.
   131	
   132	\textcite{libert2019} objects that calling interjections neglected
   133	\enquote{is an overstatement}: work on their phonetics, syntax, semantics,
   134	and pragmatics goes back a long way. What's stayed
   135	unsettled is standing rather than volume of attention, namely whether
   136	interjections constitute a lexical category, and what membership would
   137	license.
   138	
   139	\textcite[63]{cram2008} makes the historiographic point more sharply.
   140	Surveying the tradition from Isidore of Seville onward, he finds \enquote{no
   141	firmer consensus about this category than at any earlier period}, and notes
   142	that interjections \enquote{function as a touchstone or test case for various
   143	boundary controversies}, naming the syntax--pragmatics and
   144	semantics--pragmatics boundaries. The difference is in what the case is asked
   145	to reveal.
   146	Cram asks what interjections show about our theories. I ask what holds this
   147	category together.
   148	
   149	The empirical picture hasn't simplified. The evidence bearing on membership is
   150	spread across phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics, and pragmatics, and no
   151	one of them settles the question. The
   152	category resists clean definition, since no single property is necessary
   153	and sufficient, and its boundaries with nouns, verbs, adverbs,
   154	fillers, and routine formulae are all disputed.
   155	Cross-linguistically, \enquote{interjection} is at best a comparative
   156	concept \citep{haspelmath2010}.
   157	
   158	Those difficulties are what make the category question worth asking. If
   159	interjections are a category, the relevant payoff isn't an essence or a
   160	single defining feature. It's \term{projectibility}: the inferential
   161	payoff by which observing some properties of a new case licenses
   162	predictions about others \citep{Goodman1955}. For interjections,
   163	prosodic isolation, supplement function, or stance display should
   164	license expectations about non-inflection, non-referential or
   165	use-conditional meaning, sequential position, response function, and
   166	interactional uptake.
   167	
   168	That's meant in at least two distinct ways. A learner or hearer
   169	meeting an unfamiliar form reads the cues and anticipates its behaviour,
   170	without needing to recognize it as an interjection. A linguist assigns the form to the
   171	category and derives the predictions from it explicitly. Both draw on the same coupling.
   172	Section~\ref{sec:proj-partial} separates them where the difference matters.
   173	
   174	A form's \term{profile} is the particular combination of cluster properties it shows
   175	in a given grammatical environment: whether it's prosodically set off,
   176	whether it inflects, whether it refers, what stance or response it carries.
   177	The word is doing bookkeeping here and has nothing to do with the
   178	figure-and-base sense familiar from cognitive grammar. To say that a
   179	property or a category \term{licenses} an inference is to say the inference
   180	is a good default, defeasible by the particular case, not that it follows
   181	necessarily.
   182	
   183	The object of analysis is the category position, not the membership list
   184	alone. Individual items such as \mention{wow}, \mention{damn},
   185	\mention{bruh}, and \mention{fie} matter because they show what can be
   186	inferred from membership: likely prosody, syntactic independence,
   187	limits on argumenthood, stance or response function, and possible routes
   188	into or out of the profile.
   189	
   190	By \term{path dependence}, I mean that where a form came from keeps
   191	showing in how it behaves at the category's edges, long after it has
   192	arrived. \mention{Damn} came in from a verb and still takes a complement
   193	(\mention{damn these mosquitoes!}); \mention{gee} came in by clipping a
   194	religious invocation and takes nothing at all. Both sit squarely in the
   195	profile, and they'd be indistinguishable if convergence erased history. It doesn't. The leftovers of a form's source let an analyst reason backwards
   196	from how it behaves now to where it came from.
   197	
   198	Throughout, I use \term{property cluster} descriptively for the empirical
   199	pattern of co-occurring features. What's claimed for English interjections is
   200	bounded accordingly: the inferences membership licenses most strongly concern
   201	use, stance, sequence, and uptake rather than denotation.
   202	
   203	This inferential framing motivates a causal-network analysis.
   204	\textcite{khalidi2013,khalidi2018nodes} argues that natural kinds are
   205	nodes in causal structures rather than sets defined by essences. On
   206	this account, a category is theoretically useful when its place in a
   207	causal network supports reliable induction. Applied to interjections,
   208	the claim is that several diagnostics, including prosodic isolation,
   209	syntactic independence, non-referentiality, non-inflection, and
   210	interactional force, cohere because several causal pathways converge on
   211	the same pragmatic node. The resulting profile is thin but projectible, a
   212	claim Section~\ref{sec:proj-domain} makes good.
   213	
   214	Four things have to be kept apart, because they fail
   215	separately\ifblind\else\ \citep{reynolds2026projectibilityHistory}\fi. Each
   216	has a label I'll use throughout.
   217	
   218	\begin{itemize}
   219	\item The \term{projective declaration} states the claim: what's observed,
   220	  what's predicted from it, and over which cases. Here the observed source is
   221	  some part of the cluster (say prosodic separation and morphological
   222	  bareness), the predicted outcome is the rest of it (supplement function,
   223	  non-referential or use-conditional meaning, sequential availability), the
   224	  unit is a lexical item in a grammatical environment rather than a word
   225	  family, and the population is present-day English. The predicted outcome
   226	  has to be something other than the property that admitted the form, or the
   227	  prediction only restates membership.
   228	\item \term{Warrant} is the separate question of why that inference is
   229	  licensed and how strongly: what evidence supports it, and what would defeat
   230	  it. Table~\ref{tab:link-status} records this link by link.
   231	\item The profile just defined is the worldly relation the declaration posits,
   232	  and \term{stability} is the further claim that it recurs across forms and
   233	  over time. \term{Network order} is a claim of a different kind: that the
   234	  properties are linked, so that one makes another more available.
   235	  \emph{Stability and network order are independent.} A combination can
   236	  recur without revealing any direction among its parts, and a directed
   237	  dependence can hold without recurring.
   238	\item \term{Grounds} explain why the profile obtains or
   239	  persists\ifblind\else\ \citep{reynolds2026notEveryStableCluster}\fi.
   240	  \term{Stabilizers} are grounds that hold the combination together as forms
   241	  enter and leave. \term{Corrective control} is the strongest, a stabilizer
   242	  that restores the combination when something disturbs it. Only that would
   243	  license calling the cluster \term{homeostatic}, Boyd's term
   244	  \citep{boyd1999}, and the evidence here doesn't establish
   245	  it.
   246	\end{itemize}

   308	Observing that \mention{gee} is set off from what follows and never inflects,
   309	with no *\mention{geed} and no *\mention{gees}, licenses expectations not yet
   310	checked: that it will work as a supplement, that its contribution will be
   311	use-conditional rather than descriptive, and that it will turn up at the front
   312	of a turn. Those expectations would be defeated if \mention{gee} began taking a
   313	complement, on the model of *\mention{gee that it's expensive}, or acquired
   314	inflection.
   315	
   316	Diachrony matters because pragmatic profiles have histories. Forms such
   317	as \mention{gee}, \mention{damn}, or \mention{bruh} change category
   318	position as historically contentful or addressive expressions come to
   319	manage stance, sequence, and interactional alignment in recurrent
   320	contexts \citep[cf.][]{juckertaavitsainen2013}. Change over time helps
   321	explain why the pragmatic profile is stable while individual forms enter,
   322	leave, and retain source-specific residues.
   323	
   324	The network classifies lexical items in grammatical environments, not
   325	word families as wholes. A word family can contain distinct historically
   326	related lexemes. The \mention{damn} word family includes verbal
   327	\mention{damn} in \mention{I damn you} and standalone interjectional
   328	\mention{damn!}. Prenominal \mention{damn} in \mention{the damn car}
   329	belongs to that family too, but the analysis needn't decide here whether
   330	it's a noun-phrase-internal extension of the interjection lexeme or a
   331	separate adjectival lexeme. What matters is that it's syntactically
   332	integrated inside a noun phrase while retaining use-conditional
   333	expressive force.
   334	
   335	Three nodes in that network are in play here: a syntactic node, a semantic or
   336	use-conditional node, and an interactional-pragmatic node. The label
   337	\enquote{interjection} covers the forms that occupy all three at once. I write these
   338	as \intj{syn},
   339	\intj{sem}, and
   340	\intj{prag}. The sections below first
   341	describe the diagnostic cluster, then explain why these nodes overlap
   342	and why they come apart.
   343	
   344	Section~\ref{sec:cluster} documents the cluster.
   345	Section~\ref{sec:causal-framework} introduces the causal-network
   346	framework that explains why the interjection profile supports pragmatic
   347	inference. The rest of the paper identifies the network order that
   348	structures the cluster, extends \textcite{gehweiler2008}'s single-case
   349	analysis to a general claim about path dependence, and argues that
   350	interjections exhibit a distinctive form of projectibility, primarily
   351	pragmatic rather than truth-conditional.
   352	
   353	\section{The property cluster}\label{sec:cluster}
   354	
   355	English interjections exhibit a cluster of co-occurring properties. This
   356	section takes phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics, and pragmatics in turn,
   357	drawing primarily on the empirical base documented in \textcite{ameka1992},
   358	\textcite{quirk1985}, and \textcite{HuddlestonPullum2002}. It answers two
   359	questions: what properties cluster, and what local diagnostic inferences each
   360	property supports. These inferences show that individual properties are
   361	structured rather than accidental. Six properties emerge: prosodic isolation,
   362	non-inflection, syntactic non-integration, non-referentiality, emotive or
   363	stance force, and sequential use, the last being the capacity to constitute a
   364	conversational move on its own. The three nodes cut across those five rather than regrouping them, because
   365	each field needs the category to predict something different. The subscripts
   366	are used from here on, with assignment criteria given in
   367	Section~\ref{sec:cooccurrence} where the diagnostic matrix needs them, and
   368	the argument for treating the nodes as three deferred to
   369	Section~\ref{sec:fieldrelative-nodes}. Section~\ref{sec:projectibility} asks
   370	the stronger question: when does a partial observation license inference
   371	across linguistic domains? The causal question, why these properties
   372	travel together, is deferred to Section~\ref{sec:causal-structure}.
   373	
   374	\subsection{Phonology}\label{sec:phonology}
   375	
   376	Interjections are prosodically isolated, and \textcite[108]{ameka1992}
   377	states it without hedging: they're \enquote{always separated by a pause from
   378	the other utterances with which they may co-occur} and \enquote{always
   379	constitute an intonation unit by themselves}. I treat the property as
   380	strongly characteristic rather than exceptionless, because the diagnostic
   381	matrix below records partial cases (\mention{um}). That gap between a
   382	categorical statement and its boundary cases is the pattern
   383	Section~\ref{sec:cooccurrence} takes up. In writing, this isolation surfaces as punctuation
   384	(commas, dashes, exclamation marks), setting the interjection off from the
   385	clause it accompanies \citep[1350]{HuddlestonPullum2002}.
   386	
   387	The corpus evidence below is written, so prosodic isolation is tracked
   388	through proxies: punctuation, standalone position, and absence of
   389	integration into the host clause. These proxies are useful for historical
   390	and large-corpus work, but they're proxies rather than direct acoustic
   391	evidence. They aren't arbitrary ones.
   392	On \textcite[41]{Jackendoff2025Parenthetical}'s account a parenthetical is
   393	realized as an intonation phrase, so it ends in a boundary tone made of
   394	\enquote{some combination of pause, lengthening, pitch discontinuity}, and the
   395	flanking commas are what record those boundaries. Punctuation tracks prosodic
   396	isolation because it notates the same breaks.
   397	
   398	Interjections may also contain phonemes outside the
   399	speaker's dialect. The interjection \mention{uh-oh} ([\ipa{əʔo}]) is one of
   400	the few cases where a glottal stop appears in dialects that otherwise lack
   401	it. Other atypical sounds include the alveolar clicks in
   402	\mention{tut-tut} ([\ipa{ǀǀ}]), the voiceless bilabial fricative in
   403	\mention{whew} ([\ipa{ɸɪu}]), and (for dialects that no longer use it)
   404	the voiceless velar fricative in \mention{ugh} ([\ipa{əx}])
   405	\citep[853]{quirk1985}. Spelling pronunciations often arise for these forms
   406	(\mention{tut-tut} said [\ipa{tət tət}], \mention{ugh} said [\ipa{əɡ}]),
   407	suggesting that the atypical phonemes are marked even for
   408	native speakers.
   409	
   410	Prosodic isolation is a strong cue: if
   411	a novel form occupies its own intonation unit and resists integration into
   412	the surrounding prosodic contour, a listener can predict syntactic
   413	non-integration and non-referentiality. The atypical phonemes reinforce this
   414	inference. But prosodic isolation isn't unique to interjections, since
   415	vocatives, appositives, and other supplements share it \citep[cf.][]{wilkins1992}. What
   416	distinguishes interjections is the convergence of prosodic isolation with
   417	the properties below.
   418	
   419	\subsection{Morphology}
   420	
   421	English interjections characteristically don't inflect or derive. They
   422	resist the morphological operations that characterize other open-class
   423	categories: no plural (*\mention{ouches}), no tense (*\mention{ouched}),
   424	no comparative (*\mention{oucher}), no nominalization
   425	(*\mention{ouchness}) \citep[106]{ameka1992}. Since
   426	\textcite{libert2019} notes that \enquote{few, if any, papers focus on
   427	the morphology of interjections}, the apparent absence of morphological
   428	productivity may partly reflect a gap in the research rather than a
   429	firm empirical generalization.

   446	\subsection{Syntax}\label{sec:syntax}
   447	
   448	Interjections characteristically function as \term{supplements},
   449	\enquote{elements which occupy a position in linear sequence without
   450	being integrated into the syntactic structure of the sentence}
   451	\citep[1350]{HuddlestonPullum2002}. In \mention{Damn, we're going to be late!}, the
   452	interjection isn't a complement or modifier of anything in the clause.
   453	It's appended to the beginning as a parenthetical.
   454	
   455	Supplement function isn't unique to interjections. Relative clauses, noun
   456	phrases, adjective phrases, and adverb phrases can all function as
   457	supplements \citep[1356--1360]{HuddlestonPullum2002}:
   458	
   459	\ea\label{ex:supplements}
   460	  \ea \exmention{\exfocus{Ah}, so you were there after all!} \hfill (interjection, p.~1360)
   461	  \ex \exmention{We called in to see Sue's parents, \exfocus{which made us rather late}.} \hfill (relative clause, p.~1356)
   462	  \ex \exmention{A university lecturer, \exfocus{Dr Brown}, was arrested for the crime.} \hfill (noun phrase, p.~1357)
   463	  \z
   464	\z
   465	What distinguishes interjection supplements is their combination with
   466	the other cluster properties: non-referentiality, non-inflection,
   467	prosodic isolation, and emotive force. The function is shared; the
   468	category differs.
   469	
   470	A handful of interjections exceptionally take complements.
   471	\textcite[1361fn]{HuddlestonPullum2002} analyse \mention{damn} in
   472	\mention{damn these mosquitoes!} as the head of an interjection phrase
   473	(IntjP) taking a noun phrase (NP) complement, one of the very few cases where an
   474	interjection heads a phrase larger than a single word. But even here the syntax is
   475	constrained: no subject is present or intended, and there's no tense
   476	inflection. The construction is better treated as a residue of verb
   477	syntax than as full verbal behaviour, a point that matters for the
   478	recruitment pathways
   479	discussed in Section~\ref{sec:causal-structure}. A second kind of residue is
   480	internal rather than external. \mention{Goddammit} preserves a
   481	verb-object-pronoun structure inside what is now a single word, so the syntax
   482	survives as fossilized material rather than as anything the form can still do.
   483	\textcite[40]{cuenca2002} reads it the same way from the grammaticalization
   484	side, listing \mention{Goddammit} among the cases where change in function and
   485	meaning is \enquote{accompanied by phonetic weakness and word integration}. (The internal structure of
   486	lexicalized pairs like
   487	\mention{oh boy} and \mention{holy cow} is less clear and won't be
   488	resolved here.)
   489	
   490	Supplement function predicts prosodic isolation and resistance to
   491	syntactic integration:
   492	
   493	\ea\label{ex:syntactic-predictions}
   494	  \ea[*]{\exmention{I think that damn we're going to be late.}}
   495	  \ex \exmention{I think that, damn, we're going to be late.}
   496	  \ex[*]{\exmention{Damn and quickly, we left.}}
   497	  \z
   498	\z
   499	The contrast in (\ref{ex:syntactic-predictions}) is prosodic rather than
   500	syntactic: the first two differ only in whether \mention{damn} is set off.
   501	\textcite[41--42]{Jackendoff2025Parenthetical} generalizes the point beyond
   502	interjections as the IP-Break Hypothesis: placement tracks intonational-phrase
   503	breaks rather than syntactic constituency, so a host admits a parenthetical
   504	where it independently permits an IP break, provided the parenthetical is
   505	itself licensed in that position \citep[43]{Jackendoff2025Parenthetical}. He gives pairs of hosts that are
   506	\enquote{syntactically identical} but differ in where a parenthetical may go,
   507	since \enquote{the difference in the parenthetical's position is a consequence
   508	of the difference in position of the IP breaks in the host}
   509	\citep[42]{Jackendoff2025Parenthetical}.
   510	
   511	Supplement function also predicts a semantic consequence: if the form is
   512	syntactically non-integrated, its contribution is independent of the
   513	proposition expressed by the host clause. Remove the interjection and the
   514	truth conditions don't change, the hallmark of non-truth-conditional
   515	meaning \citep[cf.][166]{potts2007}.

   517	\subsection{Semantics}\label{sec:semantics}
   518	
   519	English interjections characteristically don't refer. Here,
   520	\term{non-referentiality} means absence of ordinary entity/event
   521	denotation and argumental construal while allowing semantic content.
   522	Nouns and verbs typically pick out entities or events in the world.
   523	Interjections express internal states of their users
   524	\citep{leech2006}: anger (\mention{damn}),
   525	disgust (\mention{eww}, \mention{yuck}), surprise (\mention{wow}), regret
   526	(\mention{alas}), pain (\mention{ouch}), realization (\mention{eureka}).
   527	Some interjections are more propositional than emotive: \mention{yes}
   528	and \mention{no} affirm or deny propositions, and \mention{oh} can
   529	signal recognition of new information rather than expressing feeling.
   530	
   531	\textcite{wierzbicka1992} argues that interjections have genuine
   532	semantic content, decomposable via Natural Semantic Metalanguage. The
   533	forms may have content, and \mention{yes} and \mention{no} may operate
   534	on propositions, while still lacking ordinary entity/event denotation.
   535	
   536	\textcite[119]{wilkins1992} presses harder, and his title states the
   537	objection: interjections are deictics. On his account they \enquote{convey
   538	complete propositions}, the \enquote{referential arguments in the
   539	propositions conveyed by interjections are provided by context}, and so
   540	interjections are \enquote{shifters (indexicals) by virtue of
   541	being built semantically out of basic deictic elements} such as
   542	\mention{I}, \mention{you}, \mention{now}, and \mention{here}. If that's
   543	right, calling interjections non-referential looks like a straightforward
   544	mistake.
   545	
   546	The two claims are about different things, and Wilkins's own wording shows
   547	it: the referential arguments are supplied by context, not by the form.
   548	Non-referentiality, as I use it, is a claim about what a form contributes on
   549	its own, so it leaves the contextual filling untouched. \mention{Ouch!} picks
   550	out no thing or event, and resists being made definite, quantified over, or
   551	used as an argument, whatever indexical structure its explication turns out to
   552	contain. If anything Wilkins asks more of the semantics than I do, since he has
   553	to say what it supplies for the filling to work at all, and the distributional
   554	facts I rely on hold on his account as readily as on mine. What his account does show is that
   555	\enquote{non-referential} is a poor name for the property, kept here only
   556	because it's the established one.
   557	
   558	Not everyone reads Wilkins that way. On the conceptualist analyses of
   559	\textcite{wierzbicka1992}, \textcite{ameka1992} and Wilkins, interjections
   560	encode substantial conceptual structure. \textcite[211]{wharton2000} argues
   561	from relevance theory that they encode considerably less, being
   562	\enquote{partly natural and partly coded} and falling \enquote{at various
   563	points along a continuum between showing and saying}. That
   564	dispute doesn't need settling here. Neither Wilkins's deictic slots nor
   565	Wharton's activated attitudinal concepts give \mention{ouch!} a thing or event
   566	to pick out, so the distributional property holds on either analysis. But
   567	Wharton's continuum is worth noting for a second reason. What varies along it
   568	is how much of an interjection's import is coded rather than shown, which is a
   569	gradient within the category and not a blurred edge to it
   570	(Section~\ref{sec:cooccurrence}).
   571	
   572	\textcite[241]{cruz2009} carries the dispute further. He argues that the
   573	heterogeneity of interjections, \enquote{with items incorporated from other
   574	lexical categories}, together with the openness of the class, \enquote{could
   575	indicate the existence of a continuum of more and less conceptual items}, and
   576	that the conceptual ones encode \enquote{not full concepts, but some schematic
   577	material requiring subsequent pragmatic adjustments}. What matters here isn't
   578	the continuum, which Wharton already supplies, but its cause. Cruz derives
   579	gradedness from recruitment out of other categories and from open-class
   580	coinage, which are the mechanisms Section~\ref{sec:causal-structure} treats as
   581	the cluster's sources.
   582	
   583	Wierzbicka's explications characterize the use-conditional content that
   584	\intj{sem} (Section~\ref{sec:fieldrelative-nodes})
   585	tracks. Non-referentiality is a cluster property rather than a
   586	definition.
   587	
   588	The contrast with nouns is sharpest where both share a lexical source.
   589	\mention{Jesus} as a noun refers to a person; \mention{Jesus!} as an
   590	interjection doesn't. In the interjection use, as
   591	\textcite[88]{gehweiler2008} puts it, \mention{Jesus} \enquote{has lost
   592	its propositional content, i.e.\ it is no longer used to refer and hence
   593	no longer influences truth conditions}.
   594	This semantic shift is part of the recruitment pathway from noun to
   595	interjection (Section~\ref{sec:causal-structure}).
   596	
   597	The projectibility here is mostly negative. A form that doesn't refer
   598	resists being made definite, quantified over, or used as an argument:
   599	*\mention{the damn!}, *\mention{every ouch}, *\mention{Damn surprised me},
   600	and *\mention{There's damn}. These restrictions are non-trivial because
   601	they distinguish interjections from nouns that have undergone only partial
   602	bleaching. Section~\ref{sec:synchronic-coupling} treats the same relation from
   603	the causal side: non-referentiality helps keep the form out of
   604	argument structure, which in turn supports prosodic isolation.
   605	
   606	\subsection{Pragmatics}\label{sec:pragmatics}
   607	
   608	Interjections typically carry exclamatory or directive force: \mention{ouch!}
   609	expresses pain, \mention{shh!} demands silence \citep[88]{quirk1985}. But
   610	the pragmatic range extends beyond exclamation. How far beyond is worth a
   611	number. \textcite[18]{elffers2008} reports that in Hofstede's analysis of a
   612	corpus of spoken Dutch, only 7.04\,\% of interjections
   613	\enquote{turned out to fulfil an expressive illocutionary function},
   614	against 52\,\% classed as expositive, which he glosses as directive within the
   615	conversation. The corpus is Dutch rather than English, so the figure indicates
   616	rather than establishes. It points the same way as Dingemanse's interactional
   617	focus. The emotive characterization grammars lead with covers the minority
   618	case. Interjections greet
   619	(\mention{hey}, and, at the boundary with routine formulae,
   620	\mention{hello}; see Section~\ref{sec:boundaries}), mark agreement (\mention{yes},
   621	\mention{amen}), indicate understanding (\mention{oh}, \mention{uh-huh}),
   622	and signal backchanneling, the listener's ongoing attention to a speaker's
   623	turn \citep{dingemanse2020, stivers2019}.
   624	
   625	The most common interjections tagged in the Corpus of Global Web-Based
   626	English \citep[GloWbE;][]{daviesfuchs2015} illustrate this pragmatic breadth: \mention{yes}, \mention{no},
   627	\mention{oh}, \mention{yeah}, \mention{hi}, \mention{hey}, \mention{wow},
   628	\mention{hello}, \mention{ah}, \mention{ha}. The list is dominated by
   629	interactional items, forms whose primary function is to manage the
   630	conversation rather than to contribute propositional content.
   631	\textcite[171]{poggi2009} presses the other way. An interjection, on her
   632	analysis, is a holophrastic signal whose equivalent is not a word but
   633	\enquote{a communicative act including the meaning of both a performative and
   634	a propositional content}, so that \mention{ouch!} paraphrases as \enquote{I am
   635	feeling pain}. The grain distinction from Section~\ref{sec:semantics} applies
   636	again. Poggi's propositional content belongs to the act the interjection
   637	performs, not to any clause it helps compose. Her contrast between holophrastic
   638	and articulated signals says why. Nouns and verbs characteristically convey part of a
   639	sentence, an interjection a whole one. Prenominal \mention{damn} in
   640	\mention{the damn car} is an interjection that doesn't. That explains the independence noted in
   641	Section~\ref{sec:syntax}: a form contributes nothing to a host clause's truth
   642	conditions if it isn't part of a clause at all. Holophrasis accounts for the
   643	property rather than competing with it.
   644	\textcite{dingemanse2024} estimates that roughly one in seven
   645	conversational turns is an interjection, and interactional tools
   646	dominate the most frequent cases: continuers like
   647	\mention{mm-hm} that scaffold extended turns, repair initiators like
   648	\mention{huh?} that calibrate mutual understanding, and change-of-state
   649	tokens like \mention{oh} that display evolving knowledge\footnote{%
   650	  \term{Token} does double duty in work of this kind, and I keep the senses
   651	  apart. In \term{change-of-state token} and \term{response token} it's the
   652	  interactional-linguistic sense: a minimal form deployed as a move in a
   653	  sequence. Elsewhere I use it for the unit of analysis, a lexical item in a
   654	  grammatical environment as against the lexeme that labels a family of
   655	  historically related such items.}
   656	\citep[cf.][]{norrick2009}.
   657	
   658	The boundary with \term{routine formulae} is instructive.
   659	\textcite[2--3]{coulmas1981} defines routine formulae as
   660	% "conventionalised" is Coulmas's British spelling, preserved as quoted;
   661	% verified via Ameka's verbatim reproduction at p. 108, same page range.
   662	\enquote{highly conventionalised prepatterned expressions whose occurrence
   663	is tied to more or less standard communication situations}:
   664	\mention{bye}, \mention{hello}, \mention{sorry}, \mention{thank you}.
   665	\textcite[109--110]{ameka1992} argues these differ from interjections in
   666	three ways: they include addressees, they're predictable responses to social
   667	conventions, and they're always speech acts rather than mere reflections of
   668	the speaker's mental state. \textcite[142]{wilkins1992} counters that these
   669	differences make routine formulae \enquote{a distinct pragmatic and semantic
   670	subtype of interjections}, not a separate category.
   671	
   672	The disagreement is field-relative in a causal-network sense. For a
   673	grammar, routine formulae pattern near interjections because they share
   674	prosodic isolation and pragmatic function. For speech-act analysis or
   675	interactional pragmatics, they support different predictions: addressee
   676	orientation, occurrence in standard social sequences, and conventional
   677	felicity conditions. The same form can sit close to one node
   678	for one inquiry and farther away for another, depending on which causal
   679	relations and predictions the inquiry tracks.
   680	
   681	What a projective claim licenses depends on what was observed. Observing a novel
   682	non-propositional stance form licenses expectations about standalone
   683	positioning, supplement-like syntax, backchannel availability, and
   684	resistance to embedding under reported speech (\mention{*She said that
   685	damn}). Observing a novel routine formula licenses expectations about
   686	addressee-directedness and placement in conventional social sequences.
   687	The overlap explains why routine formulae sit at the boundary; the
   688	divergence explains why the boundary is disputed.
   689	Section~\ref{sec:causal-structure} asks which links make those claims
   690	reliable.
   691	
   692	\subsection{Co-occurrence, not necessity}\label{sec:cooccurrence}
   693	
   694	These properties co-occur statistically, not necessarily. The
   695	literature's own definitions hedge, on every criterion they state.
   696	% Verified verbatim against Wilkins 1992: 124 (running head "124 D. P.
   697	% Wilkins / Interjections as deictics"), his numbered definition (1).
   698	% Note: Gehweiler's block quote at p. 73 pluralizes "construction"; the
   699	% singular here follows Wilkins directly.
   700	\textcite[124]{wilkins1992} defines an interjection as
   701	\enquote{a conventional lexical form which (commonly and) conventionally
   702	constitutes an utterance on its own, (typically) does not enter into
   703	construction with other word classes, is (usually) monomorphemic, and
   704	(generally) does not host inflectional or derivational morphemes}. Four
   705	criteria, a parenthetical hedge on each, and
   706	\textcite[73]{gehweiler2008} adopts the definition while calling those
   707	criteria the class's \enquote{obligatory properties}. No single property
   708	is necessary and sufficient.
   709	
   710	One dissent belongs here, because it's the strongest case against that last
   711	sentence. \textcite[282]{libert2012} proposes exactly one criterion:
   712	interjections have a \term{conversational valence} of~1, meaning they can be
   713	used felicitously with no addressee present, where \mention{goodbye} requires
   714	one and so has a valence of~2. If you stub your toe you can say
   715	\mention{ouch!} alone. On this account interjections are
   716	\enquote{a homogeneous word class, clearly delimited by a sole criterion}
   717	\citep[294]{libert2012}.
   718	
   719	The criterion works. What it costs is visible in how Libert reaches it, by
   720	redefinition: interjections \enquote{as I have redefined them, have
   721	only one function (as `expressions of feeling'); the function of `marks of
   722	address' is undertaken by another class} \citep[294]{libert2012}. The
   723	addressing uses are moved out of the category rather than shown to satisfy
   724	the criterion, and they take a good deal with them: \mention{hey},
   725	\mention{shh}, and \mention{psst}, and also the continuers, repair
   726	initiators, and response tokens of Section~\ref{sec:pragmatics}, all of
   727	which need someone to be there. A single property is necessary and
   728	sufficient for what's left.
   729	
   730	I take that as support for the decomposition proposed below rather than as a
   731	counterexample to co-occurrence. Libert's line between expressions of
   732	feeling and marks of address falls close to the line this paper draws
   733	between \intj{sem} and
   734	\intj{prag}, and he credits
   735	\textcite[109]{ameka1992} with the same intuition while objecting, fairly,
   736	that Ameka's gloss of conative interjections as \enquote{directed at} but
   737	not \enquote{addressed to} people is obscure. So the disagreement isn't
   738	about the facts. It's about what to do with them: Libert narrows the
   739	category until one criterion suffices, and I keep the wider class and let
   740	the properties co-occur without any one of them being definitional.
   741	That isn't a bare preference. Categories in this tradition are established by
   742	clusters of grammatical properties rather than by single criteria, since a
   743	definition has to identify \enquote{the grammatical properties that distinguish
   744	them from the concepts with which they contrast}
   745	\citep[30]{HuddlestonPullum2002}. A single criterion would make interjection
   746	the only category in the inventory picked out that way, which is a reason to
   747	doubt the standard rather than the category.
   748	
   749	Co-occurrence without necessity gets read as fuzziness, so it's worth saying
   750	what it doesn't commit me to. It doesn't make membership a matter of degree.
   751	Each diagnostic is determinate for a given form in a given environment:
   752	\mention{damn} in \mention{damn these mosquitoes!} either takes a complement or
   753	it doesn't, \mention{heavens} either carries the plural suffix or it doesn't,
   754	and Table~\ref{tab:diagnostic-matrix} records values rather than degrees.
   755	Determinacy is what makes the rest of the paper possible. The residues of
   756	Section~\ref{sec:residues} and the turnover probes of
   757	Section~\ref{sec:turnover} both measure a boundary moving, and a boundary can
   758	only be seen to move if it sits somewhere in particular at each time.
   759	
   760	What varies is typicality, not membership. Members differ in how many cluster
   761	properties they carry and in which field-relative nodes they satisfy, so the
   762	predictions membership licenses vary in strength, and a form near a boundary is
   763	more exposed to drift than one sitting well inside. That puts the gradience in
   764	stability rather than in membership, which is where the causal story of
   765	Section~\ref{sec:causal-structure} can act on it.
   766	
   767	What's fluid is the edge across time, and \textcite[144]{boyd1999} builds that
   768	in. \enquote{The properties that determine the conditions for falling under} a
   769	kind term \enquote{may vary over time (or space)} while the term keeps its
   770	definition, which he calls \enquote{the historicity of the individuation
   771	conditions for the definitional property cluster}. His target is the demand
   772	that kinds be defined by \enquote{unchanging necessary and sufficient
   773	membership conditions} \citep[164]{boyd1999}, a demand about permanence rather
   774	than about precision.
   775	
   776	Boyd doesn't make the further commitment to sharpness. He treats the
   777	extensional vagueness of a property-cluster kind as irresolvable in principle,
   778	which is indeterminacy rather than inaccessibility. I assume instead that the edge
   779	is sharp but not finitely locatable, and that the fuzzy appearance comes from
   780	measurement and from aggregation across speakers \citep{reynolds2025sorites}.
   781	Two qualifications stop that assumption doing more work than it should. The
   782	sharpness would have to be maintained rather than stipulated, by acquisition,
   783	entrenchment, alignment, and transmission, which makes how sharp a boundary is
   784	an empirical question about how strong those pressures are. And aggregation
   785	blurs: a population of speakers with sharp but individually placed thresholds
   786	behaves like a category with a fuzzy edge, so the corpus gradience reported in
   787	Section~\ref{sec:discussion} is neutral between the two pictures rather than
   788	evidence for either. None of the arguments here need the stronger claim. They
   789	need only what the previous paragraph asserts, that each diagnostic have a
   790	determinate value for a given form in a given environment.
   791	
   792	The pattern calls for explanation. Network order couples the
   793	properties: prosodic isolation reinforces syntactic non-integration,
   794	non-referentiality removes the need for argument structure, and
   795	pragmatic conventionalization entrenches the emotive function.
   796	
   797	The coupling is visible when it breaks. \mention{Damn} as an interjection
   798	(\mention{Damn!}) has the full cluster: prosodic isolation, syntactic
   799	non-integration, non-referentiality, and emotive force. Prenominal
   800	\mention{damn} in \mention{the damn car} retains emotive force, and with it
   801	non-inflection and non-referentiality, but loses prosodic isolation and
   802	syntactic non-integration together, because it's embedded inside a noun
   803	phrase \citep[cf.][]{potts2007}. Those two go together or not at all, which
   804	is what a coupling link predicts (Section~\ref{sec:synchronic-coupling})
   805	and what a bare list of six properties would leave as coincidence. A bare property list can't explain this
   806	pattern; the links between the properties can.
   807	
   808	Table~\ref{tab:diagnostic-matrix} makes the cluster inspectable.
   809	Each row gives a lexical item in a grammatical environment rather than
   810	a word family as a whole (see Section~\ref{sec:intro}), and the columns show its
   811	profile against the six cluster properties and the three field-relative
   812	nodes previewed in the introduction and defined in
   813	Section~\ref{sec:fieldrelative-nodes}. The table
   814	shows convergence and decoupling. Standalone \mention{damn!} and
   815	\mention{oh} show convergence. In \mention{the damn car}, prenominal
   816	\mention{damn} has the semantic node but lacks the syntactic one.
   817	\mention{mm-hm} has the interactional node, while its syntactic status
   818	is debatable. Boundary items (\mention{bye}, \mention{um}) share some
   819	but not all cluster properties, the pattern that
   820	Section~\ref{sec:boundaries} explains as causal-link overlap.
   821	
   822	These six recur across the sections above, but they aren't a complete
   823	inventory: free coinage by onomatopoeia distinguishes the category without
   824	belonging to any single item, so it has no column here. The last three columns
   825	are separate classifications, not sums of the first six, so they need their own
   826	criteria. A construction is
   827	\intjshort{syn} if it functions as a supplement, taking no
   828	complement and forming no constituent with neighbouring material;
   829	\intjshort{sem} if its contribution is expressive and
   830	use-conditional, leaving the truth conditions of its host untouched;
   831	\intjshort{prag} if it is usable on its own as an interactional move, which is the sequential-use column read as a field-relative classification.
   832	The semantic criterion is about meaning type rather than position, which is
   833	why a construction can satisfy it while sitting inside a host clause.
   834	Section~\ref{sec:fieldrelative-nodes} argues for the three-way division; here
   835	the columns record it.

   903	\section{The causal-network framework}\label{sec:causal-framework}
   904	
   905	The property cluster documented above has the shape
   906	Section~\ref{sec:cooccurrence} described: statistically reliable co-occurrence,
   907	no necessary and sufficient conditions, disputed boundaries, and hedged
   908	universals. That shape is what any lexical category looks like when no
   909	definition is holding it together. The tradition
   910	drew the natural conclusion, that a class with no definition behind it has
   911	nothing to license, and grammars have said little accordingly
   912	\citep[16]{HuddlestonPullum2005}. The inference doesn't hold. Membership
   913	licenses predictions about use, and those predictions cross phonology, syntax,
   914	semantics, interaction, and diachrony. Causal-network analysis explains why
   915	they're available without treating the category as an essence.
   916	
   917	\subsection{The framework}
   918	
   919	The relations that unify a kind may be local mutual-reinforcement
   920	relations, one-directional pathways, or converging historical routes
   921	\citep{khalidi2013,khalidi2018nodes}. Each of
   922	the three has work to do below: the coupling links of
   923	Section~\ref{sec:synchronic-coupling} are mutual reinforcement, the
   924	non-referentiality ratchet is one-directional, and the recruitment pathways of
   925	Section~\ref{sec:recruitment} are converging historical routes. What matters is
   926	that the category marks a place in a causal network, a position where
   927	properties, processes, and interventions are connected tightly enough to
   928	support induction, which is what lets us predict properties of new instances
   929	from observed properties of old ones.
   930	
   931	I take the structure from Khalidi and not the metaphysics. Nothing below turns
   932	on whether interjection is a kind in a philosopher's sense; what the category
   933	has to be is projectible for linguistic inquiry, a position whose causal links
   934	explain why particular observations support particular predictions. The sharp
   935	edge assumed in Section~\ref{sec:cooccurrence} is no obstacle either, since
   936	\textcite[66]{khalidi2013} holds that cluster kinds \enquote{need not be
   937	structured in such a way as to allow intermediate cases}.
   938	
   939	The four objects distinguished in Section~\ref{sec:intro} are easier to see
   940	running on a single form. Take \mention{oof} in standalone use, as in
   941	(\ref{ex:oof}).
   942	
   943	\ea\label{ex:oof} \exmention{\exfocus{Oof}. That's going to hurt.}
   944	\z
   945	
   946	\begin{itemize}
   947	\item The declaration is what calling it an interjection commits us to: having
   948	  observed that it stands prosodically separate and doesn't inflect, expect it
   949	  to resist determiners, to lack ordinary entity or event denotation, and to
   950	  contribute use-conditionally rather than descriptively, in present-day
   951	  English, in its standalone use rather than across the whole word family.
   952	\item The warrant is whatever supports that expectation, and here it is thin:
   953	  analogy to \mention{ouch} and \mention{wow}, and the links set out below.
   954	\item The profile is the combination the declaration names. Stability and
   955	  network order are further and independent claims: that the combination
   956	  recurs across forms and over time, and that the properties are linked
   957	  rather than merely co-occurring. Neither follows from one form, which is
   958	  what Section~\ref{sec:cluster} and the links below supply.
   959	\item Grounds explain how the form got there and why it stays.
   960	  \textcite{oed_oof} derives \mention{oof} as an imitative formation, which
   961	  puts its entry on the onomatopoeic route of Section~\ref{sec:recruitment}
   962	  rather than on either bleaching path. A stabilizer would be whatever keeps the
   963	  combination together as forms enter and leave, and corrective control would be
   964	  a process that restored it if something pushed \mention{oof} out.
   965	\end{itemize}
   966	
   967	The question is how strong a causal claim the
   968	co-occurrence documented in Section~\ref{sec:cluster} supports. Some relevant properties form local stabilizing relations. Prosodic
   969	isolation and syntactic non-integration reinforce one another: a form
   970	produced as its own intonation unit is less available for complementation
   971	or modification, while a form outside clause structure is more likely to
   972	receive independent prosodic packaging. \mention{Damn!} standing alone has
   973	both properties; prenominal \mention{damn} in \mention{the damn car} has
   974	neither, because sitting inside a noun phrase costs it the intonational
   975	boundary that had kept it out of the clause. Repeated use in that position
   976	can make standalone interpretation increasingly expectable for speakers and
   977	hearers.
   978	
   979	Other processes are interactional or directional. Uptake affects future
   980	use; repeated use affects expectation; expectation affects later uptake.
   981	\mention{Mm-hm} shows the loop closing: it serves as a continuer because
   982	hearers treat it as one, which is why producing it hands the floor back,
   983	which is why it keeps being produced in that slot.
   984	Loss of referential content makes argument structure less motivated;
   985	syntactic deactivation pushes a form toward standalone use; repeated
   986	conversational deployment entrenches stance, response, repair, or
   987	sequence-management functions. These relations explain why the
   988	diagnostics travel together. They establish network order, and they make
   989	maintenance plausible.
   990	
   991	The historical evidence adds a further point. Semantic bleaching,
   992	syntactic deactivation, and onomatopoeic or interactional coinage can all
   993	recruit forms into a similar pragmatic profile, but they don't do so by
   994	the same route or with the same residues. A bleached invocation (\mention{gee}), a
   995	deactivated optative (\mention{damn}), and an onomatopoeic or
   996	interactional coinage (\mention{oof}, \mention{mm-hm}) may
   997	converge on prosodic independence, supplement function,
   998	non-referential or use-conditional meaning, and interactional force while
   999	retaining different traces of their sources. The resulting category is
  1000	organized by causal relations with different temporal and
  1001	explanatory profiles.
  1002	
  1003	That structure is enough for projectibility, whatever domain the
  1004	projections turn out to fall in (Section~\ref{sec:proj-domain}). It isn't
  1005	enough to establish corrective control, since the evidence considered here
  1006	doesn't
  1007	identify a tracked category-level variable, an error signal, or a response
  1008	pathway that restores the interjectional profile when it's perturbed
  1009	\citep[cf.][]{weinberger2026HomeostasisCausalControl}.
  1010	
  1011	\subsection{Causal networks vs.\ prototype theory}\label{sec:prototype}
  1012	
  1013	Prototype theory \citep{rosch1975,taylor2003} was developed for exactly
  1014	the kind of data Section~\ref{sec:cluster} documents, and it handles
  1015	that data well: graded membership, better-and-worse exemplars, and
  1016	fuzzy boundaries via overlapping similarity spaces. It also correctly
  1017	predicts that boundary disputes (interjections vs.\ fillers, vs.\
  1018	routine formulae) should recur instead of being resolvable by
  1019	definition. These are genuine explanatory achievements, and the
  1020	causal-network account doesn't replace them.
  1021	
  1022	The accounts differ in what they buy that data with. Prototype theory buys it
  1023	with degrees of membership. The causal-network account doesn't need them,
  1024	because each field-relative cut is determinate and the disputes concern which
  1025	cut an inquiry should be using (Section~\ref{sec:boundaries}). That's the
  1026	source of a prediction prototype theory doesn't make: apparent gradience
  1027	should decompose under conditioning rather than persist as irreducible
  1028	fuzziness.
  1029	
  1030	A causal-network account adds a different kind of question. Prototype
  1031	theory explains why borderline cases should be expected: categories are
  1032	organized around better and worse exemplars. It doesn't explain why
  1033	the properties cluster in this particular pattern or what should happen
  1034	when one of their links is disrupted.
  1035	
  1036	The difference matters because the causal-network account generates
  1037	predictions about change, conditioning, and residues. It predicts that
  1038	forms recruited by different pathways should retain different traces of
  1039	their source constructions, that apparent gradience should decompose
  1040	under variety conditioning, and that some properties may be more central
  1041	to network order than others (hub-property asymmetry). These
  1042	predictions are probed for interjections in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.
  1043	
  1044	Pathway residues provide a further discriminator. If the proposed causal
  1045	links are real, messy boundary cases shouldn't be messy in arbitrary
  1046	ways. Retained complements should point to verbal sources; euphemistic
  1047	phonological deformation should point to taboo-managed invocative
  1048	sources; regional concentration should point to regional or indexical
  1049	recruitment. Prototype theory offers no analogous retrodictive test.
  1050	
  1051	\subsection{What causal structure adds}
  1052	
  1053	Several analytical stances generate different predictions for
  1054	interjections. The weakest is a \term{property list}: interjections share
  1055	prosodic isolation, non-inflection, non-referentiality, and emotive
  1056	force. That's descriptively useful, but it makes no prediction about
  1057	what happens when one property changes.
  1058	
  1059	Feature specifications are stronger alternatives, for example
  1060	[\textsc{+peripheral}, \textsc{-argument-taking},
  1061	\textsc{+expressive}]. This generates some of the same distributional
  1062	consequences: no inflection follows from the absence of phi-features,
  1063	and no argument-taking follows from the relevant syntactic feature
  1064	bundle. But feature specifications treat those consequences as
  1065	entailments of the feature bundle, not as effects of identifiable
  1066	processes.
  1067	
  1068	A constructional account is a stronger competitor. It can represent
  1069	many of the same pairings of form, meaning, and use
  1070	\citep{goldberg1995constructions,goldberg2006,croft2001}. The
  1071	causal-network claim is compatible with that representational move. It
  1072	asks a different question: which positions in a constructional inventory
  1073	are projectible, why do their properties cohere, and which changes
  1074	should make them come apart? Construction grammar models the inventory;
  1075	the projectibility question asks where the inventory supports
  1076	cross-constructional and cross-field inference. The two main pressure
  1077	points are pathway residues and field-relative decomposition. If those
  1078	payoffs fail under further testing, the causal-network account collapses
  1079	to a descriptive inventory rather than an explanatory category analysis.
  1080	
  1081	The causal-network account adds five claims that property lists, feature
  1082	bundles, and constructional representations don't settle by themselves:
  1083	
  1084	\begin{enumerate}
  1085	\item Co-movement. Prosodic isolation, supplement function,
  1086	  non-referentiality, and pragmatic force recur in a patterned way. The
  1087	  account asks which links explain why observing one property licenses
  1088	  expectations about another.
  1089	\item Asymmetry. Some properties are upstream or more diagnostic than
  1090	  others. Prosodic packaging can make syntactic integration less
  1091	  available, while the reverse relation has a different status. A flat
  1092	  feature bundle can't represent that ordering.
  1093	\item Persistence. The category profile can remain available while
  1094	  individual forms enter and leave, which is a claim about stability rather
  1095	  than about what maintains it.
  1096	\item Boundary behaviour. Different recruitment pathways should leave
  1097	  different residues at the category boundary. Feature theory can list
  1098	  residual properties but doesn't explain why they align with
  1099	  particular histories.
  1100	\item Failure. When a prediction fails, the pattern of failure should
  1101	  identify which link or declared inference has broken. If \mention{fie}
  1102	  loses all three nodes together under community-wide attrition, the
  1103	  result is a boundary condition on ordered erosion rather than evidence of corrective
  1104	  control.
  1105	\end{enumerate}
  1106	
  1107	If a feature-based account can replicate co-movement, asymmetry,
  1108	persistence, boundary behaviour, and failure without network order,
  1109	causal structure is doing no additional explanatory work. The empirical
  1110	pressure point is whether the probes in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}
  1111	support those claims.
  1112	
  1113	\subsection{Three field-relative nodes}\label{sec:fieldrelative-nodes}
  1114	
  1115	The causal-network account developed by
  1116	\textcite{khalidi2013,khalidi2018nodes} also explains why
  1117	projectibility is field-relative. If kinds are nodes in causal
  1118	structures, different fields may track different nodes because they ask
  1119	different explanatory and predictive questions. The resulting categories
  1120	remain realist: each inquiry tracks the causal relations that matter for
  1121	its explanations and predictions.
  1122	
  1123	The familiar linguistic parallel is the interrogative/question
  1124	distinction. Syntacticians track \term{interrogative}: a clause type
  1125	with properties such as subject--auxiliary inversion, fronting of
  1126	interrogative words, and polar-question syntax. Pragmatists track \term{question}:
  1127	a speech act that requests information, makes an answer
  1128	relevant, and organizes uptake. The two often coincide, and they also come apart:
  1129	\mention{Can you pass the salt?} is syntactically interrogative and
  1130	pragmatically a request, while \mention{I wonder where she went} can
  1131	raise an information issue without having interrogative clause syntax.
  1132	
  1133	Interjections create a similar problem with less terminological help.
  1134	Syntax, semantics, and interactional pragmatics all use the
  1135	word \enquote{interjection}, but each field tracks a slightly different
  1136	node: different properties, different causal links, different
  1137	predictions. I distinguish these with subscripts:%
  1138	\footnote{The interrogative/question contrast uses different words for
  1139	  the syntactic and pragmatic nodes because both terms are established
  1140	  in their respective traditions. For interjections,
  1141	  every field uses the same word, so subscripts are the honest notation.
  1142	  An alternative would have been to adopt distinct terms from the
  1143	  relevant literatures: \term{expressive} from \textcite{potts2007}
  1144	  for the semantic node, and response token from
  1145	  \textcite{stivers2019} or \term{liminal sign} from
  1146	  \textcite{dingemanse2020} for the interactional-pragmatic node.
  1147	
  1148	  The Latin
  1149	  \mention{ejaculatio}, a throwing out, mirroring \mention{interjicere},
  1150	  \enquote{to throw between} \citep{libert2019},
  1151	  would have been etymologically perfect
  1152	  for the interactional-pragmatic category, but modern English has
  1153	  rendered the term unavailable.}
  1154	
  1155	\begin{itemize}
  1156	\item \intj{syn}, the syntactician's
  1157	  category (\textit{CGEL}, Quirk et al.). Distributional cluster: supplement
  1158	  function, non-inflecting, non-argument-taking. Linked by
  1159	  prosodic-syntactic coupling (Section~\ref{sec:synchronic-coupling}).
  1160	\item \intj{sem}, the semanticist's
  1161	  category, overlapping with \textcite{potts2007}'s \term{expressives}.
  1162	  Use-conditional meaning cluster: independent of at-issue content,
  1163	  nondisplaceable, perspective-dependent, descriptively ineffable
  1164	  \citep[166]{potts2007}. Often recruited through semantic bleaching.
  1165	  Different extension: prenominal \mention{damn} in
  1166	  \mention{the damn car} is \intj{sem} but not
  1167	  \intj{syn}.
  1168	\item \intj{prag}, the interactional-pragmatic
  1169	  category, overlapping with \textcite{stivers2019}'s
  1170	  response tokens and \textcite{dingemanse2020}'s liminal signs.
  1171	  Sequential/stance cluster: turn-initial, backchannel, stance display.
  1172	  Linked by routine entrenchment and possibly by regional/indexical
  1173	  conditioning, both from Section~\ref{sec:synchronic-coupling}. Different
  1174	  extension: \mention{mm-hm} is
  1175	  \intj{prag} but debatably
  1176	  \intj{syn}.
  1177	\end{itemize}

  1224	\section[Causal structure]{Causal structure: recruitment and coupling}\label{sec:causal-structure}
  1225	
  1226	The property cluster documented in Section~\ref{sec:cluster} raises two
  1227	questions that co-occurrence alone can't answer: how do items enter the
  1228	interjectional region of the network, and which causal links make the
  1229	properties travel together? In what follows, \enquote{the
  1230	interjection cluster} refers to the broad property profile. I use
  1231	\enquote{the intersection} only for cases that belong simultaneously to
  1232	\intj{syn}, \intj{sem}, and
  1233	\intj{prag}.
  1234	A causal-network account requires both a diachronic account of
  1235	recruitment and a synchronic account of the network order among
  1236	properties.
  1237	
  1238	Table~\ref{tab:link-status} sets out each proposed link with its current
  1239	evidential status, the kind of evidence that would bear on it, and the
  1240	main way it could fail.
  1241

  1294	\subsection{Recruitment pathways}\label{sec:recruitment}
  1295	
  1296	Section~\ref{sec:cluster} already pointed to three recruitment
  1297	pathways: semantic bleaching from nouns, syntactic deactivation from
  1298	verbs, and nonce coinage via onomatopoeia. The first is historically
  1299	demonstrated; the second and third are proposed pathways with clear
  1300	examples but without the extended diachronic documentation that Path~1
  1301	has. The first two are analysed in detail here; the third is taken up
  1302	in Section~\ref{sec:turnover}.
  1303	
  1304	The pragmatic issue is how changes in communicative use become
  1305	categorially consequential: grammaticalization and pragmaticalization
  1306	matter here because they alter a form's communicative job as well as its
  1307	position in a syntactic tree
  1308	\citep[cf.][]{TraugottDasher2002, heinekuteva2002lexicon}.
  1309	
  1310	\subsection{Path 1: religious noun to interjection}\label{sec:path-religious}
  1311	
  1312	\textcite{gehweiler2008} traces the development of \mention{gee} from
  1313	\mention{Jesus} through three positions:
  1314	
  1315	\ea\label{ex:bleaching}
  1316	  \ea Proper name used as invocation (\mention{Jesus!} addressed to the
  1317	    deity)
  1318	  \ex Secondary interjection: exclamatory use, no longer addressing the
  1319	    deity (\mention{Jesus!} as an expression of surprise)
  1320	  \ex Primary interjection: phonological modification yields
  1321	    \mention{Jeez} and \mention{Gee}, and \mention{gee!} detaches from
  1322	    the source noun
  1323	  \z
  1324	\z
  1325	The two transitions don't work the same way, and Gehweiler is emphatic
  1326	about the difference. She explains the first as gradual and speaker-based,
  1327	through Traugott and Dasher's invited inferencing, and
  1328	\enquote{advanced by the syntactic isolation of \mention{Jesus}}
  1329	\citep[85]{gehweiler2008}. The second she treats as abrupt and
  1330	hearer-based: tabooistic clipping produced \mention{gee!}, and she insists
  1331	that this step \enquote{was not gradual} and \enquote{can therefore not be
  1332	explained with reference to Invited Inferencing or syntactic position}
  1333	\citep[88]{gehweiler2008}.
  1334	The positions layer cluster properties unevenly. Prosodic isolation is
  1335	already present at the invocation, because a vocative like
  1336	\mention{Jesus!} occupies its own intonation unit (contrast the
  1337	integrated argument in \mention{Put your faith in Jesus}). What changes
  1338	is the content: non-referentiality arrives with the secondary interjection, when the speaker
  1339	is no longer addressing the deity. Phonological divergence then
  1340	makes the etymological connection opaque, leaving a standalone interjection
  1341	that many speakers don't connect to its source.
  1342	For the first transition the vocative is the gateway, and what it supplies
  1343	is syntactic: an utterance-initial slot set apart from the rest of the
  1344	utterance, which is the isolation Gehweiler credits with enabling the
  1345	categorial change \citep[85]{gehweiler2008}. She denies that the same
  1346	explanation extends to the second.
  1347	
  1348	That discontinuity is an asset for the path-dependence claim rather than
  1349	an embarrassment for it. A gradual slide would leave a form with no
  1350	particular mark of where it came from; an abrupt re-analysis leaves exactly
  1351	the kind of residue the account needs, since the phonological opacity of
  1352	\mention{gee!} is a permanent trace of the clipping that produced it. The
  1353	first transition is constrained in the way Gehweiler describes: once the
  1354	invocation loses its referential force, the phonological connection to the
  1355	source is the last link to the nominal category, and weakening that link by
  1356	phonological modification enables full independence. Gehweiler's corpus data documents the
  1357	constraint: the phonological modifications (\mention{Jeez},
  1358	\mention{Gee}, \mention{Gosh}, \mention{Golly}) cluster in the
  1359	seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, with the transition from
  1360	invocation to primary interjection happening relatively rapidly once the
  1361	modifications appear \citep[80--85]{gehweiler2008}.
  1362	
  1363	Two cautions matter for the general claim. The boundary between
  1364	invocation and exclamation is hard to operationalize in historical
  1365	texts, since prosodic and interactional evidence is indirect. The
  1366	seventeenth- and eighteenth-century clustering of euphemistic forms
  1367	may reflect external taboo pressure as well as internal category
  1368	pressures. The causal-network claim doesn't require Gehweiler's sequence
  1369	to be an autonomous internal cline. It requires the weaker and more
  1370	historically plausible claim that social pressure, invited inference,
  1371	and reanalysis can jointly push a source noun toward the same
  1372	interjectional profile.
  1373	
  1374	Two processes operate within this path.
  1375	\textcite[76]{gehweiler2008} distinguishes gradual Invited Inferencing
  1376	(speaker-based: the expressive use is conversationally implicated, then
  1377	conventionalized) from abrupt reanalysis (hearer-based: the hearer parses
  1378	the exclamation as a standalone unit rather than an invocation).
  1379	Both feed the same cluster, but they operate at different timescales and
  1380	involve different agents. Their convergence supports the network-order
  1381	claim: the same node can be reached by different routes.
  1382	
  1383	The first transition generates a specific prediction: partial bleaching
  1384	should be detectable by the absence of downstream properties. A form that
  1385	has reached the secondary interjection but no further should be
  1386	non-referential while retaining the source phonology and resisting full
  1387	prosodic independence.
  1388	The prediction is testable and goes beyond historical description.
  1389	
  1390	\subsection{Path 2: verb to interjection}
  1391	
  1392	A second trajectory enters the cluster via syntactic deactivation rather
  1393	than phonological modification. \mention{Damn} originates as a verb, and it
  1394	enters by way of the optative, the minor clause type of \mention{God save
  1395	the Queen} and \mention{God help you}, where a main-clause subjunctive
  1396	survives in a narrow range of formulaic frames
  1397	\citep[944]{HuddlestonPullum2002}. \mention{God damn him} has that shape,
  1398	and what the interjectional use drops is that subject rather than an
  1399	understood addressee: \mention{damn him} is a wish, not an instruction.
  1400	Tense goes with the subject, while an NP complement survives in a few
  1401	constructions (\mention{damn these mosquitoes!}).
  1402	The complement retention shows that the syntactic deactivation is
  1403	partial: the form carries some verb properties into the interjection
  1404	cluster. In \textit{CGEL}'s
  1405	terms, it's the head of an IntjP, not a verb phrase (VP)
  1406	\citep[1361fn]{HuddlestonPullum2002}.
  1407	
  1408	The process is decategorialization: loss of verbal morphosyntax
  1409	(subject, tense) and gain of expressive force.
  1410	As a verb, \mention{damn} contributes content meaning, the condemning itself,
  1411	and uttering it performs the act. As \intj{syn},
  1412	\mention{damn!} contributes procedural meaning, a stance of frustration.
  1413	
  1414	This doesn't count as degrammaticalization in the strict sense, since the
  1415	form doesn't gain the morphosyntactic substance of a major lexical category
  1416	\citep[cf.][]{norde2009}. It's better treated as pragmatic
  1417	conventionalization with syntactic deactivation: the verbal source
  1418	loses argument-structural obligations as the interjectional use gains
  1419	procedural and interpersonal force.
  1420	
  1421	For other verb-derived interjections the gateway is the imperative rather
  1422	than the optative. Imperatives also lack an overt subject and stand alone
  1423	prosodically, so the interjectional use completes a deactivation the
  1424	construction has already begun. \mention{Come on} and \mention{look} (in their
  1425	discourse-marker uses) have lost subject, tense, and complement-taking
  1426	ability. They're standalone interjections
  1427	with no residual argument structure.
  1428	
  1429	\mention{Damn} shows partial syntactic
  1430	deactivation (complement retained, subject and tense lost), while
  1431	\mention{come on} and \mention{look} represent full deactivation (no
  1432	complement, no argument structure at all). The degree of deactivation
  1433	correlates with the degree of integration into the interjection cluster.
  1434	
  1435	This correlation generates a graded prediction: a form that retains
  1436	complement-taking ability should also retain other verb-like properties
  1437	(e.g., prosodic integration in some constructions), while a fully
  1438	deactivated form should show the complete interjection profile. The
  1439	ordering relation predicts the inferential package available at each
  1440	stage.
  1441	
  1442	\subsection{Convergent pathways}
  1443	
  1444	The two paths enter the cluster from different directions, one through
  1445	phonological modification of a bleached noun, the other through syntactic
  1446	deactivation of a verb, but both exploit a gateway construction that
  1447	already provides prosodic isolation: the vocative for Path~1, and for
  1448	Path~2 the optative or the imperative. Both converge on the same property profile.
  1449	The convergence is what the causal-network account predicts: different
  1450	recruitment processes can bring items into the same projectible profile,
  1451	producing the same cluster of co-occurring properties through different
  1452	routes.
  1453	
  1454	Both paths can be situated within \textcite{TraugottDasher2002}'s
  1455	subjectification trajectory (content~$>$~procedural,
  1456	nonsubjective~$>$~intersubjective), but they instantiate it differently.
  1457	Path~1 is grammaticalization in Traugott's technical sense: the invocation
  1458	carries content meaning (addressing an entity) plus subjective stance
  1459	(reverence); the secondary interjection loses content and gains procedural
  1460	meaning (expressive force); phonological modification completes the shift
  1461	to fully procedural, intersubjective meaning, with hearers interpreting the
  1462	form as a conventional expression, not an invocation. Gehweiler's own
  1463	verdict on that last step is worth keeping in view: because it turns on
  1464	hearer re-analysis rather than speaker-invited inference, it's the one
  1465	transition her account doesn't treat as subjectification
  1466	\citep[88]{gehweiler2008}. Path~2, as described above, is
  1467	decategorialization: loss of verbal morphosyntax with gain of expressive
  1468	force.
  1469	
  1470	Onomatopoeia (Section~\ref{sec:turnover}) is categorically different.
  1471	Nonce coinages enter the cluster with no prior lexical history (no bleaching, no
  1472	deactivation, no subjectification). Three diachronically distinct
  1473	processes (grammaticalization, pragmaticalization, nonce coinage)
  1474	converge on the same synchronic category. Grammaticalization theory
  1475	alone can't subsume all three. A causal-network account can: the shared
  1476	point is the node they converge on, with outcomes recruited by different
  1477	processes but linked by the same synchronic ordering relations.
  1478	
  1479	\subsection{Synchronic coupling}\label{sec:synchronic-coupling}
  1480	
  1481	Recruitment explains how items enter the cluster. The synchronic
  1482	question is what gives the cluster network order now, and what might
  1483	keep that order stable. Four synchronic links are relevant:
  1484	prosodic-syntactic coupling, a non-referentiality ratchet,
  1485	interactional routine entrenchment, and regional/indexical conditioning.
  1486	They don't have the same status. The first two order structural
  1487	and semantic diagnostics. The third is the central stabilizer candidate
  1488	for \intj{prag}. The fourth remains a
  1489	maintenance hypothesis.
  1490	
  1491	\subsubsection{Prosodic isolation and syntactic non-integration}
  1492	
  1493	These two properties reinforce each other, but the evidence is asymmetric.
  1494	Prosodic separation plausibly makes
  1495	syntactic integration harder, since a form in its own intonation unit is less
  1496	available as a complement or modifier. But the corpora used below are
  1497	written, and prosodic isolation is tracked there through proxies
  1498	(Section~\ref{sec:phonology}), so they can't establish that direction.
  1499	
  1500	The diachronic record does support the syntactic half.
  1501	\textcite[85]{gehweiler2008} finds that invocational \mention{Jesus}
  1502	\enquote{typically stands utterance-initially and~-- like other
  1503	interjections~-- set apart from the rest of the utterance, or even on its
  1504	own}, and argues that \enquote{only because of this syntactic isolation of
  1505	the proper name~-- and Invited Inferencing, of course~-- could the secondary
  1506	interjection \mention{Jesus!} develop from the proper name}. That's
  1507	evidence that syntactic isolation, together with invited inferencing, enabled
  1508	a categorial change, not that prosody enabled the syntax. She also limits it: as Section~\ref{sec:path-religious} noted, she takes the
  1509	step from \mention{Jesus!} to \mention{gee!} to lie outside both invited
  1510	inferencing and syntactic position \citep[88]{gehweiler2008}.
  1511	
  1512	The same coupling is visible synchronically. Standalone
  1513	\mention{Damn!} has both properties: it occupies its own intonation unit
  1514	and has no role in clause structure. In \mention{I think that, damn,
  1515	we're going to be late}, \mention{damn} remains possible only as a
  1516	parenthetical supplement, set off from the \mention{that}-clause. In
  1517	\mention{the damn car}, the form loses both properties at once: it's
  1518	inside the noun phrase and receives the prosody of the phrase.
  1519	
  1520	The prediction is concrete. If a novel form is reliably
  1521	packaged as its own intonation unit, it should resist complementation,
  1522	modification by ordinary phrase-internal material, and coordination with
  1523	integrated phrases. If it's pulled into a host phrase, prosodic isolation should
  1524	weaken together with \intj{syn} status.
  1525	
  1526	\subsubsection{The non-referentiality ratchet}
  1527	
  1528	Bleaching removes referential content, which weakens the semantic
  1529	motivation for argument structure. A form with no entity or event to
  1530	construe is less available as an argument, complement, or modifier. Once
  1531	that syntactic role is lost, prosodic isolation becomes easier to keep
  1532	in place. The result is a one-directional cascade. Once argument
  1533	structure is lost, the form occupies a different functional niche, so
  1534	ordinary pressures toward referential syntax are reduced.
  1535	
  1536	This ratchet supports a different set of predictions from the
  1537	prosodic-syntactic link. A non-referential stance form should resist
  1538	definiteness, quantification, and argument position:
  1539	*\mention{the damn!}, *\mention{every ouch}, *\mention{Damn surprised
  1540	me}. It should also resist embedding as propositional content
  1541	(\mention{*She said that damn}) unless it's quoted as a form. If
  1542	referential content becomes active again, the form should move toward
  1543	noun, verb, or routine-formula regions of the network. The ratchet
  1544	keeps the form near the interjection node by blocking return
  1545	to the source category, while prosodic-syntactic coupling links the
  1546	central structural properties.
  1547	
  1548	\subsubsection{Interactional routine entrenchment}
  1549	
  1550	The interactional-pragmatic node needs a candidate stabilizer of its own.
  1551	Conversation repeatedly places interjectional forms in recognizable
  1552	sequential slots: continuers, repair initiators, change-of-state tokens,
  1553	stance displays, and response tokens \citep{stivers2019,dingemanse2020}.
  1554	\textcite{dingemanse2024} argues that these interactional uses are
  1555	central rather than peripheral for high-frequency interjections.
  1556	
  1557	Repeated deployment in these slots favours a particular package of
  1558	properties. Two levels are in play here, and the argument needs both. On a
  1559	given occasion, a continuer such as \mention{mm-hm} works because it can be
  1560	recognized without taking over the turn or adding propositional content.
  1561	A change-of-state token such as \mention{oh} works because it can mark
  1562	an information update before the speaker builds further at-issue
  1563	content. A stance token such as \mention{bruh} works because it can
  1564	display evaluation while leaving the surrounding proposition untouched.
  1565	Across many occasions, that occasion-level fitness is supposed to accumulate,
  1566	so that the interactional routine favours short, standalone, prosodically
  1567	independent, non-referential material in the inventory at large. The
  1568	accumulation is an assumption here rather than a finding. The probes in
  1569	Section~\ref{sec:discussion} bear on the inventory-level pattern, not on the
  1570	step from one level to the other.
  1571	
  1572	This candidate stabilizer supports the projectibility of
  1573	\intj{prag}. If a novel form is recruited as a
  1574	minimal stance, response, repair, or backchannel resource, one should
  1575	expect turn-initial or standalone availability, independent prosodic
  1576	packaging, resistance to embedding under reported speech, and
  1577	availability for uptake as an interactional move. The same candidate
  1578	stabilizer also explains why the function can persist when category membership
  1579	weakens: a full clause such as \mention{I agree} can do agreement work,
  1580	but it lacks the reduced, non-propositional packaging that makes
  1581	\mention{mm-hm} a central \intj{prag} case.
  1582	
  1583	\subsubsection{Regional/indexical conditioning}
  1584	
  1585	Some interjections are sharply conditioned by region, register, or
  1586	community, and some may also do identity work. \mention{Lah} in
  1587	Singapore and Malaysia, \mention{yaar} in India and Pakistan,
  1588	\mention{haba} in Nigeria, and \mention{bruh} in African American
  1589	English \citep{louf2023} are plausible cases.
  1590	
  1591	They are not, however, the same kind of case, and the difference matters for
  1592	which node the conditioning operates on. \mention{Haba} is free-standing:
  1593	\textcite{unuabonahdaniel2020} avoid the terms utterance-initial and
  1594	utterance-final for it and its Nigerian congeners \enquote{because they are
  1595	free standing and are far more detached from the utterances than PMs}
  1596	\citep[69]{unuabonahdaniel2020}. \mention{Lah} is not. It is clause-final and
  1597	prosodically dependent on its host, and this holds on either current account of
  1598	it: \textcite[447]{lim2007} reports that Gupta takes the particles to
  1599	\enquote{function as the carrier of the intonation contour of the utterance},
  1600	while the rival analysis gives them lexically-specified tone inherited from a
  1601	substrate. That rival is weakest for \mention{lah} in particular, since
  1602	\mention{lah} is one of the few particles \textcite[463]{lim2007} does not
  1603	match to Cantonese, and its Bazaar Malay and Hokkien sources have
  1604	\enquote{neutral tone and weak stress}. Either way \mention{lah} fails
  1605	prosodic isolation, so it satisfies \intj{sem} and \intj{prag} without
  1606	\intj{syn} (Figure~\ref{fig:node-overlap}). Regional conditioning can
  1607	therefore recruit a form to the interactional node without recruiting it to the
  1608	syntactic one, which is what \mention{lah} looks like and \mention{haba} does
  1609	not. The GloWbE evidence in
  1610	Section~\ref{sec:discussion}, modelled in
  1611	Appendix~\ref{app:conditioning}, shows country and variety conditioning. It
  1612	doesn't by itself establish social indexing in the stronger
  1613	sociolinguistic sense: uptake, stance, enregisterment, style shifting,
  1614	metapragmatic awareness, or distribution by speaker group within
  1615	comparable contexts.
  1616	
  1617	Regional/indexical conditioning is a maintenance hypothesis.
  1618	The open question is whether it stabilizes the cluster profile (property
  1619	co-occurrence) or individual items (inventory persistence). If it
  1620	stabilizes the profile, regional/indexical conditioning helps constitute
  1621	the node. If it stabilizes only the item, it operates as a
  1622	lexical-persistence process alongside the interjection cluster.
  1623	
  1624	When a regional interjection is adopted by out-group speakers as ironic
  1625	quotation, the causal-network account predicts a contrast between
  1626	inventory persistence and profile stabilization. If out-group
  1627	\mention{lah} loses prosodic isolation or emotive force while retaining
  1628	its social-marking function, regional/indexical conditioning stabilizes
  1629	the inventory alone. If it retains the full profile, the link is part of
  1630	the causal structure of the category. This comparison remains for future
  1631	work.
  1632	
  1633	The interjection cluster has two structural ordering links
  1634	(prosodic-syntactic coupling and the non-referentiality ratchet), one
  1635	interactional-pragmatic stabilizer candidate (routine entrenchment), and
  1636	one regional/indexical maintenance hypothesis. Together, the first three
  1637	explain why prosodic, syntactic, semantic, and pragmatic diagnostics
  1638	travel together synchronically. They also underwrite the probes in
  1639	Section~\ref{sec:discussion}: disrupting different links should produce
  1640	different patterns of residue, conditioning, or erosion.
  1641

  1643	network. It's a schematic map of the links used in the argument rather
  1644	than a complete model of every dependency among the diagnostics.

  1728	\subsection{Causal residues at the category boundary}\label{sec:residues}
  1729	
  1730	The interjection cluster is a convergence point, but convergence doesn't
  1731	erase history. Forms recruited through different pathways can share the
  1732	same pragmatic profile while retaining traces of the source construction
  1733	that brought them there. Those traces explain why the category is both
  1734	recognizable and persistently irregular. The irregularities are pathway
  1735	signatures rather than noise around a prototype.
  1736	
  1737	This gives the category a second kind of inference. Section~\ref{sec:projectibility}
  1738	develops forward projectibility: observing some properties of a novel
  1739	form licenses predictions about other properties. Historical pragmatics
  1740	also needs a retrodictive counterpart to projectibility:
  1741	observing a residual property can license an inference about the pathway
  1742	that produced it. A causal-network account can make that inference
  1743	because it links properties to the recruitment pathways of
  1744	Section~\ref{sec:recruitment}, ordering relations, and source
  1745	constructions.
  1746

  1748	pathway leaves behind, and the retrodictive inference each one licenses.

  1798	invocative recruitment through euphemistic phonology. \mention{Lah}

  1801	predicts fuzzy boundaries.

  1803	different historical routes.

  1860	\subsection{Stability through turnover}\label{sec:turnover}
  1861	
  1862	Pathway residues record how interjectional forms entered the network. Turnover shows the complementary fact: the interjection
  1863	profile can remain available while its lexical inventory changes.
  1864	Individual forms enter and leave, but the broad profile remains
  1865	available for new material.
  1866	
  1867	The Corpus of Historical American English (COHA) illustrates this
  1868	turnover. In the 1820s the six most frequent interjection lemmas are
  1869	\mention{no}, \mention{oh}, \mention{yes}, \mention{nay}, \mention{o},
  1870	and \mention{ah}. By the 2010s half that list has gone: \mention{nay},
  1871	\mention{o}, and \mention{ah} are replaced by \mention{yeah},
  1872	\mention{hey}, and \mention{uh}, while \mention{no}, \mention{oh}, and
  1873	\mention{yes} hold their places throughout (Figure~\ref{fig:turnover}).
  1874	\mention{Nay} falls from 175 tokens per million to 2.2; \mention{yeah},
  1875	absent from the 1820s altogether, rises from nothing to 723.%
  1876	\footnote{Part-of-speech--tagged frequencies in COHA \citep{davies2010},
  1877	  extracted 2026-07-27 and archived in
  1878	  \texttt{analysis/hand-extracted/} as
  1879	  \texttt{nay-yeah-coha-by-decade.csv} and
  1880	  \texttt{interjection-lemmas-coha-by-decade.csv}. The 1820s ranking is taken from the complete
  1881	  tagged list for that decade, 111 forms, so it does not depend on the
  1882	  all-corpus top twenty; \mention{farewell}, \mention{yea}, \mention{ay} and
  1883	  \mention{lo} are frequent early and absent from that top twenty, and none of
  1884	  them displaces the six named here.
  1885	  Frequencies are normalized per million words. The interjection tag
  1886	  captures a conventional inventory rather than a theoretically motivated
  1887	  one; the point here is the turnover pattern, not the tagging decisions.}
  1888	The items change while the projectible category position remains
  1889	available.

  1905	
  1906	Regional variation shows the profile's availability under different
  1907	local inventories.
  1908	The GloWbE corpus \citep[1.9~billion words across 20 English-speaking
  1909	countries]{daviesfuchs2015} shows that candidate interjectional forms
  1910	are distributed unevenly across varieties, with \mention{lah} almost
  1911	exclusively in Singapore and Malaysia, \mention{yaar} in India and
  1912	Pakistan, and \mention{haba} in Nigeria. The corpus evidence establishes
  1913	distributional conditioning; the linguistic analysis identifies the
  1914	shared profile that makes those forms comparable.
  1915	
  1916	The cluster is also continuously replenished by onomatopoeic coinage. Two
  1917	clarifications, because \term{onomatopoeia} is often handled as though it
  1918	named a category alongside noun and verb. It doesn't. It names a relation
  1919	between a form and a sound, and forms standing in that relation are spread
  1920	across categories: \mention{bang} is a noun in \mention{a loud bang}, a
  1921	verb in \mention{he banged the door}, and an interjection in
  1922	\mention{Bang! You're dead}. So the claim isn't that a category of
  1923	onomatopoeias gets absorbed into the interjections. It's that imitation is
  1924	one of the routes by which new forms arrive at the interjectional profile.
  1925	
  1926	Nor is the route closed. The stock of imitable sounds isn't fixed by the
  1927	stock of animals: devices supply \mention{beep}, \mention{ping}, and
  1928	\mention{whirr}, and any sound can be re-rendered, which is how comics gave
  1929	English \mention{bam} and \mention{pow} beside older forms. Nonce
  1930	interjections can be freely coined \citep[74]{quirk1985}, and new coinages
  1931	immediately adopt the cluster profile: they're prosodically isolated,
  1932	non-referential, emotively laden, and syntactically non-integrated.
  1933	
  1934	This generates a testable prediction: a newly conventionalized
  1935	interjectional use should exhibit the full cluster profile without
  1936	having traversed the documented noun-to-interjection or
  1937	verb-to-interjection paths. Take \mention{bruh}. \textcite{oed_bruh} records it as a noun from 1894, as a
  1938	title before a man's name, and from 1967 as a familiar form of address, where
  1939	every quotation is vocative (\mention{Hey, bruh}, \mention{Be cool, bruh}). The
  1940	standalone use addressed to nobody is later, and the entry doesn't record it as
  1941	an interjection at all. Its rise shows up in COCA, from 0.01 per million in
  1942	1990--94 to 1.09 in 2015--19.\footnote{Tokens sit overwhelmingly in television
  1943	  and film (1.44 per million) and are absent from the spoken section, so the
  1944	  record is scripted representation of speech rather than recorded speech. COHA
  1945	  has the form absent before the 1980s and nine tokens in all. Figures are raw
  1946	  string frequencies; see \path{analysis/hand-extracted/}.} That use is
  1947	prosodically isolated, non-inflecting, non-referential, and syntactically
  1948	non-integrated, even though the same word family includes the nominal lexemes
  1949	just described. The category position remains
  1950	available even as its members turn over.
  1951	
  1952	\textcite{dingemanse2020} characterizes such items as liminal signs,
  1953	signs at the boundary between sound and speech, whose liminality is
  1954	functional, not defective. Cultural evolutionary processes \enquote{sample
  1955	the landscape of possible resources} \citep[191]{dingemanse2020}. The
  1956	causal-network account describes that landscape as a property space
  1957	structured by the recruitment pathways and synchronic links above.

  1959	The recruited, ordered, and replenished cluster is now ready for the
  1960	paper's central inferential question: what can partial evidence license?

  1962	\section[Projectibility]{Projectibility: what the category lets you predict}\label{sec:projectibility}
  1963	
  1964	Section~\ref{sec:cluster} showed what each property, individually, lets
  1965	you infer. Section~\ref{sec:causal-structure} identified the ordering
  1966	and stabilizing links that couple those properties. This section asks a
  1967	different question: what does the package license, and for whom?
  1968	Section~\ref{sec:residues}
  1969	treated retrodictive inference from residue to recruitment history; here
  1970	projectibility means forward inference from observed properties of
  1971	a form to properties not yet observed.
  1972	
  1973	That formulation matters for the question of \emph{whose} inference is at
  1974	issue. Speakers and hearers use partial cues to anticipate how a form will
  1975	behave before they've observed its full distribution, and they do so without
  1976	holding the category label; analysts run the same inference explicitly, from
  1977	category membership, for field-specific purposes.
  1978	Section~\ref{sec:proj-partial} separates the two.
  1979	
  1980	This section first shows how partial observations support participant
  1981	inference, then how the same structure yields different predictions for
  1982	syntax, semantics, and interactional pragmatics, and finally what fixes the
  1983	domain those predictions fall in. As in Section~\ref{sec:intro}, bare \enquote{interjection}
  1984	continues to refer to forms in the extension of all three field-relative nodes
  1985	unless a subscript specifies otherwise.
  1986	
  1987	The running case is the word family \mention{damn}. Standalone
  1988	interjectional \mention{damn!}, complement-taking \mention{damn these
  1989	mosquitoes!}, and prenominal \mention{damn} in \mention{the damn car}
  1990	belong to the same word family. They needn't share a lexeme.
  1991	
  1992	\subsection{Partial-observation inference}\label{sec:proj-partial}
  1993	
  1994	Projectibility becomes circular if it says only this: if X is an
  1995	interjection, it has interjection properties. That's trivially true of
  1996	any category. Causal-network projectibility is different. Observing some
  1997	cluster properties in a novel form licenses inference to other
  1998	properties you haven't yet observed.

  2052	use for the first time observes a partial package: standalone position,
  2053	prosodic isolation, morphological bareness, and stance meaning. From
  2054	those observations, the three field-relative nodes license a battery of
  2055	further expectations:
  2056

  2059	    won't inflect within that use (*\mention{bruhs}, *\mention{bruhed}),
  2060	    will resist syntactic integration (*\mention{I think that bruh,
  2061	    we're late}), and won't take a determiner or function as an argument
  2062	    (*\mention{the bruh}, *\mention{Bruh surprised me}). This leaves
  2063	    room for separate nominal or verbal lexemes in the same word family.
  2064	  \ex \intj{prag}: the form should be available for
  2065	    turn-initial or standalone interactional use.

  2071	
  2072	\begin{itemize}
  2073	\item Near-entailments (a): the
  2074	  \intj{syn} predictions mostly extrapolate
  2075	  within the same domain from observed distributional facts. Any
  2076	  framework recognizing the relevant morphosyntactic properties
  2077	  generates many of the same predictions. These are real but not specific
  2078	  to the causal-network account.
  2079	\item Cross-domain inferences (b, c): the
  2080	  \intj{prag} prediction (interactional-pragmatic
  2081	  distribution from phonological form) and the
  2082	  \intj{sem} prediction (use-conditional meaning
  2083	  from stance expression) involve genuine leaps across domains. These are specific to the causal-network account: they rely on
  2084	  the cluster's internal links, not on any single property.
  2085	\end{itemize}
  2086	All three are licensed because identifiable links order the cluster's
  2087	properties. The non-referentiality ratchet
  2088	(Section~\ref{sec:synchronic-coupling}) links loss of referential content with
  2089	syntactic non-integration. Prosodic-syntactic coupling then links
  2090	syntactic non-integration with prosodic isolation. The inferences are
  2091	grounded in network order, not in definitions.
  2092	
  2093	Who does the projecting, though? A hearer meeting \mention{Bummer!} or
  2094	\mention{groovy} for the first time doesn't run a taxonomy and then read
  2095	off consequences. So it's worth separating two levels that the argument so
  2096	far has run together.
  2097	
  2098	\term{Category-driven projection} is the analyst's. Given that a form
  2099	belongs to the interjection cluster, expect the linked properties. That's
  2100	projectibility in Goodman's sense, claimed throughout the preceding
  2101	sections. \term{Cue-driven inference} is the participant's. The
  2102	\mention{bruh} case above describes it without naming it: what the hearer
  2103	has is standalone position, prosodic isolation, morphological bareness, and
  2104	stance meaning, from which the stance reading follows. No category
  2105	label need be represented for the reading to go through.
  2106	
  2107	The two aren't rivals. Cue-driven inference is reliable \emph{because} the cluster is
  2108	coupled: the links in Section~\ref{sec:synchronic-coupling} are what make a
  2109	partial package informative about the rest. Analyst and participant are two
  2110	consumers of one causal structure, drawing on it for different purposes and
  2111	with different explicitness. So \mention{Bummer!} and \mention{groovy} tell
  2112	against an account on which the category label does the inferential work,
  2113	and for one on which the coupling does. The label is the analyst's
  2114	bookkeeping device for a structure participants exploit without naming.
  2115	
  2116	Fields differ in which links they track
  2117	(Section~\ref{sec:proj-fieldrelative}); analyst and participant differ in
  2118	whether the category figures in the inference at all. The two indices are
  2119	independent. Nothing here says that speakers tacitly represent
  2120	interjection-hood, which the account doesn't require.

  2122	\subsection{What each node projects}\label{sec:proj-fieldrelative}
  2123	
  2124	The three interjection nodes established in
  2125	Section~\ref{sec:fieldrelative-nodes} do their work as different packages of
  2126	inference, not as rival definitions.
  2127	
  2128	\intj{syn} projects syntactic behaviour:
  2129	supplement function, resistance to \mention{that}-complementation,
  2130	resistance to coordination with non-interjections, and no ordinary
  2131	argument structure. These predictions are grounded in the
  2132	prosodic-syntactic coupling link from
  2133	Section~\ref{sec:synchronic-coupling}. \intj{sem}
  2134	projects meaning type, and here the projection has to reach past the
  2135	properties that admitted the form. Independence, nondisplaceability,
  2136	perspective dependence, and descriptive ineffability are what
  2137	Section~\ref{sec:fieldrelative-nodes} uses to assign the node, so
  2138	predicting them would only restate membership.
  2139	\textcite{potts2007} lists two further properties of expressive content on
  2140	the following page, and neither does admission work here. One is immediacy,
  2141	where expressives \enquote{achieve their intended act simply by being
  2142	uttered}. The other is repeatability, where repeated use has an effect
  2143	\enquote{of strengthening the emotive content, rather than one of
  2144	redundancy} \citep[167]{potts2007}. Both are testable and both can fail.
  2145	\mention{Damn, damn, damn} intensifies where \mention{The car broke, the car
  2146	broke, the car broke} doesn't, and a form that flattened into redundancy
  2147	under repetition would defeat the prediction. \intj{prag}
  2148	projects interactional-pragmatic behaviour, and the same discipline applies.
  2149	Turn-initial or standalone positioning and stance display are admission
  2150	criteria. What they let us predict is uptake: that the form will be treated

  2188	\subsection{Category vs.\ function}
  2189	
  2190	\textit{CGEL}'s distinction between category and function sharpens the
  2191	projectibility point: what does the \intj{syn} category label add
  2192	beyond the supplement function label? Supplements of any category
  2193	(noun phrases [NPs], adjective phrases [AdjPs], relative clauses) share
  2194	prosodic isolation and syntactic
  2195	non-integration. But \intj{syn} adds a package of
  2196	predictions that no other category-in-supplement-function supports:
  2197	
  2198	\begin{itemize}
  2199	\item Non-referentiality. Supplements can be referential
  2200	  (\mention{a university lecturer, Dr Brown, \ldots}); interjections
  2201	  characteristically can't.
  2202	\item Emotive/interpersonal force. Not all supplements express
  2203	  stance (\mention{Dr Brown} expresses none); interjections
  2204	  characteristically do (\mention{ugh}, \mention{yay}).
  2205	\item Free coinage. No other category in supplement function
  2206	  can be freely coined via onomatopoeia. Comics gave English
  2207	  \mention{bam} and \mention{pow}; nothing comparable mints new
  2208	  relative clauses.
  2209	\item Non-inflection. NP supplements inflect freely
  2210	  (\mention{a lecturer}, \mention{two lecturers}); interjections don't
  2211	  (*\mention{ouches}).
  2212	\end{itemize}
  2213	
  2214	The function label tells you about syntactic integration. The category
  2215	label tells you about inference: it licenses a package of predictions
  2216	that the function label alone can't provide. The category/function distinction matters
  2217	beyond the \textit{CGEL} tradition: any framework that distinguishes what kind of
  2218	thing an expression is from what job it's doing in a particular clause
  2219	will face the same question. The causal-network answer is that the
  2220	category earns its keep by licensing inferences that the function
  2221	doesn't.

  2223	\subsection{What sets the domain of projection}\label{sec:proj-domain}
  2224	
  2225	Categories don't all project in the same domain, and what fixes the domain
  2226	is where the causal links run. Nouns project morphosyntactically:
  2227	\mention{lecturer} takes a determiner, inflects for number, and fills an
  2228	argument slot. Categorizing something as a noun tells you almost nothing
  2229	about its pragmatics, since nouns aren't a pragmatic category. Interjections
  2230	come out the other way, and the reason is the same reason.
  2231	
  2232	For interjections, the situation is reversed. The three nodes overlap,
  2233	and the richest projectibility belongs to
  2234	\intj{prag}, the interactional-pragmatic
  2235	cluster.
  2236	\textcite{dingemanse2024} reaches the same conclusion from interactional
  2237	data: the most frequent and functionally central interjections are
  2238	interactional tools (continuers, repair initiators, change-of-state
  2239	tokens) rather than emotive exclamations, and their primary
  2240	projectibility is about sequential position and conversational
  2241	management. For forms at the intersection of all three nodes, the strongest inferences
  2242	are about use, not denotation. \mention{Oh} shows the
  2243	asymmetry. From \intj{syn} you get what any
  2244	supplement gives you: prosodic isolation, no inflection, no argument structure.
  2245	From \intj{prag} you get a turn position,
  2246	availability as a response token, and the display of evolving knowledge
  2247	described in Section~\ref{sec:pragmatics}. Same form, and the two nodes differ
  2248	in how much they buy.
  2249	
  2250	The contrast with nouns and verbs concerns where the density lies, not how
  2251	much structure each node carries.
  2252	\intj{syn} is thin next to those nodes, lacking
  2253	the network of agreement, argument realization, derivation, and inflectional
  2254	behaviour that makes them so stable. What the three nodes divide between them
  2255	is the kind of prediction each supports, set out in
  2256	Section~\ref{sec:proj-fieldrelative}.
  2257	
  2258	Why pragmatic in this case? Because causal links generate predictions
  2259	within the domains they operate in. The prosodic-syntactic coupling from
  2260	Section~\ref{sec:synchronic-coupling}
  2261	predicts prosodic and syntactic behaviour, with no corresponding
  2262	prediction about morphological paradigms or truth-conditional content.
  2263	Regional/indexical conditioning predicts uneven persistence across
  2264	varieties, which is why \mention{lah} and \mention{yaar} are entrenched in some
  2265	Englishes and absent from others, with no corresponding prediction about
  2266	inflectional regularity. The strongest established links among the three
  2267	interjection nodes are prosodic and interactional; the regional or
  2268	indexical link remains a maintenance hypothesis. These links and
  2269	hypotheses underwrite predictions in their respective domains.
  2270	
  2271	The same point holds outside interjections. Noun projectibility is
  2272	morphosyntactic because the underlying processes (agreement, structural
  2273	analogy, discourse frequency) operate in morphosyntactic domains.
  2274	
  2275	This connects to the content/procedural distinction. The central
  2276	projectibility of interjections is procedural and use-conditional rather
  2277	than denotational \citep{TraugottDasher2002}. The
  2278	\intj{sem} (expressive) cluster is the semantic
  2279	reflex: use-conditional meaning is the semantic signature of procedural
  2280	content. The same pattern shows up elsewhere. \term{Animacy} projects grammatically (argument realization,
  2281	reference tracking); \term{colour} projects ecologically (edibility,
  2282	toxicity) but usually doesn't project grammatically in the way animacy
  2283	can \citep[cf.][]{seifart2010}. In each case, the domain of
  2284	projectibility follows from the underlying processes.
  2285	
  2286	The richest projectibility is pragmatic, but
  2287	\intj{syn} still belongs in the grammar
  2288	because it's projectible and has its own causal links. Neutralize
  2289	prosodic isolation and the form is reclassified, which is what happens to
  2290	prenominal \mention{damn} in \mention{the damn car}. The syntactic category
  2291	licenses distributional inference: it predicts supplement function,
  2292	resistance to embedding under reported speech (*\mention{She said that damn}),
  2293	and coordination restrictions, even if those predictions are less rich than the
  2294	pragmatic ones. A thin syntactic node is still a projectible syntactic
  2295	node. The framework requires reliable inference, and
  2296	\intj{syn} does.

  2298	\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
  2299	
  2300	\subsection{Empirical probes and boundary conditions}
  2301	
  2302	Section~\ref{sec:projectibility} asked what categorizing a novel form as
  2303	an interjection lets you predict about that form. At the category level,
  2304	the causal-network account should also explain patterns across the
  2305	interjectional region as a whole. As Section~\ref{sec:prototype} argued, what
  2306	the causal-network account adds to what prototype theory already accommodates
  2307	is network-order predictions with named defeat conditions. The probes
  2308	below address profile stability, boundary behaviour, and failure. They
  2309	aren't tests of corrective control.
  2310	
  2311	The strongest probe is pathway residue. If interjections are recruited by
  2312	different causal pathways, residual properties should line up with
  2313	recruitment history. The defeat condition is random distribution:
  2314	retained complements, euphemistic deformation, atypical phonology, and
  2315	regional skew should show no relation to source pathways. The evidence
  2316	in Section~\ref{sec:residues} supports the residue prediction, and the
  2317	pre-registered morphology-gain study adds an exit-residue case:
  2318	\mention{wowed}, \mention{booed}, \mention{oohed}, and \mention{shooed}
  2319	gain syntactic integration while retaining source-interjection semantics.
  2320	The payoff is pragmatic and diachronic: boundary failures diagnose
  2321	recruitment routes and explain why later uses keep source-specific
  2322	residues.
  2323	
  2324	A second probe asks whether conditioning recovers structure. Apparent
  2325	gradation at the interjection boundary should decompose when conditioned
  2326	on register, dialect, or community. The defeat condition is parity
  2327	between conditioned and pooled models. A proof-of-concept probe uses
  2328	GloWbE frequency data \citep{daviesfuchs2015}: for the 10 most frequent
  2329	interjection-tagged items plus regional and boundary items,
  2330	frequency-per-million was extracted by country (20 countries) by raw
  2331	string search, and the coefficient of variation (CV; standard deviation
  2332	divided by the mean) was computed across countries.
  2333	
  2334	The results show three tiers of geographic conditioning. Regional
  2335	interjections (\mention{lah}, \mention{yaar}, \mention{haba}) are
  2336	near-categorical: \mention{lah} occurs at 9.35 per million in Malaysia
  2337	and 6.72 in Singapore but near zero elsewhere; \mention{yaar} at 2.88
  2338	in Pakistan and 2.33 in India but near zero elsewhere; \mention{haba}
  2339	at 8.78 in Nigeria but near zero elsewhere.
  2340	
  2341	Core interjections show
  2342	intermediate but structured variation: \mention{ah} has the highest
  2343	CV among core items (0.71), with Singapore (60.42) and Jamaica (53.16)
  2344	at eight times the rate of Kenya (7.62). Even broadly distributed items
  2345	like \mention{yeah} (CV 0.55) show a 6:1 ratio between the United
  2346	States (103.78) and Tanzania (16.85). Only \mention{yes} and
  2347	\mention{no} approach uniformity (CVs of 0.21--0.22). Boundary items
  2348	continue the pattern: the fillers \mention{um} (CV 0.61) and
  2349	\mention{uh} (CV 0.76) and the routine formula \mention{bye} (CV 0.61)
  2350	all show structured geographic variation.
  2351	
  2352	These coefficients are estimated from twenty countries, so they carry more
  2353	uncertainty than a bare figure suggests, and it's worth saying which
  2354	comparisons survive it. Bootstrapping over the twenty sections, the
  2355	uniformity of \mention{yes} and \mention{no} is the firm result: their
  2356	intervals sit below eleven of the thirteen other core items, overlapping only
  2357	\mention{hello} and \mention{ha}. The ordering within the rest of the core
  2358	band isn't a result at all. No two of those items have separable intervals,
  2359	so \mention{uh} at 0.76 isn't distinguishable from \mention{yeah} at 0.55,
  2360	and the figures should be read as a single undifferentiated band rather than
  2361	a ranking. For the regional items the coefficient is the wrong summary
  2362	altogether: with a form absent from eighteen of twenty sections the mean sits
  2363	near zero and the coefficient becomes unstable, so \mention{lah}'s interval runs from 0.89 to 4.27. Their concentration is
  2364	better read off the counts than off a dispersion statistic.
  2365	
  2366	Conditioning on country recovers structure at every level of this
  2367	sample. Variety structures the apparent gradience between core
  2368	interjections, fillers, and boundary items.
  2369	
  2370	A Bayesian multilevel model (negative binomial with crossed random
  2371	effects for item and country) supports the same point. Adding country as
  2372	a grouping factor improves prediction over an item-only baseline: the
  2373	country-level random-effect standard deviation is 0.44 (95\% credible
  2374	interval [CrI]: 0.28--0.65), and the conditioned model outperforms the
  2375	unconditional model by 18 expected log predictive density (ELPD) units
  2376	(standard error [SE] 13) on exact leave-one-out cross-validation.
  2377	
  2378	The descriptive CV results are the primary evidence; the model shows
  2379	that the pattern survives a crossed random-effects structure. Full model
  2380	specification and results are in the
  2381	supplementary materials.\footnote{\ifblind Available at
  2382	  [link removed for blind review].\else Available at
  2383	  \url{https://github.com/BrettRey/interjections-hpc}.\fi}
  2384	Several methodological limitations remain. GloWbE's
  2385	part-of-speech tagger, trained on inner-circle English, fails to tag
  2386	regional interjections (\mention{yaar} and \mention{haba} receive no
  2387	interjection tag at all), which means the tagged frequencies
  2388	underestimate regional items. The \mention{ha} row needs a criterion
  2389	rather than a caveat, and the criterion has to be settled before the figure
  2390	rather than after it. Iterated \mention{HA! HA!} is a rendering of laughter,
  2391	closer to eye dialect than to a lexical item; \mention{ha} used for sarcasm or
  2392	scepticism is an interjectional use. The line is harder to draw in a written
  2393	corpus than a spoken one, since nothing was vocalized for the spelling to
  2394	represent, and iterated \mention{haha} in digital writing has arguably
  2395	conventionalized into a form in its own right. Nothing here rests on a claim
  2396	that imitative forms fall outside the category, which would contradict the
  2397	recruitment route described above.
  2398	
  2399	Hand-coding the tagged concordance for all twenty sections shows how much
  2400	turns on that one decision, and the figures reported above count iterated
  2401	laughter rather than excluding it. Laughter is 51.5\% of tagged \mention{ha}
  2402	in the United States and 63.0\% in Great Britain, so the two readings are far
  2403	apart: across six defensible codings of the laughter and residual-token
  2404	questions, \mention{ha}'s coefficient of variation runs from 0.40 to 0.83.
  2405	A single figure would report one path through that set rather than the set
  2406	\citep[cf.][]{gelmanloken2014}.
  2407	
  2408	Two results should be kept apart, because one survives the spread and the
  2409	other doesn't. The dispersion statistic doesn't: it isn't identified without
  2410	the criterion decision, and the 0.41 above corresponds to counting laughter.
  2411	The ranking does. Coding also shows what proportion of tagged tokens are
  2412	interjections at all, which runs from 34.5\% in Bangladesh to 84.0\% in Great
  2413	Britain, with \term{hectare} the contaminant in East Africa and South Asia and
  2414	personal names in Singapore, where only 6.1\% is \term{hectare}. Correcting
  2415	each section by its own rate moves Kenya from fifth place among the twenty to
  2416	fifteenth, and it does so under every one of the six codings. Pooling the
  2417	twenty rates to allow for their unequal precision, which runs from about 8,100
  2418	tagged tokens for the United States down to 420 for Ghana, moves no section by
  2419	more than 0.6 percentage points, because the variance between sections is much
  2420	larger than the sampling variance within them.
  2421	
  2422	Country differences in GloWbE may also reflect corpus composition,
  2423	genre mix, internet ecology, and orthographic convention as much as
  2424	dialect or community structure. A spelling-variant check bears the last of
  2425	these out, in an unexpected direction. \mention{Ya} and \mention{yeh} look
  2426	like variants of \mention{yeah} that a single-string query would miss, but
  2427	both concentrate where they're homographs of substrate words:
  2428	\mention{ya} peaks in Tanzania (19.5 per million) and Malaysia (13.9),
  2429	\mention{yeh} in Singapore (5.6), Pakistan (4.9), and India (4.9), and no
  2430	\mention{yeh} token in the corpus carries the interjection tag at all.
  2431	Folding both strings into \mention{yeah} would drop its dispersion from a
  2432	CV of 0.55 to 0.46, but only by importing non-English tokens unevenly across
  2433	varieties, so the figures below keep the strings apart. Lengthened spellings
  2434	point the same way and put a number on it. Summing \mention{umm},
  2435	\mention{ummm}, and \mention{ummmm} into \mention{um} moves its dispersion
  2436	from a CV of 0.61 to 0.60 once Pakistan is set aside, so lengthening is
  2437	negligible in general; including Pakistan it rises to 0.72, because Pakistani
  2438	English returns nearly eight times more \mention{umm} than \mention{um}
  2439	where the median variety returns a third as much. A twenty-token sample
  2440	settles what that signature is: nineteen are the Arabic teknonym
  2441	\mention{Umm}, as in \mention{Umm Salama}, drawn from hadith and biographical
  2442	material, and the twentieth mentions the filler rather than using it. Raw
  2443	strings are weakest, then, exactly where a variety's substrate supplies a
  2444	homograph, which is a sharper and more testable caveat than orthographic
  2445	variation at large, and one that bears on the varieties this section
  2446	compares. The regional items carry the most exposure, since they're
  2447	substrate-derived to begin with. Hand-coding sixty-nine \mention{yaar}
  2448	tokens puts the English-matrix rate at roughly 60\% in Pakistan and 65\% in
  2449	India, the remainder being Tamil \mention{yaar} \enquote{who}, proper names,
  2450	and tokens sitting inside Roman-script Hindi or Urdu. The rates are estimated
  2451	from twenty-token samples and are too coarse to correct with, but they're
  2452	large enough that raw-string figures for these items should be read as upper
  2453	bounds. Raw strings are imperfect proxies too:
  2454	\mention{yes}, \mention{no}, \mention{ha}, \mention{ah},
  2455	\mention{oh}, \mention{well}, \mention{right}, and \mention{like} are
  2456	not automatically interjection tokens. Some are clausal, quoted,
  2457	discourse-marker uses, literary representations, or noise. The
  2458	methodological payoff is that conditioning by variety reveals structure
  2459	that pooled category labels hide.
  2460	
  2461	A third, weaker probe concerns hub-property asymmetry. One property may
  2462	be more central to network order than others, with prosodic isolation
  2463	and non-referentiality as the main candidates. The defeat condition is
  2464	equal contribution by all properties. The present evidence leaves this
  2465	prediction open.
  2466	
  2467	Onomatopoeic interjections like \mention{pop} can be
  2468	syntactically integrated into clause structure; when prosodic isolation
  2469	is neutralized, as when \mention{pop} in \mention{pop went the cork}
  2470	is an adverb rather than a standalone interjection
  2471	\citep[152]{meinard2015}, reclassification is immediate. Restoring
  2472	partial referentiality, by contrast, allows the form to hover between
  2473	noun and interjection uses (as with \mention{God}). This suggests
  2474	prosodic isolation may be the hub, but the evidence is indirect.
  2475	
  2476	The probes differ in evidential weight. Pathway residue is the main
  2477	explanatory payoff, conditioning by variety is a proof of concept, and
  2478	hub-property asymmetry is an open conjecture. Prototype theory describes
  2479	the gradience but doesn't predict the trajectory of entry, the
  2480	pathway-specific structure of boundary cases, or the structure hidden in
  2481	apparent variation.
  2482	
  2483	At the framework level, if a feature-based account
  2484	(e.g., [\textsc{+peripheral}, \textsc{-argument-taking},
  2485	\textsc{+expressive}]) generates the same predictions without causal
  2486	structure, the network account would be a relabelling. The
  2487	causal-network payoffs are pathway residues and the field-relative
  2488	decomposition; if both fail under further testing, the account collapses to a descriptive
  2489	property list.

  2491	\subsection{Boundary disputes as causal-link overlap}\label{sec:boundaries}
  2492	
  2493	The boundary disputes that embarrassed classical category theory
  2494	(interjections vs.\ nouns, verbs, adverbs, fillers, routine formulae)
  2495	are field-relative predictions of the causal-network account. Each
  2496	boundary reflects partial causal-link overlap and differences in what a
  2497	field needs the category to predict.
  2498	
  2499	Routine formulae (\mention{bye}, \mention{hello}, \mention{sorry}) share
  2500	prosodic isolation and pragmatic function with interjections but add
  2501	addressee-directedness and social predictability
  2502	\citep[109--110]{ameka1992}. For grammar, those shared properties place
  2503	them near interjections. For speech-act analysis, the additional
  2504	properties matter more, because they predict conventional response slots,
  2505	addressees, and felicity conditions. They're structured by additional
  2506	processes (social convention, politeness norms) that prototypical
  2507	interjections don't require.
  2508	
  2509	Fillers (\mention{um}, \mention{uh}) share prosodic distinctiveness and
  2510	syntactic non-integration but differ in emphasis, pause distribution,
  2511	and citation-introduction ability \citep{oconnell2005}. They occupy a
  2512	neighbouring region of the property space, structured by partially
  2513	overlapping links (processing-pressure management rather than
  2514	emotive expression).
  2515	
  2516	The Ameka-Wilkins debate can be reframed in these terms. Ameka's
  2517	separation is right for a field that needs to predict social routines and
  2518	speech-act felicity \citep[109]{ameka1992}. Wilkins's inclusion is right
  2519	for a field that needs to track pragmatic and semantic overlap with
  2520	interjections \citep[142]{wilkins1992}. A causal-network account accommodates both
  2521	observations because projectibility is indexed to explanatory purpose.

  2523	\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
  2524	
  2525	English interjections are a category whose members support projectible
  2526	inferences about use. Assigning a form to it lets an analyst predict
  2527	prosodic packaging, supplement function, use-conditional stance,
  2528	sequential position, response function, and uptake, and the coupling that
  2529	licenses those predictions is what lets speakers and hearers anticipate the
  2530	same behaviour from partial cues. The category is thin as syntax but rich
  2531	as pragmatics.
  2532	
  2533	The reason is that the familiar interjection profile is stable and
  2534	causally ordered. Diachronic pathways recruit forms into the
  2535	profile: grammaticalization via semantic bleaching, pragmaticalization
  2536	via syntactic deactivation, and nonce coinage via onomatopoeia.
  2537	Synchronic links then structure the profile: prosodic-syntactic
  2538	coupling, the non-referentiality ratchet, interactional routine
  2539	entrenchment, and possibly regional/indexical conditioning. These links
  2540	explain why partial observations support further inferences.
  2541	
  2542	The field-relative decomposition shows why the traditional label
  2543	is useful but not uniform. Syntax, semantics, and interactional
  2544	pragmatics track overlapping nodes with different predictive payoffs.
  2545	Boundary cases such as \mention{damn these mosquitoes!}, prenominal
  2546	\mention{damn}, fillers, routine formulae, and obsolete \mention{fie}
  2547	aren't embarrassing exceptions; they show which links are active and
  2548	which have weakened.
  2549	
  2550	The broader pragmatic payoff is path dependence. Interjections don't
  2551	merely express emotion; they enter utterances with histories that shape
  2552	stance, sequence, and uptake. \mention{Gee}, \mention{damn},
  2553	\mention{bruh}, \mention{wowed}, and \mention{fie} matter because they
  2554	show projectible profiles, boundary behaviour, exit residues, and
  2555	failure conditions. They show how a category with no classical essence can support pragmatic
  2556	inference.
  2557	
  2558	The account isn't specific to interjections. Wherever properties cluster
  2559	without a definition holding them, the same questions arise: which links are
  2560	active, what recruited the members, and what happens when a link fails.
  2561	
  2562	The evidence supports a bounded declaration, a stable profile, and network
  2563	order, and it motivates candidate grounds without settling them. It doesn't
  2564	establish corrective control. That limit matters: pragmatic categories can be
  2565	stable and inductively useful without being maintained by a
  2566	perturbation-sensitive control system. English interjections are one
  2567	such category.

summarise_loo <- function(x, label) {
  ks <- loo::pareto_k_values(x)
  bad_k <- sum(ks > 0.7, na.rm = TRUE)
  max_k <- max(ks, na.rm = TRUE)

  cat(sprintf("%s: %d observations with Pareto k > 0.7 (max = %.2f)\n",
              label, bad_k, max_k))

  list(bad_k = bad_k, max_k = max_k)
}

exact_loo <- function(fit, label) {
  x <- loo(fit, reloo = TRUE)
  summarise_loo(x, sprintf("%s after exact reloo", label))
  x
}

diagnostic_loo <- function(fit, label) {
  x <- loo(fit, moment_match = TRUE)
  diag <- summarise_loo(x, sprintf("%s after moment matching", label))
  list(loo = x, bad_k = diag$bad_k, max_k = diag$max_k)
}

# Conditioning model with measurement error on `ha`.
#
# The published model treats every count as exact. The 2026-07-27 audit shows
# that is false for `ha`: hand-coding the tagged concordance for all twenty
# sections puts the share of tagged tokens that are genuine interjections
# between 34.5% and 84.0%, and the shortfall is regionally patterned (hectare in
# East Africa and South Asia, personal names in Singapore). A point-estimate
# correction would understate the uncertainty, so the validity rate enters as a
# beta posterior per country and is propagated by refitting over draws.
#
# Two questions:
#   1. Does the country random effect survive correcting `ha`?
#   2. Does the model comparison (m1 over m0) survive it?
#
# Note the audit measures precision only. Tokens the tagger missed are not
# recoverable from a tagged concordance, so this corrects false positives and
# not false negatives.

# One replicate: draw each country's validity rate from Beta(k+1, n-k+1),
# thin `ha`'s count by it, leave every other item untouched.
make_replicate <- function(spec) {
  if (spec == "uncorrected") return(long)
  k <- if (spec == "laughter_in") validity$k_laughter_in else validity$k_laughter_out
  p <- rbeta(nrow(validity), k + 1, validity$n_coded - k + 1)
  draw <- tibble(country = validity$country, rate = p)
  long %>%
    left_join(draw, by = "country") %>%
    mutate(count = if_else(item == "ha", as.integer(round(count * rate)), count)) %>%
    select(-rate)
}

fit_pair <- function(d) {
  m0 <- brm(count ~ 1 + offset(offset) + (1 | item), data = d,
            family = negbinomial(), chains = 2, cores = 2, iter = 1500,
            warmup = 750, seed = 2026, save_pars = save_pars(all = TRUE),
            silent = 2, refresh = 0)
  m1 <- brm(count ~ 1 + offset(offset) + (1 | item) + (1 | country), data = d,
            family = negbinomial(), chains = 2, cores = 2, iter = 1500,
            warmup = 750, seed = 2026, save_pars = save_pars(all = TRUE),
            silent = 2, refresh = 0)
  l0 <- loo(m0); l1 <- loo(m1)
  # Compute the difference directly rather than from loo_compare's row order,
  # which is sorted by fit and so cannot be indexed positionally with a fixed
  # sign. Positive means the country model predicts better.
  d_point <- l1$pointwise[, "elpd_loo"] - l0$pointwise[, "elpd_loo"]
  adv <- sum(d_point)
  se  <- sqrt(length(d_point)) * sd(d_point)
  sd_country <- as.data.frame(m1)[["sd_country__Intercept"]]
  list(elpd_diff = adv, se_diff = se,
       sd_med = median(sd_country),
       sd_lo = quantile(sd_country, .025), sd_hi = quantile(sd_country, .975))
}

"""Bootstrap intervals for the per-item coefficients of variation.

The CVs in §6.1 are computed across twenty country sections and reported as
point estimates, then compared with one another. Twenty units is few enough
that the comparisons need intervals before they can carry weight. This
resamples countries with replacement and reports percentile intervals, then
lists which pairwise comparisons survive.
"""
import csv
import random
import statistics as st
from pathlib import Path

SRC = Path(__file__).parent.parent / 'data' / 'glowbe-interjection-frequencies.csv'
REGIONAL = {'lah', 'yaar', 'haba'}


def cv(v):
    return st.stdev(v) / st.mean(v)


def main(B=4000, seed=11):
    random.seed(seed)
    est = {}
    for row in csv.DictReader(SRC.open()):
        v = [float(y) for k, y in row.items() if k != 'item']
        boots = sorted(cv([random.choice(v) for _ in v]) for _ in range(B))
        est[row['item']] = (cv(v), boots[int(.025 * B)], boots[int(.975 * B)])

    for it, (c, lo, hi) in sorted(est.items(), key=lambda r: -r[1][0]):
        print(f"  {it:8} {c:5.2f}  [{lo:5.2f}, {hi:5.2f}]")

    core = [k for k in est if k not in REGIONAL]
    mid = [k for k in core if k not in ('yes', 'no')]
    sep = [(a, b) for i, a in enumerate(mid) for b in mid[i + 1:]
           if est[a][1] > est[b][2] or est[b][1] > est[a][2]]
    print(f"\n  separable pairs within the core band (excluding yes/no): "
          f"{len(sep)} of {len(mid)*(len(mid)-1)//2}")
    top = max(est['yes'][2], est['no'][2])
    print("  yes/no separate from: " +
          ', '.join(sorted(k for k in mid if est[k][1] > top)))


if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()

# Jeffreys intervals for proportions
jeffreys_ci <- function(k, n, alpha = 0.05) {
  lo <- qbeta(alpha / 2, k + 0.5, n - k + 0.5)
  hi <- qbeta(1 - alpha / 2, k + 0.5, n - k + 0.5)
  c(lo, hi)
}

fit_decline <- function(k_col, n_col, data, label) {
  cat(sprintf("Fitting %s...\n", label))

  formula <- bf(paste0(k_col, " | trials(", n_col, ") ~ t"))

  priors <- c(
    prior(normal(0, 1.5), class = "Intercept"),
    prior(normal(0, 0.5), class = "b", lb = NA, ub = 0)  # beta < 0
  )

  fit <- brm(
    formula,
    data = data,
    family = binomial(),
    prior = priors,
    chains = 4, cores = 4, iter = 4000, warmup = 2000,
    seed = 2026,
    silent = 2
  )

  fit
}

# Draft Preregistration: `fie` in COHA, 1820-2019

## Status and Scope

This is a preregistered analysis plan for an existing corpus, not a preregistration before data collection. The confirmatory commitments here concern extraction, coding, modeling, and reporting for one target item, `fie`, in COHA from 1820 to 2019.

The analysis is conditional on attested `fie` tokens. It is not a model of the overall frequency decline of `fie` in COHA, though raw token counts by decade will be reported.

## Research Question and Prediction

**Research question:** Within attested COHA tokens of `fie`, do the interactional, semantic, and syntactic properties associated with interjectionhood decline at different rates across time?

**Pre-committed prediction:** The properties should fray in the order interactional first, semantic second, syntactic last. In terms of ED50 parameters, the predicted ordering is:

```text
tau_int < tau_sem < tau_syn
```

where each `tau` is the calendar year at which the model-estimated probability of a property falls below 0.5.

## Data Source and Extraction

- Corpus: Corpus of Historical American English (COHA), 1820-2019.
- Target item: the orthographic token `fie`, matched case-insensitively as a standalone word.
- No variant pooling: forms such as `fye`, `fy`, `fieh`, or longer strings containing `fie` are excluded unless they are tokenized as the exact word `fie`.
- Unit of analysis: one attested token of `fie` in running text.
- Time bin: decade.
- Context for coding: the sentence containing the token, plus the immediately preceding and following sentence when available.

Duplicate hits created by export or query artifacts will be removed by document ID, year, and concordance context. Repeated uses of `fie` within the same document remain separate tokens.

## Go/No-Go Threshold

Before coding or modelling, the following minimum data requirement must be met:

- **Total analysable tokens:** at least 50
- **Decade coverage:** at least 3 decades with 3+ tokens each

If either threshold is not met, the study is reported as pre-registered but untested due to data sparsity. The prediction remains on the record. No modelling, no hand-waving, no exploratory substitution.

These thresholds are set before data extraction and will not be revised after inspecting token counts.

## Inclusion and Exclusion Rules

Included:

- Tokens of `fie` used in running English text, including dialogue and quoted speech.

Excluded:

- Metalinguistic mention rather than use, as in discussion of "the word `fie`"
- Dictionary headwords, glossaries, bibliographies, page headers, running heads, advertisements, and other metadata
- Non-English passages
- OCR noise or unreadable hits
- Hits with too little readable context to apply the coding rubric

All exclusions will be counted and reported.

## Coding Protocol

Each analyzable token will receive three binary codes, assigned independently:

| Property | Code `1` if... | Code `0` if... |
|---|---|---|
| `interjection_syn` | `fie` is syntactically non-integrated: it does not fill an argument, complement, or modifier slot in the surrounding clause, and its removal leaves no grammatical gap. Standalone uses and clause-peripheral exclamations count as `1`. | `fie` is syntactically integrated, metalinguistically quoted, or otherwise functions as part of ordinary clause structure. |
| `interjection_sem` | `fie` contributes expressive or evaluative stance without referential or truth-conditional lexical content. | `fie` refers to the word itself, contributes descriptive lexical content, or is unusable as an expressive token in context. |
| `interjection_int` | `fie` functions as an interactional move or stance display directed at the speech situation, addressee, or prior talk, such as exclamation, reproach, reaction, or turn-level stance marking. | `fie` does not function as an interactional move in context. |

Additional coding rules:

- The three properties are coded independently. A `1` on one property does not force a `1` on the others.
- For `interjection_int`, code the represented speech act, not the author's act of writing it. A character saying *fie* to another character in a novel counts as a live interactional move if it functions as stance display within the fictional dialogue.
- Coding will be done from a randomized coding sheet with the decade column hidden, so the coder is blind to time period during coding. Note: archaic syntax in concordance lines may partially reveal period; this is unavoidable but mitigated by randomisation.
- The full rubric and token-level coding sheet will be published in the supplement.

## Coding Reliability

Brett will complete a blind second-pass recode on a simple random 10% subset of analyzable tokens, rounded up to the next whole token. The subset will be sampled with random seed 2026 and presented in randomized order with original codes hidden.

This is an intra-coder stability check, not an external inter-coder validation. The following will be reported for each property:

- Percent agreement
- Cohen's kappa

If disagreement exceeds 20% on any property, that coding criterion will be flagged as underdetermined. The main analysis will still be reported under the preregistered rubric; the rubric will not be revised after inspecting disagreement patterns except to correct clerical errors.

## Descriptive Reporting Before Modeling

Before fitting any model, the paper will report:

- Total analyzable `N`
- Per-decade `N`
- Raw proportion per decade for each property
- 95% Jeffreys intervals for the decade-level proportions

The raw proportion plots come first, before any model-based estimates.

Decades with `N = 0` will be shown in the count table and count plot but will not enter the property model because there is no denominator for a property proportion.

## Confirmatory Statistical Model

For each property separately, let:

- `n_d` = number of analyzable `fie` tokens in decade `d`
- `y_pd` = number of those tokens coded `1` on property `p`

The confirmatory model is a Bayesian logistic decline fitted to decade-level binomial counts:

```text
y_pd ~ Binomial(n_d, theta_pd)
logit(theta_pd) = alpha_p + beta_p * t_d
beta_p < 0
```

where `t_d` is decade midpoint, centered and scaled in 10-year units.

The `beta_p < 0` constraint assumes monotone decline: `fie` can only lose properties over time, not rebound. This is a substantive assumption, not just regularisation. Both the confirmatory and sensitivity models are monotonic; neither can detect a revival period.

This simple logistic form is the confirmatory model for two reasons:

- it removes the researcher degree of freedom involved in choosing between a smoother and a simple decline
- it makes the ED50 parameter exact rather than interpolation-dependent

### Priors

- `alpha_p ~ Normal(0, 1.5)`
- `beta_p ~ Normal(0, 0.5)`, truncated to negative values

These are weakly regularizing priors on the log-odds scale. They allow shallow or steep decline but rule out implausibly erratic trajectories for a single item observed by decade.

### Estimation and Diagnostics

- 4 chains
- 2000 warmup iterations and 2000 post-warmup iterations per chain
- random seed: 2026

Convergence criteria:

- no divergent transitions
- `Rhat <= 1.01` for population parameters
- bulk ESS and tail ESS of at least 400 for population parameters

If the confirmatory model fails these diagnostics after standard tuning adjustments, that failure will be reported explicitly. It will not trigger a post hoc change in the confirmatory model.

## Confirmatory Estimand

For each posterior draw and each property, define:

```text
tau_p = -alpha_p / beta_p
```

and transform `tau_p` back to calendar year. This is the ED50: the year at which the model-estimated probability of the property equals 0.5.

If `tau_p < 1820`, it will be reported as "before 1820." If `tau_p > 2019`, it will be reported as "after 2019." These are not treated as failures of estimation; they mean the 0.5 crossing lies outside the observed window.

The primary confirmatory quantity is:

```text
Pr(tau_int < tau_sem < tau_syn)
```

This probability will be reported as a posterior quantity, not converted into a binary success/failure decision.

## Sensitivity Analysis

As a robustness check, I will also fit a Bayesian logistic model with decade treated as an ordered monotonic predictor rather than a linear slope. Because ED50 is not exact under this parameterisation, the sensitivity model will report the posterior distribution of the decade at which each property's predicted probability crosses 0.5, obtained by numerical interpolation of the fitted curve. The following will be reported:

- Posterior distributions of the interpolated crossing decades for each property
- `Pr(tau_int < tau_sem < tau_syn)` computed from the interpolated posteriors
- Visual comparison of the fitted curves with the confirmatory model

This sensitivity analysis will be reported regardless of outcome. It is not the confirmatory model, and it will not replace the confirmatory model on the basis of fit, sparsity, or substantive convenience.

## Sparse Data and Uninformative Results

No decade will be excluded because of low token count. If any decade has fewer than 5 tokens, it remains in the analysis.

If posterior intervals are wide or order probabilities are diffuse, that will be reported as an informative outcome rather than treated as a failed analysis. The primary result is always `Pr(tau_int < tau_sem < tau_syn)`. The following labels are informal glosses on that probability, not independent judgments:

- "consistent with the predicted ordering": `Pr(tau_int < tau_sem < tau_syn) > 0.75`
- "consistent but uninformative": `Pr(tau_int < tau_sem < tau_syn)` between 0.25 and 0.75
- "inconsistent with the predicted ordering": `Pr(tau_int < tau_sem < tau_syn) < 0.25`

## What Will Be Reported Regardless of Outcome

- Total analyzable `N`
- Per-decade `N`
- Exclusion counts by reason
- Raw proportions by decade with 95% Jeffreys intervals
- Posterior distributions of `tau_int`, `tau_sem`, and `tau_syn`
- `Pr(tau_int < tau_sem < tau_syn)`
- Convergence diagnostics
- Reliability statistics for all three coding variables

No post hoc threshold-based exclusion beyond the preregistered go/no-go rule, no post hoc model switching, and no selective reporting will be used.

The supplement will include:

- the token-level dataset

interjection\_syn before interjection\_sem.


in the earliest analyzable derivative tokens for each source form. If
the earliest analyzable verbal tokens are syntactically integrated but
semantic residue is already absent, the morphology-gain perturbation does
not support the predicted ordering.

### 1. One-sentence summary

The paper argues that English interjections constitute a genuine lexical category because observing part of their prosodic, syntactic, semantic, and interactional profile licenses defeasible predictions about unobserved properties, with corpus studies probing conditioning, pathway residue, and failure.

### 2. Strengths

- The empirical claims are unusually well bounded. Section 6.1 calls the GloWbE analysis a “proof-of-concept probe,” identifies the CVs as primary, and explicitly separates these tests from corrective control ([main.tex:2300](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2300), [main.tex:2378](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2378)). The conclusion likewise distinguishes established stability and network order from merely candidate grounds ([main.tex:2562](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2562)).

- The `ha` audit is used well within its proper scope. Coding all twenty sections exposes country-varying precision, demonstrates that the CV depends materially on whether laughter counts, and propagates the resulting uncertainty through sensitivity refits ([main.tex:2399](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2399), [main.tex:2753](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2753)). The explicit distinction between precision and recall is exactly right ([main.tex:2619](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2619)).

- The preregistered COHA work reports an informative failure rather than rescuing the hypothesis: the predicted ordering for `fie` receives posterior probability 0.13 and is declared unobserved ([main.tex:2947](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2947)). Blinded time coding and reported intra-coder reliability further strengthen that result ([main.tex:2893](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2893)).

### 3. Weaknesses

- The GloWbE estimand does not match the strongest prose. The paper predicts that apparent category gradience should “decompose” under conditioning and concludes that country “recovers structure at every level” ([main.tex:2324](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2324), [main.tex:2366](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2366)). But m1 adds only a common country random intercept; it tests whether overall token rates differ by corpus section, not whether item types or category boundaries behave differently by country. The relevant varying-slope model, m3, is exploratory and cannot be ranked reliably ([main.tex:2694](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2694), [main.tex:2710](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2710)). Moreover, observation-level LOO predicts a missing cell for an already-observed item and country; it does not test generalization to a new item or variety. Recast the result as “GloWbE section predicts overall rates,” or fit an adequately supported item-by-country/type interaction and use leave-one-item- or leave-one-country-out validation appropriate to the intended projection. Given ΔELPD = 17.9 with SE = 13.3, “directionally favourable but uncertain” is warranted; “outperforms” or “preferred” is too firm ([main.tex:2370](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2370)).

- The validity disclosures are sufficient to warn readers but also undercut the variety interpretation. Core counts are tag-restricted, whereas `haba` and `yaar` are raw strings; recall can be zero, regional raw strings contain non-English and proper-name tokens, and “country” belongs to a webpage rather than a speaker ([main.tex:2627](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2627), [main.tex:2652](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2652)). Correcting `ha` precision shows that this one contaminant does not drive m1, but it validates neither the other seventeen outcomes nor recall. The manuscript should report actual audit sample sizes, sampling protocol, coding reliability, and sensitivity models restricted to consistently measured, manually validated items. Otherwise “country” must be interpreted as corpus-section composition—including genre and internet ecology—not linguistic variety.

- The COHA conclusions need narrower wording. In the morphology study, overt derivatives are selected and the success criterion then includes `verbal_syntax = 1`; thus syntactic integration is substantially built into the sampling frame, while the real empirical result is preservation of broadly coded source semantics among 10–17 early tokens per family ([main.tex:2774](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2774), [main.tex:2823](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2823)). Report that conditional result rather than a general morphology-gain trajectory. For `fie`, Pr(predicted ordering)=0.13 supports rejection of that ordering, but overlapping marginal ED50 intervals do not establish “simultaneous decline” ([main.tex:2947](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2947), [main.tex:2955](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2955)). Establish simultaneity with a joint model and a declared equivalence margin for pairwise ED50 differences; also report predictive checks for the monotonic logistic form given the acknowledged late rebounds.

### 4. Key question

What precisely is the held-out prediction that “country conditioning” improves—an unseen token for a known item and corpus section, a new item, or a new English variety—and can that prediction be demonstrated on consistently hand-validated outcomes rather than tagger-dependent counts?

### 5. Verdict

**Revise & Resubmit** — the theoretical argument is strong and the transparency exemplary, but the principal GloWbE model does not test the decomposition claim attributed to it, and both the measurement regime and COHA interpretations require substantial recalibration.
