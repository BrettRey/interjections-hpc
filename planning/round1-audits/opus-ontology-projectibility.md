# Claude Opus 4.6 Thinking: ontology and projectibility audit

## Verdict

The approved three-category architecture is coherent, but `main.tex` is not yet compliant. Three high-severity defects recur: bare *interjection* receives fourth-category standing in the abstract and conclusion; `INTERJECTION_syn` repeats supplement function on admission and target sides; and `INTERJECTION_prag` repeats backchannel behaviour on both sides. The strongest surviving objection is not local circularity but relabelling: without a genuine cross-category held-out success, the three categories may add no projective value beyond CGEL interjections, Potts-style expressives, and response or liminal signs.

## Strongest surviving objection

The three categories map closely onto existing classifications. If the rewrite cannot produce at least one non-trivial cross-category prediction whose source membership is established independently and whose target is genuinely held out, then the architecture may be a useful redescription without the advertised projective payoff. Known canonical convergences and known boundary cases do not answer this objection; a held-out form or unobserved environment would.

## Lead verification notes

- The reviewer is right about leakage and fourth-category force, but several declaration statuses are too generous. D1–D5 depend on links whose evidential status is itself contested; D5 in particular is not automatically demonstrated by ordinary distributional examples.
- D15 is conditional on a preselected derivative sample and an inclusion criterion that already requires VP-head syntax and semantic residue. It demonstrates residue within that conditioned design, not a general exit process.
- The reviewer correctly treats D13 as supported/reconstructive and D16 as failed. The evidence/methods audit will determine the final ledger.
