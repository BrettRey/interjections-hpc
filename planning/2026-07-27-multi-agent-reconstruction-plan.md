# Multi-agent reconstruction plan
## Outcome
Produce one architecturally coherent paper with a single authorial voice, using agents mainly to expose error, assemble evidence, and pressure-test claims. The lead editor alone owns the ontology, claim hierarchy, and prose in `main.tex`.

The paper's governing architecture is:

1. There are three legitimate, overlapping interjection categories: grammar-facing, semantics-facing, and pragmatics-facing, with the third centred on interactional pragmatics.

2. Their common bearer is a conventionalized linguistic sign-type whose grain may be fixed by contrasts in grammar, meaning, or interaction, with no grammatical priority.

3. The subscripts index the principal family of projections each category supports, not the disciplinary provenance of all its properties.

4. Bare _interjection_ is a conventional cover for canonical cases satisfying all three classifications. The overlap has no independent category standing and need not generate a fourth projective payoff.

5. Projective claims, not categories themselves, are the primary bearers of warrant.

## Load-bearing assumptions to test first
| Assumption | What would falsify it? | First test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The existing manuscript can be reconstructed rather than replaced. | The three-category architecture cannot be stated without discarding most empirical sections or changing their inferential role. | Map every section to retain, rewrite, relocate, or cut under the governing architecture. |
| The three categories have independently defensible extensions. | Their membership criteria merely rename broader classes such as expressives or response tokens, or are tailored around the examples. | Produce a comparative category table with bearer, admission criteria, contrasts, cases, and independent targets. |
| The three categories support distinct projective families. | Once admission criteria are removed from the target side, a category has no non-trivial target left. | Build a source–target–warrant matrix for every claimed projection. |
| The causal network adds more than a flat cluster or constructional inventory. | Residues, asymmetries, and failure patterns are equally captured without causal ordering. | Run an explicit rival-account audit against each empirical probe. |
| The present empirical record warrants more than a prospective hypothesis. | Forward projections are supported only by examples or by properties used in admission. | Grade each declaration as demonstrated, supported, motivated, or open. |

The strongest anti-thesis is: _the manuscript identifies three useful descriptive classifications and several histories, but supplies no evidence that any classification supports licensed reach beyond its admission criteria_. Every major revision should make that anti-thesis harder to sustain.
## Agent design
### Lead editor — single owner
The lead editor:

- fixes the architecture and terminology;

- writes the new outline and claim hierarchy;

- decides among conflicting reports;

- rewrites all load-bearing prose;

- is the only agent permitted to edit `main.tex` during reconstruction;

- maintains Brett's voice and the manuscript's calibrated evidential language.


This role should not be parallelized.
### Specialist agents — bounded, read-only workstreams
Run no more than three specialist agents alongside the lead in any round.

1. **Category ontology and projectibility**

  - Test the proper-noun/proper-name analogy.

  - Specify the common bearer and the status of bare _interjection_.

  - Audit admission–target leakage and field-relativity.

  - Deliver a source–target–warrant matrix with exact manuscript locations.

2. **Linguistic architecture and rivals**

  - Test the three cuts against CGEL, expressives, response tokens, liminal signs, fillers, and routine formulae.

  - Ask whether each extension is discovered or manufactured.

  - Compare the account with feature, prototype, and constructional analyses.

  - Deliver a category-comparison table and a rival-account objection matrix.

3. **Evidence and methods**

  - Audit GloWbE, morphology-residue, _fie_, turnover, regional counts, and pathway claims.

  - Separate what the analysis estimates from what the prose says it establishes.

  - Deliver a claim–evidence ledger classifying every empirical claim as established, supported, illustrative, reconstructive, or unsupported.

4. **Source and historical integrity**

  - Verify diachronic pathways, quotations, source characterizations, and the status of unread or proxy sources.

  - Distinguish documented histories from reconstructive hypotheses.

  - Deliver only source-grounded corrections and unresolved research requests.

5. **Adversarial journal review**

  - Used only after a coherent rewrite exists.

  - Review as a philosopher of science, CGEL grammarian, interactional linguist, historical pragmaticist, and methods reviewer.

  - Deliver ranked fatal, major, and minor objections, each with a concrete defeat condition.

## Where redundancy is worth paying for
Use independent redundant outputs only twice:

1. **Architecture gate:** two models independently test whether the three-category ontology, common bearer, and non-category status of the overlap are coherent and accurately represented by the proposed outline.

2. **Submission gate:** two models independently review the complete revised manuscript without seeing each other's reports.


Do not request redundant outputs for source extraction, numerical checking, bibliography work, or mechanical prose edits. Those tasks benefit more from division of labour and verification than from multiple broad opinions.

## Execution rounds
### Round 0 — Architecture contract

**Status: complete and approved, 27 July 2026.** The contract was independently reviewed by Agy/Gemini 3.1 Pro and Agy/Claude Opus 4.6 Thinking, revised once, and approved in Roughdraft after the common bearer was made field-neutral.

The lead writes a two-page architecture contract fixing:

- bearer;

- three category cuts;

- meaning of each subscript;

- status of bare _interjection_;

- category-versus-label distinction;

- declared central contribution;

- strongest warranted world-side commitment;

- claims explicitly not made.


Two independent reviewers test the contract. The lead revises it once. No manuscript editing begins before Brett approves it.

**Gate:** Can every central claim be stated without treating the overlap as a fourth category or privileging syntax ontologically?
### Round 1 — Manuscript decomposition
Three specialist agents work in parallel on:

- ontology/projectibility;

- linguistic categories/rivals;

- evidence/methods.


They write separate Markdown reports and never edit `main.tex`. Each finding must include:

- exact location;

- claim at issue;

- severity;

- reason;

- evidence or argument required;

- recommended disposition: retain, rewrite, relocate, cut, or investigate;

- confidence.


The lead combines the reports into one revision map and resolves contradictions explicitly.

**Gate:** Does the empirical core survive the new architecture with an honest inferential role?
### Round 2 — New outline and declaration matrix
The lead produces:

- a new section outline;

- a hierarchy of central, supporting, and illustrative claims;

- the three category definitions;

- a source–target–bearer–scope–warrant–failure matrix;

- a claim–evidence ledger;

- a retain/rewrite/relocate/cut map for the old manuscript.


Brett reviews this combined artifact in Roughdraft.

**Gate:** Does each category retain at least one non-trivial, independently warranted projection, or is its status explicitly prospective?
### Round 3 — Reconstruction
The lead rewrites the argumentative spine in this order:

1. three-node section and terminology;

2. projectibility section;

3. causal-network framework and evidential grading;

4. discussion;

5. introduction and abstract;

6. conclusion and title.


Specialists may supply narrowly requested evidence packets while the lead writes, but they do not draft competing replacement sections and do not edit the source.

After each major section, run one targeted review against the architecture contract. Avoid full-board review until the manuscript reads coherently end to end.

**Gate:** Does every strong verb correspond to evidence in the ledger, and does every projection exclude its admission criteria from its targets?
### Round 4 — Independent submission review
Freeze a review copy. Run:

- one redundant pair of whole-paper reviews from different model families;

- one methods/evidence audit;

- one linguistics/category audit.


Reviewers work independently. The lead groups correlated objections rather than treating reports as votes.

The lead then writes a disposition table: accept, modify, reject, or investigate, with reasons. Brett reviews only decisions that would materially alter the architecture or central claim.

**Gate:** Can a hostile reviewer accurately state the paper's ontology, principal evidence, strongest result, and defeat conditions after one reading?
### Round 5 — Single-voice polish and verification
Only after substantive closure:

- one prose editor performs the final voice pass;

- run projectibility, style, proofreading, and bibliography audits;

- build and visually inspect the PDF;

- verify all figures, captions, numerical claims, quotations, and source statuses;

- run one final title–abstract–conclusion consistency check.


No multi-agent prose rewriting occurs in this round.
## Coordination rules
- Agents never edit the same file concurrently.

- Review agents are read-only unless the lead requests a bounded patch.

- Every report names evidence and exact locations; unsupported taste judgments are discarded.

- The lead treats multiple model reports as correlated evidence, not votes.

- Contradictions are surfaced in a disposition table rather than silently averaged.

- New claims enter the manuscript only after being added to the declaration and evidence matrices.

- A failed declared prediction is demoted, split, scoped, or retired—not rescued by post-hoc narrowing.

- Stop broad review cycles once they cease producing new high-severity objections.
