# KWIC audit: `haba` in GloWbE Nigeria

Two samples, 2026-07-27. `haba_uh` returns nothing, so these are untagged
string queries. The second was pulled with better randomization at Brett's
initiative, and it overturned two conclusions from the first.

## Why both are kept

| sample | n | distinct sites | top-2 site share |
|---|---|---|---|
| first (interface default) | 18 | 7 | 50% (daikeji ×5, nigeriafilms ×4) |
| randomized | 20 | 13 | 35% |

**What the first sample got wrong.** From it I coded zero substrate-matrix
tokens and a uniformly standalone or utterance-initial position. The
randomized sample contradicts both:

- **Hausa matrix appears.** *"Haba mutane mu, ku karba Yesu as the Lord and
  saviour"* is Hausa, not English. Two further tokens sit in Hausa vocative
  frames: *"Haba mallam kura!"*, *"haba Mallam!"*
- **A medial, intensifying use appears.** *"it is constitutionally wrong and
  haba very bad for teachers and students"*, where *haba* is inside the
  clause rather than set off from it.

Neither category is common, which is why a sample clustered on five blog
sources missed them entirely. Rare categories are what randomization buys.

## Coding, randomized sample

Roughly: English matrix, standalone or initial ~10; Nigerian Pidgin matrix
~6; Hausa-involved 3; English matrix, medial intensifier 1.

**The Pidgin count is not a judgement I should be trusted on.** Whether
*"Haba, dis is too much for me to bear"* or *"Haba! Thirty thousand wen
eighteen million naira waz returned"* counts as English text is a question
about the Nigerian English–Pidgin continuum, and Brett is not a speaker of
the variety either. It needs the literature, not either of our intuitions.

## Comparison across the three regional items

On orthographic position, from the well-spread samples:

| form | position | bearing on \intj{syn} |
|---|---|---|
| *lah* (MY) | clause-final, attached, 20/20, punctuation always follows | fails prosodic isolation |
| *yaar* (PK) | mobile: initial, medial, final; frequently comma-set | plausibly satisfies it |
| *haba* (NG) | usually standalone or initial with `!`, but at least one medial intensifier | mostly satisfies it |

The three are not the same categorial kind. Any claim that they share the
full interjection profile has to be made item by item, and none of these
positional readings substitutes for a prosodic study.
