## Why this is worth doing, beyond patching a concession

§2.1 quotes \textcite[108]{ameka1992} twice: interjections are "always separated by a pause from the other utterances with which they may co-occur" and "always constitute an intonation unit by themselves." The paper then hedges to "strongly characteristic rather than exceptionless" because the diagnostic matrix records \mention{um} as partial. That hedge is currently a judgement call. Both claims are stated in units that a prosodically segmented corpus measures directly, so the judgement could become a rate.

**The stronger use is calibrating the proxies rather than replacing them.** If punctuation, standalone position and non-integration can be shown to track actual intonation-unit boundaries at some measurable rate, the proxy list stops being a confessed limitation and becomes a validated instrument. That retroactively strengthens the GloWbE and COHA work instead of competing with it, and it is a better return than bolting a prosody section onto a paper that doesn't need one.

## Santa Barbara Corpus (SBCSAE) -- the right instrument

Verified at source 2026-07-26, not from memory:

- **Free**, transcripts *and* audio (WAV/MP3)
- 60 conversations, SBC001--SBC060, roughly 249,000 words
- CC BY-ND 3.0 US (analysis fine; note the no-derivatives term before redistributing processed data)
- **Timestamps aligned at the level of individual intonation units**, which is the same unit Ameka's claim is stated in
- Two transcript formats: `.trn` (LDC Callhome) and `.ca` (CHAT, for CLAN/TalkBank)
- <https://www.linguistics.ucsb.edu/research/santa-barbara-corpus-spoken-american-english>; audio also via TalkBank; physical media via LDC (LDC2000S85, LDC2003S06, LDC2004S10)

No REB needed: secondary analysis of a public corpus. That makes it markedly cheaper than the two experiments currently blocked on Humber REB.

## London-Lund, if a spoken diachronic arm is wanted

LLC-1 carries prosodic annotation by design: tone units, location and direction of nuclear tones, and pauses graded by length. LLC-2 is contemporary spoken British English, built on LLC-1's principles precisely to support diachronic comparison, and released with audio alongside enhanced orthographic transcription, so prosodic annotation is left to the user.

Caveat worth stating up front: it is British, and the paper's diachronic evidence is COHA, which is American. Pairing them introduces a variety confound that would have to be handled rather than ignored.
