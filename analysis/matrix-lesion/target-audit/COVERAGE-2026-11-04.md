## CGEL supplementation (P1-6)

Source: `literature/00-CGEL.md` (printed: Huddleston and Pullum 2002,
Ch. 15, Coordination and supplementation). Survey checked around
subsections (h) PPs and AdvPs [30] and (i) Interjections [31],
printed p. 1360 (PDF p. 1380).

Findings:

1. The anchor-negation scope sentence ("The supplements differ from
   the most central modifiers in that they do not fall within the scope
   of negatives in the anchor...") closes subsection (h), illustrated
   by *in my opinion* [30ii] and *as you know* [30iii], immediately
   before subsection (i).
2. Subsection (i) explicitly classes central interjections (*ah, hey,
   oh, oops, ouch, sh, ugh, wow*) as used "on their own, or as
   supplements with clausal anchors" [31i], and *damn* in [31ii] as
   reanalysed interjection in supplements.
3. **Scope ruling (Brett Reynolds, 2026-11-04, from the printed
   page): the supplement properties stated in (h), including
   exclusion from anchor-negative scope, are general properties of
   supplements and apply to interjections, even though they appear
   before the interjection subsection.** Inheritance of
   PROP-P11-CGEL-SCOPE to DAMN_REACT, OUCH, and WOW therefore stands
   on a recorded reading, not automatic label matching. It remains a
   single proposition in a single outcome direction.
4. Checked absence: the supplementation survey contains no further
   anchor-negation scope generalization and no interjection-specific
   negation statement. Remaining scope discussion in the chapter
   concerns *or*-coordination under negation (source of the COFFEE
   control, PROP-P11-CGEL-OR) and *if*-scope in distinguishing
   supplements from coordinates. Neither adds P11 evidence.
5. Packet coverage for this proposition was adequate: `packets-v3`
   P11 excerpts include the scope sentence together with the full
   subsection (i) inclusion text. The pre-correction report's remark
   that CGEL's supplement chapter was touched "only obliquely" does
   not apply to this proposition. It remains untested as a P12
   (prosody) source: no hosted-prosody coding was attempted from
   Ch. 15 in this pass, and none is claimed.

## Heine prosody qualifications (P1-7)

Source: `literature/heine_2023_grammar-of-interactives_ch1.md`,
section 1.3.1.4 closing paragraphs (lines 247-249), immediately before
section 1.3.1.5, in the same discussion as every audited P12
generalization.

Qualifying propositions, coded as PROP-P12-HEINE-QUAL in
`proposition-map.json`:

- "When combining with other text units, especially in fast speech,
  prosodic boundaries may be weakened or disappear entirely" (Heine et
  al. 2021: 51-52, via Heine).
- O'Connell, Kowal, and Ageneau (2005): 45% of interjections occurred
  without preceding or following silent pauses; no evidence for
  temporal isolation in German and English media interviews; singling
  out by loudness, intonation, articulation rate instead.
- Biber et al. (1999: 1082): English inserts "have a tendency to
  attach themselves prosodically to a larger structure".

Packet check: none of these passages appears in `packets-v3.md` (five
string probes, zero hits), while the generalizations they qualify
were retrieved and coded. Packet content was therefore more
categorical than its source section.

Coding decision: no coder assertions were fabricated. The
qualifications are recorded at proposition level. PROP-P12-HEINE-SETOFF
and PROP-P12-HEINE-LEFTPERIPH are marked contested. Their audited
strengths record what the source generalizations claim; they must not
be read at freeze time as settled characteristic hosted separation.
The qualifications do not by themselves create negative cells for
sampled items. They do establish that builder coverage, not only
source silence, limited this audit. Any production packet builder must
retrieve qualification and counterevidence passages with the same
priority as generalizations, and CONFLICT must be an expected outcome
class for P12.

## Potts repeatability, Quirk emphasis, Stange token (P1-8)

Potts (2007) section 2.6 read in full (`literature/Potts - 2007 - The
expressive dimension.md`). The section frames repetition across
discourses ("items of this type are used repeatedly", "build
throughout a discourse"), exemplifies with mixed-position accumulation
(34a-c) and discourse repetition (35), and treats *big big big* as a
compositional descriptive exception. Checked absence: no claim of
adjacent repetition of a single specified *damn* use, and no within-use
adjacent effect. The distributed-recurrence[mixed] correction stands
as the section's only *damn* reading.

Quirk et al. (1985) checked in triage: note [b] (pp. 982-983) plus
section 18.58. Emphasis effect explicit; adjacent pattern not
established for the OK claim. Filed in verdict correction notes for
PROP-P10-QUIRK-EMPH.

Stange (2016) checked in triage: ch. 4, Side note 1, example (65).
Source discards the token as *whoa*. Both assertions rejected as wrong
use (PROP-P10-STANGE-WHOA65). Full context was present in packets, so
this failure is coding and model review, not retrieval.
