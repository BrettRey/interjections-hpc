## Gutzmann (2013), "Expressives and beyond": property extraction for the 46 bearers

I read the whole chapter: printed pp. 1–51 (PDF pp. 2–52). The offset is confirmed: PDF page 3 has the running head "2 DANIEL GUTZMANN", so printed page = PDF page − 1. No RESPONSIBILITY NOTICE is needed.

**The finding most likely to change Brett's coding (row 45):** Gutzmann classes expressive adjectives *in intensifier use* as **mixed (truth-conditional)**, not expletive. He names only *fucking* and *goddamn*, in "Rufus is fucking tall". That bears on DAMN_DEGREE and BLOODY_INTENS, but only at class level; the chapter never puts *damn* or *bloody* in that frame. He also calls *very* a "truth-conditional intensifier" whose repetition strengthens without redundancy (rows 38–39).

**No bastard use from the 46 appears.** Every *bastard* example is the NP-internal epithet "that bastard Kresge", which is neither BASTARD_SUPP nor YOU_BASTARD. Nondisplaceability, immediacy, perspective dependence and the question test are established on that use, so they're listed in the tail, not the table.

**How the quotations are copied:** each newline in the file is replaced by one space, and nothing else is changed. So words hyphenated at a line end appear as in the file ("exple- tive", "need- ing"). Curly apostrophes (’) and the em-dashes the file uses for minus signs ("(—functional)") are kept. Property labels that combine two names (e.g. "P07 (=[−2d])") mean one statement supports both.

### 2. NEW properties

- **NEW:FUNCTIONAL [±f]**, p. 27 ("which I call their functionality"). Defined on p. 4: "whether a use-conditional expres- sion applies to an argument or already comes saturated". 
  - [−f] (isolated): ouch, oops, Damn!, Oh!, interjections as a class; also slurs (cur, Kraut).
  - [+f]: damn attributive; epithets and expressive attributive adjectives as a class; interjectional *man* with a gradable adjective (p. 31); the English personal dative "I want me an iPod" ("not isolated", p. 15); verum *do* (its argument is the proposition, p. 20).
  - This is a semantic property. Slurs are integrated in the syntax yet isolated (p. 9), and fn 17 separates damn's syntactic argument from its attitude target. So it is not P04, and it can't be read off P03.
  - The source does link it to distribution within expletives: saturation leads to standalone use ("Therefore", p. 6), and isolation is "mirrored by" peripheral-only position (p. 6).
- **NEW:TWO_DIMENSIONAL [±2d]**, p. 26 ("I call this criterion dimensionality"): whether a UCI "carries truth- conditional meaning as well". I coded it through P07 (−2d gives P07 Y; the truth-conditional part of a +2d item gives P07 N) rather than duplicating rows.
  - [−2d]: ouch, oops, damn/bloody/fucking attributive, interjections.
  - [+2d]: fucking/goddamn as intensifiers, *man*, cur, Kraut/Boche, du/Sie and tu/vous, Japanese *mono*.
  - The test is omission, or comparing truth conditions (pp. 5, 33).
- **NEW:RESOURCE_SENSITIVE [±rs]**, p. 28: "shunting UCIs consume their argument whereas functional expletives do not". It applies only to one-dimensional functional UCIs.
  - [−rs]: damn attributive (p. 30).
  - [+rs]: Japanese *yokumo*, German non-inflected verb constructions (*grins*), and unexpectedness intonation, which is hedged ("could be understood", p. 27) and contradicted in fn 24, p. 19 ("ui is not a shunting UCI").
  - No English word in the 46 is [+rs].
- **NEW:UCI_TYPE** (Table 1, p. 28; the OCR of the table cells is garbled, so I reconstructed it from the text on pp. 27–28 and the p. 48 summary). There are five types:
  - isolated expletive (−f −2d): ouch, oops.
  - isolated mixed (−f +2d): cur, Kraut.
  - functional expletive (+f −2d −rs): damn attributive, *ja*, personal datives, verum; topicalization is listed as expletive on p. 26.
  - shunting (+f −2d +rs): see the previous bullet.
  - functional mixed (+f +2d): interjectional *man*.
- **NEW:ATTITUDE_TARGET** (my label for the source's notion): what the use-conditional content applies to, whether nothing, a nominal argument or a proposition (pp. 12, 27).
- **NEW:MULTIFUNCTIONAL**, p. 10: one UCI in several functions. damn is attributive and interjective; fucking/bloody are attributive and intensifying, and the dimension feature changes between those uses. p. 6 adds man and shit as interjective uses of other words. items-v4 already captures this by splitting uses.
- **NEW:INDEX_LIKE**, p. 49: "more like indices/signals instead of symbols". Class only.
- **NEW:TARGETS_PRIOR_TC_CONTENT**, p. 35: hedged, NO only.
- **Mapped to existing properties, not new:** "appear only in peripheral positions" maps to P03 N. Omissibility is the test for P07 / −2d. Scopelessness (p. 9, citing Potts 2005: 41, tested on Kraut) maps to P11N. Potts's repeatability maps to P10S.

### 3. Warnings against using a property as a diagnostic

- **Repeatability:** "Contra Potts (2007c), I think that repeatability is neither a necessary nor sufficient condition for use-conditional content and therefore, it cannot be used to differentiate between truth-conditional and use-conditional con- tent." (p. 47). Truth-conditional counterexamples: *big big big*, *very very very* (p. 46); isolated UCIs aren't repeatable (p. 47); Geurts's objection in fn 40 (p. 47); restated on p. 49. Relevance to the study: P10S Y is not diagnostic of the EXPRESSIVE grouping.
- **Descriptive ineffability (Potts's version):** "The theoretical status of descriptive ineffability as a sound property of use-conditional content is not clear." (p. 42); "not something that is unique to use-conditional content alone" (p. 43); the property "does not prove to be very helpful" (p. 49). Only the modified version (92), p. 44, is kept.
- **Nondisplaceability:** "nondisplaceability seems to be not as strong a constraint on use-conditional meaning as Potts’s (2007c) formulation in (79) suggests" (p. 40). Also: "Some are relatively easy to shift while others may never be displaced." (p. 40).
- **Negation, denial and question tests (P11N):** "they alone are not suffi- cient to show that an expression contributes use-conditional content since other kinds of meaning, like presuppositions, show the same behavior with respect to negation and questions" (p. 37). The denial test is also confounded by the answer particle *no* (p. 35), and "I didn’t know that" only looks like a denial (p. 36).
- **The properties aren't independent of one another:** "These, however, follow more or less from the fact that UCIs are not part of the truth conditions of the sen- tence in which they are used." (p. 32). Immediacy is "also a consequence" of the same fact (p. 45). Counting Potts's six as separate diagnostics would count one fact several times.
- **UCIs are not a natural class:** "I do not want to presuppose that all expressions that contribute use-conditional instead of truth-conditional content form a natural class of expressions." (p. 32). Common properties are "a bonus and not as a necessary con- dition" (p. 32).
- **Supplement position doesn't diagnose use-conditional meaning:** "it is not obvious that supplements convey conventional non-truth-conditional content. Instead, it is reasonable to assume that they primarily have truth- conditional content." (p. 22). This bears on reading P02 as expressive evidence.
- **Functionality is semantic, not positional:** slurs "can be regarded as being isolated UCIs as well" although they are integrated (p. 9); see also fn 17 (p. 12).
- **Features vary by use, not by form:** p. 10 on *fucking*/*bloody*, and "To study the relations between different functions of expressives and, more generally, UCIs in order to find generalizations regarding their shiftability is an interesting research question" (p. 10).
- **The word-level groupings are loose:** "The resulting categorization is, of course, not clear-cut and serves primarily the function of providing structure" (p. 4).
- **Inconsistencies inside the chapter:**
  - It says "four types" on p. 31 (twice) but five on pp. 27–28.
  - Interjections are "expressives in the narrow sense" on p. 6 but "other kinds of UCI besides expressives in the narrow sense" on p. 47.
  - *man* is listed among the expletive interjections on p. 6 but analysed as mixed on p. 31.
  - Unexpectedness intonation is a shunting UCI on p. 27 but explicitly not one in fn 24, p. 19.
