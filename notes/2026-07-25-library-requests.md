Three of the six unread interjection papers resisted every open route. Routes worked for each: publisher DOI page, CORE, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Unpaywall, OpenAIRE/BASE, the author's institutional repository, the journal's own archive, and the Internet Archive. The other three were obtained and are now cited (Elffers 2008, Cuenca 2002, Wharton 2000).

All three stay commented out in `references-local.bib` until read. Nothing uncited-because-unread gets a live entry.

## 1. Cruz (2009) — easiest, try this first

**DOI: `10.2478/v10016-009-0015-9`** (resolves in one step through a library proxy)

- Quoted title: `"Might interjections encode concepts? More questions than answers"`
- Locator: *Lodz Papers in Pragmatics* vol. 5, no. 2 (2009), pp. 241–270
- Publisher: De Gruyter / Sciendo

**Why it failed openly:** De Gruyter returns HTTP 202 with a zero-byte body from every path (bot challenge, not a paywall). A logged-in browser session should sail through. The journal is nominally open access, so the library link may not even be needed, just a real browser.

## 2. Poggi (2009) — closed, metadata confirmed from the publisher

**DOI: `10.1007/978-3-642-00525-1_17`**

- Quoted title: `"The Language of Interjections"`
- Locator: in *Multimodal Signals: Cognitive and Algorithmic Issues*, Springer (2009), pp. 170–186
- ISBN: 978-3-642-00525-1

Pagination and DOI verified against the Springer chapter page on 2026-07-25. The chapter PDF is genuinely closed (both `/content/pdf/` and `/chapter/` return HTML). A library SpringerLink subscription is the route; many institutions hold the LNCS/LNAI series in full.

## 3. Cram (2008) — hardest, no DOI found

- Quoted title: `"The Exceptional Interest of the Interjection"`
- Author: David Cram
- Locator: *Henry Sweet Society Bulletin* vol. 50 (2008), pp. 57–65
- ISSN: 0267-4971 *(inferred from the Taylor & Francis DOI stem `10.1080/02674971`, which encodes the journal ISSN; worth a glance to confirm)*

**Why it failed:** no OpenAlex, CORE, or Semantic Scholar record. The society's own site returns 404 at the bulletin archive path. The volume is on Taylor & Francis (the sibling article, Elffers 2008 in the same volume, carries `10.1080/02674971.2008.11827189`), so **searching T&F for the journal and browsing to volume 50 is the likely route.** A DOI adjacent to Elffers's probably exists but I won't guess at the suffix.

Fallback if T&F has no volume 50: the Bulletin is a small society journal, so an ILL request on the title-plus-volume locator is more reliable than chasing a link.

## What each bears on, if obtained

- **Cram** — the historiographic framing in §1. His title suggests the same reversal the paper makes (interjections as unusually interesting rather than marginal), so he may be a predecessor worth crediting or a position worth distinguishing from.
- **Cruz** — §2.4 directly. He is in Wharton's relevance-theoretic line, asking whether interjections encode concepts. Now that Wharton is engaged in §2.4, Cruz is the natural follow-up and may complicate the encode/activate distinction the section leans on.
- **Poggi** — §2.5 and the interactional node. Her multimodal angle bears on prosodic isolation and on gesture-accompanied use, which the paper touches but doesn't develop.
