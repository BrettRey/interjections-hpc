1. **Answer in one sentence:** Choose a modified **A**: put compact operational definitions immediately after the three nodes are introduced in the Introduction (after lines 268–275), leave §3.4 to justify the field-relative decomposition, and reserve §5.2 for genuinely non-definitional projections.

2. **Reasoning**

- The strongest case for the current order is coherent: §2 first establishes the empirical properties, §3.4 then interprets their regrouping through the causal-network framework, and §5.2 finally cashes out the predictive payoff. This avoids presenting theoretically loaded nodes as empirical givens.

- Nevertheless, Brett’s instinct is right. The Introduction decodes the subscripts at lines 268–275, so they aren’t wholly undefined notation, but it doesn’t give criteria for assigning a form to a node. That becomes a real problem when Table 1 records node membership at lines 735–805. A reader cannot independently interpret or contest those checkmarks without reading forward to §3.4.

- The manuscript actually advertises the problem: lines 296–298 say that “the subscripts stay out of the way” until §3.4, although they occur at line 513 and repeatedly from lines 662–805. I would flag that inconsistency, but as a correctable exposition defect, not a substantive objection.

- The damaging defect is the collapse of definition into projection. For the semantic node, lines 1077–1084 and 1896–1901 recycle essentially the same Potts-derived properties; the source attribution has not been independently verified here. Thus “membership projects meaning type” presently amounts to “membership in the cluster defined by meaning-type properties predicts those properties.” That directly invites the charge that projectibility is distributional description with philosophical relabelling.

- The subscripts themselves are not objectionable. They’re economical notation for potentially non-coextensive analyses, and the footnote at lines 1055–1070 explains the choice. I would complain only if the nodes remain extensionally or inferentially indistinguishable. The problem is what the subscripts do, not their typography.

3. **What each of the three sites should carry**

- **Introduction, after lines 268–275:** Give the minimum operational specification needed to read Table 1: whose classification each node represents, the evidence used to assign membership, and one extension-separating example. Call these *identification* or *coding criteria*, not projections. Include no causal-link claims and no catalogue of predicted consequences.

- **§3.4 “Field-relative projectibility”:** Keep the interrogative/question analogy, the argument for why different inquiries may track non-coextensive groupings, and the evidence that the three extensions genuinely diverge. Put the causal ground of each node here only insofar as it justifies the decomposition. Remove the property inventories and every generic “projects X” sentence. This section should answer: *why three nodes rather than one?*

- **§5.2 “What each node projects”:** State source-to-target inferences that go beyond the early identification criteria. For the syntactic node, resistance to *that*-complementation, coordination, and phrase-internal modification can do real work if those weren’t used to assign membership. For the semantic and interactional nodes, the present lists must be divided into identification evidence versus independently testable outcomes. If no independent semantic outcomes can presently be defended, say so and demote that node’s projectibility claim; repeating nondisplaceability and ineffability isn’t a projection.

4. **Strongest objection to my own answer**

Early operational definitions risk making the three-way decomposition look stipulated before §2 has established the underlying facts. But the paper has already incurred that cost by using the nodes in the Introduction and Table 1. A compact coding specification makes the commitment inspectable; it needn’t pre-empt §2’s evidence. Simply adding an early summary while retaining both later lists would be worse than the present arrangement.

5. **Cost if Brett does nothing**

A real reviewer could notice both defects, but the likely formal complaint is the **definition/projection duplication**, because it bears directly on whether projectibility contributes anything beyond ordinary distributional analysis. I would treat that as a major conceptual weakness and use it in support of rejection. The forward dependency is noticeable—especially in Table 1—but probably merits only a pointed request for reorganization. The subscripts alone wouldn’t enter my grounds for rejection; their failure to mark independently motivated categories with non-circular predictive payoffs would.
