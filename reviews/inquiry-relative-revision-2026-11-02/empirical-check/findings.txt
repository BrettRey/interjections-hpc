3. The full-sample assignment is automated; the 40-row check is a development check

analysis/filter-ha-concordance.py:17-60 is a deterministic regular-expression classifier, with outputs interjection, hectare, name, typo, or unclear. It uses reduplication, laughter words, punctuation, units, numbers, name patterns, and typography rules. analysis/pool-ha-rates.py:22 imports that classifier; lines 41-53 apply it to every exported row. Laughter and unclear-token decisions are then varied mechanically. Exact replay of all 20 stored validity rows leaves no independent human adjudication step in this computation.

The header and comments call the 40 KE/TZ examples hand-coded. More informative provenance is in the original commit 9d87451be58c3ecb416ddfee8142d0d25babad82: it explicitly says the initial filter scored 88% and two rules were added to raise performance to 39/40 on these same examples. It also acknowledges that "South Korea's Ha" had been wrongly gold-labelled hectare. The label file and classifier first appear together in that commit. Therefore this is a development-set check: the rules were adjusted after inspecting its errors. It is not held-out validation of the resulting classifier, and it cannot establish country-general accuracy. The scoped archive also does not independently establish the identity of the person who supplied the initial labels merely by calling them hand-coded.

Current archived-code replay against the 40 stored labels gives:
  Interjection versus non-interjection agreement: 39/40.
  Exact category-label agreement: 37/40.
The exact-label mismatches are validation line 13 (stored hectare, predicted name: South Korea's Ha), line 27 (stored hectare, predicted typo: "3 ha School"), and line 28 (stored hectare, predicted interjection: garbled text containing "ha?"). Only line 28 changes the binary retained/not-retained decision. This explains why 39/40 is reproducible for the relevant binary task while not describing five-category accuracy.

Required correction: describe a rule-based screen checked and tuned against 40 labelled Kenyan/Tanzanian examples. Describe the twenty-country rates as screen-derived estimates under the specified treatment of laughter and unclear cases. Do not turn that in-sample check into independent manual validation, a transferable accuracy guarantee, or a claim that every uncertain row was adjudicated. The sensitivity calculations show dependence on selected coding choices; they do not quantify all classifier errors or establish representative/random sampling of the exported contexts.

4. What the leave-one-out comparison predicts

analysis/conditioning-model.R:20-46 reshapes 18 item rows across 20 country columns into 360 country-item aggregate count observations. Each row is a single likelihood observation. The model formulas at lines 93-129 use item intercepts, and in m1/m2 country intercepts; m2 also includes item type. The comparison at lines 161-179 calls loo(fit, reloo = TRUE) without a country-, item-, document-, or token-level grouping holdout.

Accordingly, pointwise LOO removes one aggregate cell. The training data still contain 359 cells, including the same item's counts in the other 19 countries and the same country's counts for the other 17 items. For m1/m2, the country effect is learned from those other items and the item effect from those other countries. Other model parameters and item-type information are also learned from the retained data. The country corpus-size offset remains supplied.

The estimand is predictive performance for an omitted aggregate within the observed item-by-country design. This is a coherent target if stated; it is not a test on an entirely unseen country, entirely unseen item, held-out document population, later period, or newly classified sign-type. It also does not test the incremental payoff of the manuscript's proposed inquiry-relative classifications. A change of generalization target would require a different validation design, not a reinterpretation of the existing numbers.

Terminology qualification: the code requests pointwise LOO with reloo. The reloo flag enables exact refitting for problematic observations; it does not itself establish that all 360 folds were refitted exactly. "LOO with reloo for problematic observations" is safer than an unqualified assertion of fully exact leave-one-out refits. No model was refitted in this check.
