Source: L. R. Franklin-Hall, "Natural kinds as categorical bottlenecks," *Philosophical Studies* 172: 925–948 (2015), doi:10.1007/s11098-014-0326-8. I read it in full. Printed page = PDF page + 924, and every quotation was checked against its page.

## 1. The account

She builds her account by repairing two weaker anti-realist views. The *simple pragmatist* view says kinds are the categories that best serve our aims. It fails because odd aims would make a kind of "three ceiling fans and a geranium" (938). The *simple epistemic* view restricts the aims to epistemic ones. She cites Magnus (2012) and Boyd (1991) as instances: they "make the natural kinds relative to ‘fields’ or ‘disciplinary matrices’" (938), and n. 18 says she is setting aside most of their theories. This view is partially objective, "invariant across assessors enjoying distinct stances or points-of-view" (938). Two features of the world secure that. One is robust clustering: other inquirers' features "will often belong to the same clusters that we have identified" (939). The other is "multi-use" architecture: neurons serve many explanations (939). But clustering isn't everywhere. For immunological polymorphism and dental wear, "it seems that different categories would have been appropriate" (940). So field-relative kinds "will still depend on what our particular epistemic projects … happen to be" (940).

Bottlenecks are the categories that we and many inquirers with other aims "would sanction in common, thereby converging on a single set of categories and kinds from multiple, distinct starting positions or points-of-view" (940). The principle: "the natural kinds are groupings that match those categories that well serve actual inquirers along with (what I will call) ‘neighboring agents’—those differing somewhat from actual inquirers in their particular epistemic aims and cognitive capacities" (940). Stance-independence becomes "the defining property of natural kinds" (940). A category needn't be optimal for every agent, "but rather that it well serve the purposes of most" (940). A footnote then sets out a ladder. Pathological kinds serve neither us nor most neighbours, and "The neutral kinds correspond to categories that well serve either actual inquirers or some group of neighboring agents, but not both." (940 n. 21).

**Alternative agents.** Neighbours lie "within some threshold of closeness to us" in an epistemic agent space (940). The space's structure and the threshold are "fixed by us, rather than being properly objective" (941 n. 22). Because capacities vary as well as aims, mere familiarity (QWERTY, the constellations) can't pass as naturalness. And "many of these neighboring inquirers exist, not in adjacent possible worlds, but in our own future" (946). The brief's "wide range" comes from the abstract ("a wide range of alternative agents", 925). The principle she actually uses says neighbours differ only "somewhat" (940). Three linguistic inquiries into the same expressions are plausibly neighbours, so Brett's case falls inside her space.

**Anti-realist yet objective.** The principle is status-dependent: it is "made true by us" (944). Its grounding is normative, in reflective coherence (945). In the end it depends "on our values, particularly on what purposes we think the entire theoretical apparatus of science should serve" (947). The objectivity is the invariance described above, and the world's causal architecture supplies it (927). She assumes feature realism (934) and stays agnostic on the causal order (927 n. 3).

## 2. The inquiry-relative paper

**Support.** Inquiry relativity is her premise, not her target. Of the different ways ecologists and evolutionary biologists partition organisms, she says "Each classification is rational given the distinct patterns each type of scientist is motivated to predict and explain" (933). Her clustering passage (939) gives a world-side reason why crosscutting classifications are "mutually informative". The paper claims that membership in the lexical category projects to meanings, conversational uses, intonational packaging, and phonology. That is just what she would count as evidence of a bottleneck.

**Challenge.** In her terms the paper defends the simple epistemic view at a finer grain (inquiries rather than fields). Its categories are therefore more stance-sensitive than those of the view she rejects. On her ladder, a category that serves one inquiry well isn't natural, and n. 21 makes it neutral at best. She doesn't say how sub-inquiries count within "actual inquirers", so the exact rung is unclear. She ranks categories; the paper holds that "Several classifications can be correct without one superseding the others."

**What the paper needs to say.** Three things:
- Category standing (projectibility within an inquiry) isn't naturalness.
- Breadth of service is a further, comparative achievement. The paper can recognize it without letting it supersede inquiry-local categories.
- Its kind of objectivity is weaker than hers: invariance across investigators who share a question, not across questions.

Suggested wording, for §1 after the Dupré/Khalidi paragraph or for §8.1:

> Franklin-Hall (2015: 940) reserves natural-kind status for categories that serve both actual inquirers and most neighbouring agents, whose epistemic aims and capacities differ somewhat from ours. Category standing here is a narrower achievement, warrant for projection within an inquiry. A category that also serves neighbouring inquiries, as the lexical category interjection is argued to do for meaning and realization, has a further, comparative achievement, and lacking it doesn't demote a class that serves one inquiry well.

> Each inquiry sets what counts as success, but once its question is fixed, evidence settles membership and projection, so verdicts are invariant across investigators who share the question, though not across inquiries.

## 3. The study

There's a partial analogue. `properties-v4.csv` assigns each property a field stratum, and FREEZE-v4 §2 ("Multiple targets") fixes the strata for stratified reporting. The proxy would be a grouping's per-projection gains, loss(M0) − loss(M2), laid out across the strata it isn't assigned from:
- INTJ is assigned from distribution, morphology, and P07. Its outward targets are in morphosyntax (P06), meaning (P08, P09, P10S, P11N), and realization (P12, P13).
- EXPRESSIVE is assigned from meaning. Its targets reach distribution, morphology, morphosyntax, and realization (P01–P06, P09, P12, P13).

Her "most" suggests summarizing with a median or a share of strata, not a mean. v4 forbids choosing summaries after the fact, so this one would have to be declared in advance and reported as a profile with no cutoff.

The INTJ-versus-EXPRESSIVE comparison on shared targets is a pairwise version. A grouping that wins on every shared target is the more bottleneck-like on that sample; a reversal is inquiry dependence. The target loadings of the Round 7 two-feature model would show most directly which grouping carries which stratum. The leave-one-source-property-out experiment measures cohesion (her robust clustering), not bottleneck-ness.

Limits:
- (a) Only recovery of concealed properties is measured. Her "serve" also includes representation and explanation (938).
- (b) Cognitive capacity never varies.
- (c) Interaction, the paper's third inquiry, isn't measured. P14 is a descriptor, and sequential-action projections are outside v1 (§5).
- (d) The strata are the analyst's individuation of points of view. She grants "it remains doubtful that any ordering of what is more or less partially objective could itself be objective, as that would require an objective individuation of points-of-view" (938 n. 19), though n. 22 lets us fix it.
- (e) Diffuse association could produce breadth on its own. The bonds null world has to separate the two first.
- (f) The pools of outward targets are thin: P10 and P11 are sparse, and P12 is shallow.

## 4. Conflicts with Brett's commitments

1. **What confers standing.** For her it's convergence across agents; for Brett it's projectibility beyond the assignment evidence. Her criterion demotes categories that project well within one inquiry.
2. **One set or several.** She speaks of "a single set of categories" (940); Brett holds that several groupings are correct. Her remark about pluralism concerns realism (929). I found no passage on whether two bottlenecks can crosscut, so the question is unaddressed, not denied.
3. **Values or evidence.** She grounds kinds in our values (947). The paper says "The evidence, rather than the label or departmental boundary, determines which proposed categories deserve recognition." The conflict is mild: on her view values choose the principle, and evidence then applies it.
4. **Silence, not conflict.** She says nothing on bearers, converging diagnostics, absences, or single-property membership, and she treats categories as groupings of individuals.
