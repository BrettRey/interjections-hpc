### 1. One-sentence summary

The paper argues that English interjections constitute a genuine lexical category because partial phonological, syntactic, or semantic evidence licenses defeasible predictions about unobserved grammatical and interactional behaviour, with those correlations explained by convergent historical pathways and synchronic causal coupling.

### 2. Strengths

- The strongest version of the argument is genuinely stronger than “interjections share several properties”: the paper explicitly distinguishes observed from predicted properties and prohibits using an admission criterion as its own prediction ([§1, lines 219–230](</Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:219>)). In principle, that converts category status into an empirical question about held-out predictions.

- The paper is unusually candid about evidential status. It labels routine entrenchment an assumption rather than a finding ([§4, lines 1557–1570](</Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:1557>)), documents serious GloWbE measurement problems ([§6.1, lines 2384–2458](</Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2384>)), and reports that the preregistered *fie* prediction failed ([Appendix C, lines 2933–2936](</Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2933>)). That is excellent scholarly practice.

- The pathway-residue proposal is the paper’s most promising substantive contribution: complement retention, euphemistic deformation, and regional concentration are treated as historically structured boundary behaviour rather than miscellaneous exceptions. This could become a useful diachronic generalization if tested against serious alternative analyses.

### 3. Weaknesses

- **The projectibility apparatus never demonstrates predictive value beyond an ordinary distributional description.** The paper itself concedes that the syntactic predictions are available to “any framework recognizing the relevant morphosyntactic properties” ([§5.1, lines 2072–2078](</Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2072>)). The supposedly distinctive cross-domain predictions are asserted immediately afterward but never evaluated on unseen forms ([lines 2079–2091](</Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2079>)). Worse, the paper ultimately calls the category label “the analyst’s bookkeeping device” and says that cue-driven coupling does the inferential work ([lines 2098–2114](</Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2098>)). That largely concedes the hostile reader’s point: a learner exploits distributions; “projectibility” redescribes that fact. The necessary fix is a held-out prediction study: classify unfamiliar items using a stipulated subset of cues, predict independently annotated semantic and sequential properties, and compare accuracy against cue-only, feature-based, and constructional baselines. Without incremental predictive value, the philosophical framing earns nothing.

- **The empirical probes don’t test the central claim.** The GloWbE study shows that word frequencies vary by webpage country. It doesn’t show that conditioning decomposes categorial gradience, that the cluster properties co-occur, or that any proposed causal link exists. Even its model comparison is modest relative to its uncertainty, \(\Delta\)ELPD \(=17.9\), SE \(=13.3\), while item variation greatly exceeds country variation ([Appendix A, lines 2731–2751](</Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2731>)). The morphology-gain probe establishes that inflected derivatives such as *wowed* head VPs while retaining recognizable source meaning ([Appendix B, lines 2804–2845](</Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2804>)); that is ordinary lexical-semantic persistence, not evidence for interjectional network order. Most damagingly, the paper defines the residue hypothesis’s alternative as “random distribution” ([§6.1, lines 2311–2319](</Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2311>)). Randomness is a straw comparator: historical, constructional, and conventional distributional accounts also predict source-specific residues. Either replace these illustrations with direct token/type-level tests of conditional dependencies among diagnostics, preferably using spoken interaction, or sharply reduce the empirical claims.

- **The category claim lacks a falsification threshold and is protected by the analysis’s flexibility.** Exceptions can be reassigned among three field-relative nodes; the traditional category is then merely their “zone of overlap” ([§3.4, lines 1218–1233](</Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:1218>)). A failed prediction identifies a broken link or, in the *fie* case, becomes a “boundary condition” caused by community-wide attrition ([§3.3, lines 1100–1104](</Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:1100>)). Reclassification likewise handles loss of prosodic isolation ([§5.4, lines 2286–2296](</Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2286>)). The nominal concession that the account collapses if an alternative generates the same predictions ([§6.1, lines 2483–2489](</Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2483>)) is not operationalized. The paper needs an explicit decision rule: how much out-of-sample predictive improvement must the category provide after the observed cues and construction are controlled, and what result would require abandoning—not merely relocating—a case? It must also decide whether it is defending a lexical category, the thin \(\mathrm{Intj}_{syn}\) node, or a broader family of expressive and interactional constructions. At present those are allowed to substitute for one another.

### 4. Key question

What concrete result, with what quantitative threshold, would make you conclude that *interjection* is not a genuine lexical category, rather than redescribing the result as a boundary case, a different field-relative node, community-wide attrition, or failure of one causal link?

### 5. Verdict

**Reject** — the manuscript offers an admirably transparent and potentially valuable distributional-diachronic description, but it neither demonstrates that projectibility adds predictive power nor supplies a falsifiable test of the claimed lexical-category status.
