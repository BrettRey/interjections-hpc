# Revision decisions: inquiry-relative categories
The paper's question is what warrants treating a grouping as a category for a particular inquiry. Its answer should be demonstrated through a completed comparison, with English interjections supplying the case. This is a bounded revision of `inquiry-relative-categories.tex`; the existing `main.*` files stay as they are. No new corpus collection or model fitting is proposed.
## 1. Category, class, and descriptive standing
I propose an explicit local terminology: a **class** is a grouping assembled for a stated purpose; **classification** assigns objects to such groupings. A **category** has an empirically supported role in organizing a recurrent distinction, inference, or explanation; **categorization** assigns an object to such a category. Convenience classes can be useful without thereby establishing a category. {==This wording==}{>>use the wording from my Words that won't stand still book<<}{id="c1" by="user" at="2026-11-03T01:09:19.548Z"} is our convention, not a distinction that Dupré, Khalidi, or Haspelmath themselves make.

Descriptive standing will mean warranted use for a specified descriptive task. A second-property prediction is not required. The expressive case accomplishes something definite: it preserves a semantic relation across independently established grammatical constructions which a single grammatical interjection category obscures. That descriptive achievement does not establish every expressive diagnostic, a shared mechanism, or predictive improvement.

I propose to define the semantic category broadly as **conventional expressive uses conveying an evaluative or affective stance**, with the selected interjection-related uses as the worked slice. `INTJ_sem` will be a local reference to that slice, not a new name for all expressives. I will distinguish this from conventional instructions for managing interaction.

For the interactional proposal, I will replace unspecified “minimal” with a bounded working criterion: conventional, non-elliptical formats that can themselves perform a sequential action without a lexically specified host. Continuers, repair initiators, and closings qualify under that proposal. Ordinary imperatives do not qualify merely by standing alone; their action can be compositionally supplied by clause structure. Host-dependent politeness markers require a separate account. The boundary remains a proposal to be compared with narrower action categories.
## 2. A completed assessment and an honest membership table
The main positive case will develop Ameka's distinction between phonological and grammatical groupings. It will identify the disputed grammatical conclusion, the phonological evidence offered for it, the descriptive grouping that evidence can support, the unresolved grammatical issue, and the strongest alternative analysis. The result should be determinate: phonological resemblance can support an explicitly phonological comparison without settling grammatical category membership. A further claim about a coherent phonological stratum remains open.

I will state explicitly that the present table demonstrates expressive crossing. Its grammatical and interactional columns coincide over the displayed cases; their difference is motivated by their questions and evidence, not an extensional dissociation demonstrated by that table. I will not insert a weak new example simply to fill a cell.

For _mm-hm_ and _huh?_, I will explain the conventional non-elliptical uses and the additional grammatical basis for treating them as interjections, or mark the assignment provisional where the checked source does not settle it. _Bang!_ requires comparison with its independently attested nominal uses. Standalone occurrence alone will not serve as a sufficient diagnostic in either case.
## 3. Bearers and an adverse case
The complement-taking table row will identify **the lexical head _damn_ in the complement-taking construction**, distinguishing its category from the external distribution of the whole phrase. I will work through one explicitly hypothetical adverse observation: what initially identifies the conventional use, what unexpected distribution would revise its profile, and what independent recurrent evidence would justify recognizing a further use. A failed prediction alone will not warrant splitting the sign.

The _gee_ history will remain a documented explanation of one outcome. I will say directly that this does not establish the comparative merits of a broader ancestry category.
## 4. Intellectual positioning and Hill
Dupré and Khalidi will supply the pluralist background; Haspelmath will supply the related distinction between language-particular description and cross-language comparison. The contribution here is the joint treatment of bearers, competing linguistic categories, different achievements, and the scope of evidential failure.

Hill's nine considerations will enter only the assessment of a specified causal explanation: strength, consistency, specificity, temporality, biological gradient, plausibility, coherence, experiment, and analogy. Hill called them viewpoints and rejected both a mandatory checklist and an automatic proof. Any linguistic analogue of biological gradient needs a defensible intensity variable. They will not become nine requirements for recognizing a descriptive category.

A short application will show why, for example, the history of _gee_ does not support a claim that newly acquired prosodic isolation caused its semantic change: the source invocation was already peripheral, and the proposed causal contrast has not been observed.

## Subsequent direction and implementation

Brett's later direction supersedes the proposed three-part architecture above: the revision now uses named groupings, makes expressive and depictive signs a central comparison within semantic inquiry, and distinguishes contribution, iconic presentation, procedural encoding, and rival analyses. No category quota is assigned to a field or inquiry.

The book's terminology also supersedes the looser descriptive-standing proposal above: descriptive classes can be scientifically useful, but empirical categories require co-occurring properties that support induction for particular purposes.
