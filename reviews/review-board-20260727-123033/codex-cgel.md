## 1. One-sentence summary

The paper argues that English interjections constitute a genuine lexical category because a causally ordered cluster of prosodic, morphosyntactic, semantic, and interactional properties licenses defeasible predictions from observed properties to unobserved ones.

## 2. Strengths

- The strongest version of the argument is clear and appropriately falsifiable: the paper distinguishes declaration, warrant, profile, stability, network order, and grounds, and explicitly declines to infer homeostasis from stability ([§1, lines 218–245](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:218)). The failed preregistered prediction for *fie* is reported as a failure rather than redescribed as support ([§4.6, lines 1828–1858](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:1828)).

- The paper understands that supplement is a function, not a lexical category: §2.3 explicitly shows NPs and relative clauses in the same function and says, correctly, “The function is shared; the category differs” ([lines 448–468](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:448)). Distinguishing word families, lexemes, and construction-specific profiles is likewise necessary and well handled ([§1, lines 324–333](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:324)).

- The pathway-residue argument is the paper’s strongest empirical contribution: retained complementation, euphemistic deformation, and atypical phonology are formulated as differentiated retrodictions rather than as undifferentiated prototype effects ([§4.6, lines 1737–1803](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:1737)).

## 3. Weaknesses

- The paper has not yet established *interjection* as a lexical category on distributional grounds. Its criterion for \(\textsc{interjection}_{syn}\) is supplement function plus absence of complementation or construction with neighbouring material ([§2.6, lines 822–833](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:822)), but supplement is a function, while morphological invariability and non-argumenthood characterize many expressions outside the proposed category. Indeed, the inference that non-inflection prevents determiner-taking or agreement control is invalid ([lines 421–444](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:421)). Section 5.3 then supplies mostly semantic tendencies, and “NP supplements inflect freely” attributes lexical inflection to a phrase; comparison with “mint[ing] new relative clauses” mixes lexical coinage with phrase formation ([lines 2190–2212](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2190)). The revision needs distributional paradigms distinguishing Intj heads from N, V, Adj, and Adv heads, a separation of standalone IntjPs from IntjPs in supplement function, and an explicit account of exceptional complementation. At present *damn these mosquitoes* is both only partially \(\textsc{interjection}_{syn}\) because it takes a complement ([lines 868–876](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:868)) and unqualifiedly \(\textsc{interjection}_{syn}\) because it heads an IntjP ([§5.2, lines 2167–2178](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:2167)).

- The three “field-relative nodes” are presently more notational than analytical. \(\textsc{interjection}_{sem}\) is an expressive meaning type and \(\textsc{interjection}_{prag}\) an interactional function, not alternative lexical categories; relabelling them *interjection* does not establish independent nodes ([§3.4, lines 1155–1177](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:1155)). The operationalization confirms the problem: \(\textsc{interjection}_{prag}\) is defined simply as usability “on its own as an interactional move” ([line 831](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:831)), yet *bye* receives only partial membership, and the criterion would admit indefinitely many clauses and routine formulae. Reserve *interjection* for the independently established morphosyntactic category, call the other two dimensions expressive meaning and interactional function, and then test whether category membership predicts them. Otherwise the intersection at lines 1218–1222 merely builds the desired extension into the notation.

- The treatment of *damn* conflates clause type, verb form, and illocutionary force. Section 4.3 calls the optative a “minor clause type,” derives *damn him* by dropping the subject of *God damn him*, and says “Tense goes with the subject” ([lines 1390–1406](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:1390)). But the manuscript’s own source construction already contains a subject with plain/subjunctive *damn*, so loss of subject cannot explain loss of tense; nor does the wish interpretation by itself establish an optative syntactic category. The same problem recurs when properties already supplied by an imperative source—normally no overt subject and plain verb form—are counted as later “deactivation” in *come on* and *look* ([lines 1421–1439](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/main.tex:1421)). The revision should separately represent source-clause syntax, verb form, optative/directive force, and the synchronic IntjP analysis, and should not infer the diachronic transition from synchronic complement residue alone. The precise characterization attributed to *CGEL* here also needs verification against the source.

## 4. Key question

What distributional contrast identifies standalone *oh*, *wow*, or *damn* as heads belonging to a distinct lexical category before their stance, use-conditional meaning, sequential position, or uptake is consulted?

## 5. Verdict

**Revise & Resubmit** — the projectibility and residue arguments are substantial, but the central lexical-category claim currently depends on a function and two non-syntactic classifications rather than on an adequate independent distributional analysis.
