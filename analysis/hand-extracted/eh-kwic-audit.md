# KWIC audit: bare-string `eh` in GloWbE India and Pakistan

Samples pasted by Brett 2026-07-27, 20 tokens each, bare string.

## Result: clean

| variety | interjection | contaminants | rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| IN | 19 | 1 typo | 95% |
| PK | 17 | 1 typo, 1 mention, 1 quoted Arabic | 85% |

Almost all tokens are the ordinary tag-question or exclamative use: *"Trying to
be contrarian just to be cool, eh?"*, *"Worlds apart, eh?"*, *"Fat chance,
eh?"*, *"EH?!"*.

## The one systematic contaminant

A transposition typo for *the*, where the space falls one character early:

> IN: "the law attaches great importance **tot eh** Will of a parent" (*to the*)
> PK: "nephew saw the episode **int eh** washroom" (*in the*)

Two of forty tokens. Typing errors are not regionally distributed, so this adds
noise rather than bias, which is the opposite of the *hectare* problem in `ha`.

The other two are one metalinguistic mention (*when you say "Eh?" about the man
wanting the woman*) and one transliterated Quranic phrase (*"Eh DAY NASIRATUL
MUSTAQEEM"*, for *ihdina s-sirata l-mustaqim*).

## Why a clean result is worth recording

It shows the check discriminates. Across the items probed so far the rates are
`ha` 35–75%, `eh` 85–95%, `yaar` 60–65%, `haba` about 85% on the randomized
pull, `umm` in Pakistan effectively 0%. So raw-string counts are not uniformly
unreliable; specific forms have specific homographs, and only some of those
homographs track a variety. That is a more useful claim than a blanket caveat,
and it means `eh`'s figure in the conditioning table needs no qualification.
