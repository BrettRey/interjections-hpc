1. **One-sentence summary**

The paper’s strongest claim is that *interjection* earns lexical-category status because partial evidence or category membership supports defeasible forward and retrodictive inferences across phonology, syntax, meaning, and interaction, while causal pathways and synchronic couplings explain why those inferences are reliable.

2. **Strengths**

- Projectibility is the declared epistemic payoff, not an afterthought. The abstract and Section 1 foreground what membership licenses (lines 68–92, 158–172), while lines 218–245 carefully distinguish declaration, warrant, stability, network order, grounds, and corrective control. Mechanisms are explicitly presented as explaining projectibility rather than constituting categoryhood by themselves.

- The paper understands the circularity problem. Section 5.2 explicitly rejects predicting admission criteria and offers potentially genuine targets: semantic immediacy and repeatability, plus recipient uptake (lines 2134–2154). These targets have stated defeat conditions. The failed *fie* prediction is also reported honestly rather than rescued by post-hoc narrowing (lines 1828–1849).

- The conclusion cashes out the category in inferential terms: “Assigning a form to it lets an analyst predict” prosody, supplement function, stance, sequence, response, and uptake (lines 2525–2540). It also correctly limits the world-side conclusion to stability and network order, declining corrective-control or homeostasis claims (lines 2562–2567).

3. **Weaknesses**

- **Section 5 doesn’t consistently honour Section 1’s anti-circularity rule.** The *bruh* example begins with observed “standalone position, prosodic isolation, morphological bareness, and stance meaning” but then predicts non-inflection, “turn-initial or standalone” use, and expressive meaning (lines 2050–2068). Lines 2098–2105 even derive “the stance reading” from an observed package that already contains “stance meaning.” Similarly, Section 3 defines \(\textsc{interjection}_{prag}\) partly through backchannel use (lines 1168–1176), whereas Section 5 presents backchannel availability as a projection (lines 2148–2154). Freeze the admission features prospectively and exclude every target from them; semantic repeatability and independently measured recipient uptake are the clearest genuinely non-trivial candidates.

- **The paper declares projectibility more successfully than it demonstrates it.** Section 1 promises a separate warrant specifying evidence and defeat conditions (lines 218–230), but Section 5 offers examples and uncalibrated “should” claims rather than an out-of-sample test. The Discussion’s empirical probes concern pathway residue, geographic conditioning, and node erosion (lines 2300–2489), not the headline forward predictions from partial cues or membership to unseen prosodic, semantic, and uptake behaviour. The revision needs at least one explicit predictive evaluation with fixed source cues, held-out targets, scope and tolerance, and comparison against supplement-only, feature-bundle, or cue-only baselines.

- **The architecture still gives mechanisms the privileged position.** The section titled “Projectibility” begins only at line 1962, after the extensive causal-network and recruitment analysis; the Discussion calls pathway residue the “strongest probe” (lines 2311–2322) and “main explanatory payoff” (lines 2476–2481). The final generalization likewise asks which links are active and what recruited the members (lines 2558–2560). Move the projective declarations and their evaluation immediately after the property-cluster section; then let the causal sections explain why each successful projection holds. That would make the paper’s organization enact its thesis.

4. **Key question**

What independently measured outcome does assigning a held-out form to *interjection* predict better than the exact cues used to assign it—and where does the paper demonstrate that incremental predictive gain?

5. **Verdict**

**Revise & Resubmit** — projectibility is admirably central to the stated argument, but its non-circular predictive payoff remains less securely demonstrated than the mechanisms proposed to explain it.
