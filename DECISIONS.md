2026-03-14 — Target journal: Journal of Historical Pragmatics (Benjamins). Chosen for fit with diachronic dimension (semantic bleaching paths, nay→yeah shift) and because JHP already publishes interjection work (Gehweiler 2008 on gee!). Brett already has multiple papers in ELL and JL.

2026-03-14 — The HPC angle on interjections: the property cluster (non-referential, non-inflecting, prosodically isolated, syntactically supplemental, emotively laden) is maintained by identifiable mechanisms (semantic bleaching, prosodic isolation reinforcing syntactic independence). The distinctive contribution is that interjections push HPC harder than "respectable" categories because they're widely denied or marginalised as a category. The projectibility question is unusual: inference is primarily pragmatic rather than truth-conditional.

2026-03-14 — Primary empirical base: Brett's Wikipedia "Good Article" on English interjections, which covers phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics, pragmatics, variation, and the history of the category in English grammars. Corpus data from GloWbE and COHA already present.

2026-07-27 — Singlish particle prosody resolved from the sources Brett fetched. All four filed in literature/ and entered in references-local.bib.

Brett's judgement that *lah* is "not its own intonational unit but rather the fall of the larger unit" turns out to be Gupta's published position, not an intuition. Lim (2007: 447): "Gupta holds that particles function as the carrier of the intonation contour of the utterance (1992; 2006)", against Platt (1987), who "queries this against the possibility of their being tone unit nuclei, but does not come to a conclusion", and Platt and Ho (1989: 221), for whom it may vary by speaker.

More useful still, *lah* is the particle where the rival lexical-tone analysis is weakest. Lim (2007: 463): "all the SE particles, with the exception of lah, what and perhaps ah, can be seen to be the exact match of particles in Cantonese, including the maintenance of lexical tone". The tone analysis rests on the Cantonese-derived *lor*, *leh* and *meh*; *lah* comes from Bazaar Malay and Hokkien, whose particles Lim notes "have neutral tone and weak stress".

The point that matters for the paper is that the two rival analyses agree on what it needs. On Gupta's account *lah* carries the utterance's contour; on the lexical-tone account it is a weakly stressed neutral-tone enclitic. Neither makes it a prosodically independent unit, so *lah* fails the prosodic-isolation criterion for interjection_syn either way and the paper need not pick a side. With the KWIC finding that it is clause-final and attached in 20 of 20 tokens, that supports interjection_prag without interjection_syn: a third decoupling case beside prenominal *damn* and *mm-hm*.

Recorded honestly: Gupta (1992, 2006) is quoted here at second hand through Lim, and should be read directly before the paper attributes the claim to Gupta. Likely target is Gupta, A. F. (1992), "The pragmatic particles of Singapore Colloquial English", Journal of Pragmatics 18(1), 31--57.

It adds references only and does not touch the text quoted here, but it is recorded in the bib entry's note field so it travels with the citation.
