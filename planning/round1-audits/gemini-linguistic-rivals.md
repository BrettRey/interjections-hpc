# Gemini 3.1 Pro: linguistic categories and rivals audit

## Scope and verdict

Read-only audit of `AGENTS.md`, the approved architecture contract, and `main.tex`. The reviewer accepts the three-category architecture as coherent in principle but finds a high burden of independent motivation: `INTERJECTION_syn` risks merely renaming the CGEL word class; `INTERJECTION_sem` risks merely restricting Potts-style expressives; and `INTERJECTION_prag` risks becoming an overly broad label for interactional sign-types.

## Strongest surviving objection

`INTERJECTION_prag` may remain functionally tautological even after local circularity is removed. In interactional work, turn position, action formation, and recipient uptake can be mutually constitutive parts of one sequential phenomenon. Predicting next-turn treatment from a classification already established through the same action sequence may therefore redescribe an interactional norm rather than project to an independent target. The strongest repair is to classify a sign-type in one context or environment and predict its behaviour in an unobserved sequential environment.

## Lead verification notes

Two parts of the raw review require correction before synthesis:

- Its claim that construction grammar cannot explain convergent constraints is too strong. A constructional account can use inheritance and network relations; the paper must compare explanatory and projective performance rather than stipulate constructional failure.
- Its claim that the overlap figure straightforwardly places *bye* and *um* inside `INTERJECTION_prag` may overread spatial placement. The caption itself says their interactional standing is disputed. The real defect is ambiguity between diagram, caption, and admission rule, not an already-established contradiction.
- Its proper-noun/proper-name gloss is too syntactic and does not capture the user's intended analogy, in which *proper name* is a legitimate semantics/onomastics-facing category with mixed properties. The manuscript should use the analogy for ontological parity and cross-cutting extensions, not merely for word-versus-phrase structure.
