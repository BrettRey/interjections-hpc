The binding problem is not merely 46 rows. EXPRESSIVE often has only P07 and P08 effectively visible: P10 and P11 were item-direct for only 4 and 2 of 12 sampled items ([FREEZE-v4 §2](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/analysis/matrix-lesion/FREEZE-v4.md:231)), and P11 is inapplicable without a host. [CONVERGENCE-CHECK.md](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/queue/english-interjections/analysis/matrix-lesion/CONVERGENCE-CHECK.md:162) already concedes that with two effective diagnostics the model cannot settle which split is the stance class. At 15% P10/P11 documentation with scarce negatives, convergence gave +0.007 against +0.004 for the anchor rule, essentially indistinguishable.

CrossCat would make matters worse: it learns feature partitions from co-occurrence, forbids P07 from belonging to both systems, and has no treatment of undocumented versus documented absence. Grade-of-membership likewise cannot distinguish genuine intermediacy from uncertainty through one concealed cell. Erosheva and colleagues' own simulation found 5,000 by 10 data "too small to provide reliable distinction among different grades of membership," which I verified in the source text.

The simpler analysis better matched to the paper is a descriptive lesion study: for each projection, compare M0 with every single diagnostic and with the unthresholded convergence score, then ask whether M2 adds anything beyond them. That directly tests cliquish stability in the documented record without pretending that a two-class latent variable has been identified as the category. It also preserves your commitments better. FREEZE currently says a posterior is epistemic and "never reported as a degree of membership," yet the architecture still invites readers to treat the latent class as the grouping itself.
