# The wham–womb phoneme–arousal mapping
- **Cite**: Kuo, Li, Yu, McBeath & Benitez (2026), *Cognition*.
- **What it is**: A preregistered forced-choice study asked 56 English-speaking adults and 53 children aged 5–7 to map pseudowords differing only in /æ/ versus /u/ onto matched high- versus low-arousal cartoon scenes. Thirty-two yoked pairs (e.g., *smad–smood*) kept valence aligned across scenes.
- **Key finding/data**: Wham–womb-consistent choices exceeded chance for adults (65%, *p* < .001) and children (56%, *p* < .001); adults were stronger (*p* = .002). After controlling scene-selection bias, effects were *d* = 1.76 and 0.85, respectively; 96.9% of adult and 78.1% of child word pairs pointed in the predicted direction.
- **Plugs into**: **Sound symbolism / HPC phoneme stratum**: /æ/→higher arousal and /u/→lower arousal is a useful projectibility profile whose support varies by age and word frame, cautioning against a categorical or universal kind claim. **Interjections**: the proposed facial/vocal mechanism motivates testing /a, æ/ in pain interjections, but this paper itself supplies no interjection data.
- **Strength**: strong.
