The structure now carries that argument; the remaining problem is that readers often have to invent the examples that would make the distinctions intelligible.

## Start with §2.3

Use **The damn car won’t start** to distinguish the expressive contribution from an account of its encoding:

> The sentence describes a failure to start and conveys a negative attitude through *damn*. Calling *damn* expressive identifies that contribution. A procedural account asks how the expression guides the hearer toward the attitude. On such an account, the same use can be expressive in contribution and procedural in encoding. These descriptions answer different questions about one utterance.

Then return to **Bang!** in a narrative about a backfire. It depicts the noise; identifying that contribution and its iconic presentation doesn’t yet decide the conceptual/procedural question.

Make genuine rivalry concrete by explicitly stipulating two incompatible hypotheses about the same *damn* use: it encodes only conceptual content, or only an interpretive procedure. The point is the incompatibility of these exclusive claims, not a claim that these exhaust the options or summarize Potts and Wharton. In particular, conceptual content mustn’t be equated with asserting “I am annoyed.” Wharton supports the procedural possibility; the hypothetical rival will be identified as our illustration.
