## Contamination types

1. **Tamil *yaar* = "who".** A true homograph, the same problem as Arabic
   *Umm*. Appears in Sri Lanka (a Tamil proverb, a couplet) and in Indian
   sources as Tamil song titles (*Yaar Yaar Sivam* from *Anbe Sivam*).
2. **Proper names.** *Ahmad Yaar Khan*, *Yaar Mohammed Khan*, and *Amy Yaar*
   (a figure in Sri Lankan reporting).
3. **Roman-script Hindi/Urdu matrix.** The token sits inside a non-English
   clause: *"Jaane de naa yaar, sabko pata hai"*, *"aur wahan mera yaar chala
   gaya janab"*, *"Arey kya yaar, in dabbo ko hatta yaha se"*.
4. **Urdu lexical items and quotation.** *chaar yaar*, glossed in the source as
   "four friends"; *Yaar e Khush Khasaal*; quoted Urdu speech inside English
   reporting frames.

## Position: unlike *lah*

*Yaar* is mobile and frequently set off by commas.

> **Yaar**, you are right · Oye **Yaar**, this is it · **Yaar** Rohit, my
> mother likes your films · because I love my sister, **yaar**! · I totally
> get that **yaar** but somehow · damn **yaar**! · What's the big deal,
> **YAAR**?

Initial, medial and final, and often comma-delimited on one or both sides,
including in vocative frames with a following name (*Yaar Rohit*, *Yaar
Chintu*). Compare *lah*, which was clause-final and orthographically attached
in 20 of 20. On the paper's written proxies *yaar* looks like an address term
of the *man* / *mate* type and plausibly satisfies \intj{syn}, where *lah*
does not.

**Consequence.** The three regional items are not the same categorial kind.
Any claim that they share the full profile needs to be made item by item.
