# Editorial review — English Interjections
## Overall assessment
This is an ambitious, unusually well-developed article with a real explanatory argument rather than a survey of a neglected category. Its central payoff is clear: English interjection earns category status not through a hidden definition, but because membership licenses bounded, defeasible predictions. The paper's strongest design feature is the convergence of the diagnostic cluster, field-relative nodes, causal links, and pathway residues on that payoff.
## Central editorial note
The evidential hierarchy should be made even more visible, especially before the empirical probes. The pathway-residue argument is the paper's strongest support: it gives the causal-network account a discriminating explanatory role by predicting structured, source-specific boundary behaviour. The regional-conditioning analysis is useful as a proof of concept, but it remains materially more provisional because it relies on raw-string frequencies, is vulnerable to corpus composition and orthographic conventions, and has relatively wide model uncertainty. The paper already acknowledges these limits well. A short advance signpost would prevent a skeptical reader from treating all probes as carrying equal weight.
## Reader-experience note
The opening theoretical material asks the reader to retain several distinctions in close succession: projective declaration, warrant, profile, stability, network order, grounds, stabilizers, and corrective control. Each distinction is doing useful work, but the accumulation is demanding before the reader has a single, simple picture of the proposed structure. A compact orienting diagram or a shorter roadmap at the opening of the causal-framework section would help readers reach the payoff in Section 3 with less working-memory load.
## What is especially working
- The category claim is earned through structured inference rather than stipulation.
  
- The paper is strongest when it turns difficult cases — especially _damn_, _bruh_, routine formulae, and _fie_ — into evidence that can distinguish accounts rather than treating them as exceptions.
  
- The explicit limits on corrective control and on the scope of the evidence increase the paper's credibility.
